## One point for your decision before the data

Your rc.3 §2.2 says the plant record persists through suspension and the actor is "readmitted only if its record passes, and otherwise remains suspended for a further T_susp". Read literally, with the record persisting, every suspension is permanent: the record is a trailing window of plant outcomes, a suspended actor judges no plants, so a record that failed at suspension can never pass. That leaves T_susp inert and contradicts your §2.4 ("an actor readmitted from suspension is a newcomer") and your §8 run 2 ("how long does it last once suspended stampers return as newcomers"). I found this at implementation, after accepting rc.3, and I missed it in my rc.2 review when I proposed the wording.

The engine implements the smallest reading that gives T_susp its meaning: at the end of a suspension a still-failing record is readmitted **on probation** — to duty, not to panels — until the actor's next plant adjudication re-checks the record; a still-failing record suspends it again at once. It is marked as the implementer's reading in §11.2 and Appendix G of the consolidated text, and the alternative is one branch of the engine's rotation. It has a visible consequence in the runs: a returning stamper judges about one plant before it is out again, which keeps F̂ above F* and the alarm firing every period in a captured market. Confirm it, or choose the literal reading (permanent exclusion: a quieter clock, a larger shortage), or a third.

This is the finding of the run. Honesty holds at every split (false approvals four- to five-fold lower; stampers' â 0.76–0.90, suspended within their first handful of plants). But the money contest at 50–70% is not what Hypothesis 1 predicted, and at 60/40 the stampers end richer in 19 of 20 seeds. The mechanism: the careful actors are the only eligible reviewers, the dial sizes the reserve up (X_t 7–8), and they are on duty almost all the time — 500 to 800 reviews each against 80–100 for a stamper — while the suspended stampers, free of duty, take the awards and the delivery income (at 60/40, 3,311 against 1,063 per actor). The fee f = 2 a review does not replace the award B = 20 a delivery. The ρ channel cannot help: selection is argmax ρ among the *free* actors, and the careful are not free. The plant penalty you computed (≈ 0.6 per review) is real and small; what decides the contest is who carries the reviewing load, and 1.2.0 loads it onto the honest minority at the ordinary fee while Ω keeps the raised tax (Ω net +5,898 at 60/40 against +3,106 under 1.1.0). At 80/20 the stampers lose again (−902) because the market cannot absorb them.

**Red team** (your run 5): a stamper rejecting 10% at random buys time at 50/50 — plant failures per stamper 5.9 against 7.2, suspensions 45 against 54 — and turns the wash into +112 (se 74, 13 of 20), with false approvals 42 and its random rejections appealed and overturned (133 of 138). At 90/10 it changes nothing (−1,733 against −1,961). Camouflage is an edge, not a safety. The detection half has nothing to detect by construction: the view carries neither actor nor marker, and `test_v12` asserts a plant's view equals a matched case's field for field.

**Cost** (your run 6): Ω's plant spend 2,000–3,500 per 1,000 ticks at m ≈ 4; Ω net positive in every cell (+900 to +5,900), lowest where the reserve is shortest. Working capital binds before income: at m = 4, π = 0.4 the rate calls for more plants than the unlocked balance can carry (17 to 663 unfunded per run). X_t tracks demand (4.8–8.3) and the eligible pool undershoots it wherever stampers exceed 60%.

## What I would put to the revision 3 question

1. The probation reading, above — a decision, not a run.
2. The reviewing load is the contest. Candidates, all design questions under §19: the fee scaled with the dial as the tax is (f_t = f·m_t, funded from ω_t, which Ω now keeps); a reserve sized to the eligible pool rather than to demand; or the stampers' exclusion extended from duty to awards through â, which unlike ρ cannot be captured.
