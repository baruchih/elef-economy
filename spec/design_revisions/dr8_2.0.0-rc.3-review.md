## The one reading

**"Two of $N_T$" — reserved, or checked?** §2.6 says a delivery's summons needs room for itself and its inspection seat. In run 10 a troop took the inspection seat of a troop's delivery only 0–1.3 times a tick at rest — members and probationers take most pinned audits inside $w_R$ — so the second place is usually not used. If the summons only *checks* for two free places, deliveries can fill 39 of 40 and the inspections then arriving are refused for capacity: the hazard the clause is for. If it *reserves* the second place, at most twenty deliveries are in the field and §6's "about twenty-six deliveries and their seats" is wrong. **I will reserve, and release on seating:** a troop delivery holds two places from its summons until its initial review is assigned — to a market reviewer, after which it holds one; to a troop, after which the seat holds its own — and the capacity reading counts places held, not troops. That keeps the clause's meaning, puts the field at 20–40 deliveries depending on how fast the market seats them, and makes §6's twenty-six a plausible middle rather than a bound. If the text means "checked", one word in §2.6 settles it and the engine follows.

## For the record

- The crowd's requesters are ordinary requesters for the engine (wallet, issuance, the posting lock); the driver adds them at `--spike-from` and stops their posting at `--spike-from + --spike-ticks`, and they leave when their last open request closes — their wallets stay in $M$ as hoards until then and are retired from the readings, not burned, when they go (nothing in the rules burns a leaver's wallet; the text says "leave", and I read that as "post no more"). If the reviewer wants the crowd's unspent credits to leave $M$ with them, that is a burn kind to name.
