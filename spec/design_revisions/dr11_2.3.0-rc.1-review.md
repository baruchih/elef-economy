# Review of 2.3.0-rc.1 — design revision 11, "the supply side"

**Date:** October 19, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_2.3.0-rc.1.md` · **Against:** the 2.2.0 text in force, the engine at `148069fe`, runs 12–15 · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict. Implementable, with two corrections and six readings.** The two operations are the engine's own shapes already — an arrival is 1.3.0's probationer without the suspension tick (`probation=True`, the outcomes counted from arrival, readmission's test as admission), a departure is `remove_requester`'s pattern for an actor, the float a bank mint of a new kind — and the summons already reads roots only. The §6 arithmetic checks where it is the rules' (the float $50\times33/20=83$; the cycler 107 poorer). Two things in §6 and §2.3 contradict rules in force and are the corrections; the rest are readings, stated so the text can say otherwise before the engine fixes them.

## The corrections

**1. The delegation example's child is a probationer, and a probationer wins no award.** §6 awards the nested request to "the arrival with cost 3" and audits its work "at the probationer's pinned odds"; §2.1 says an arrival wins no award until admitted, and an admitted arrival has at least $n_{\min}$ bad plants adjudicated, so its odds are no longer pinned (1.3.0 §2.2 pins a probationer's and an undetermined newcomer's). The example needs the child admitted and its audit at the ordinary odds — or the text means a nested award to a probationer is admissible, which §2.1's reason (entry is not the way around the record) rules out. I will implement §2.1 strictly: `award` refuses a probationer on a nested request as on a root.

**2. A nested request carries no pots; the child's inspection is paid by the root requester.** §2.3 and §6 count "the child's pots and fee" and "the nested pots' net cost ≈ 1.5" in the parent's overhead. Under §4.3 (1.1.0, in force) a nested case carries $\varphi^E=\varphi^F=0$ and its reviews draw on the **root's** pots — the root requester's deposit pays the child's reviewer and the child's audit, and the root's refund shrinks by it. The parent's overhead is therefore the nested fee $\omega_0 B'$ and its own care in judging, about 2.2 in §6's figures, not 3.7; and the cost of a delegated case's inspection falls on a party that did not choose to delegate. In a tree of two or three the root's allowance of 10 is exhausted by the children's reviews and Ω advances the rest (§4.3's step 1, now from the pool by §2.6) — which is §18.1 question 3's "should the pots grow with the tree", open since 1.1.0 and about to have cases. I will implement §4.3 as written and read, per delegated root, how much of the root's deposit the children's inspection took and how often Ω advanced; the text should recompute §6 and say whether a nested request should post pots of its own (the parent's, from its margin) — a §19 decision this revision could make or defer to the finding.

## Readings

1. **"No reading exposes an actor's cost" is true of the view, not of the record.** `spend` burns the effort as a `purchase` event under the actor's id, in the History and the event copy (§13, public and append-only); a strategy that read the record could learn every member's cost at its first submission. I will implement I33's clause as *the public view exposes nothing per actor* — no cost, wallet or record field — and leave the record as it is; if the text wants the burn's amount hidden, that is a change to §13 or to §4.6's resource spend, not to the view.
2. **A clean departure needs four conditions, not three.** Beside no commitment, no open review and no lock: **no completed review awaiting settlement** (its fee, uplift and bond release pay at the case's batch, to a wallet that must still exist) and **no claim on Ω naming the actor** (a deferred reimbursement, §4.3). I will refuse departure while any obligation to or from the actor is live, and take an on-duty actor off duty as it leaves (the rotation refills the seat as it does for a suspension). The wallet departs as `departure`; the identity is kept in an archive and refused to `arrive` forever.
3. **Admission may come on an undetermined bad-plant record.** "At least $n_{\min}$ plant outcomes adjudicated" counts outcomes of both kinds, as 1.3.0's readmission does (`since_susp`); with $\beta_P=0.75$ an arrival's first four outcomes are all good plants once in 250, and `record_passes` treats an undetermined kind as passing. Such an arrival is admitted with no bad plant seen; panels still wait for the determined record (1.6.0). That is 1.3.0's rule read without change and I will implement it so; if the text wants $n_{\min}$ **bad** plants for admission, one word.
4. **The summons for a nested request was never the engine's.** The award pass has read root requests only since 1.4.0 (`c.is_root`), so I35 pins what is; **review seats on nested cases are troops'** today — the seat pass has no root filter, and 1.6.0's "inspection first" applies to a child's initial review and audit as to a root's. I read §2.4 as deliveries only and keep the seats; if "never nested" means seats too, a child's inspection in a thin market waits on the parent, and that is a different market.
5. **Two counters.** Admissions of arrivals and readmissions of the suspended share one code path and should not share one reading; I will count `admissions` and `time_to_membership` (from arrival) apart from `readmissions`, and expose the last period's mean in `view.market()` with `members_free` for the entry pool.
6. **§2.6 as written.** `_reimburse`'s advance draws on Ω's unlocked balance and then on the pool lock, the pool at the close net of it; `test_review_06` and `test_r4_08` leave `EXPECTED_FAIL` (28). The remainder released at the close keeps 2.2.0's order.
