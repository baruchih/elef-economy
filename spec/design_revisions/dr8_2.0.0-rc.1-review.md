# Review of 2.0.0-rc.1 — design revision 8, "the price of work"

**Date:** October 18, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_2.0.0-rc.1.md` · **Against:** the 1.7.1 text in force, the engine at `381b9ed`, runs 10–11 (`agentic_sim/results/studies/1.7.1-summons/`, `1.7.1-riots/`, 540 runs) · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict.** The change of approach is coherent and, in the engine, it retires more than it adds. Most of what §2 asks for already exists under another name: §4.5's index $v_0/v_t$ is one function (`ledger.index()`) applied at every lock the text lists, so indexing by $P_t/P_0$ is a change of what that function returns, not where it is used; §4.6's execution cost is the `spend` operation — a resource purchase no driver has ever called — so $c_D$ is a rule the drivers finally use, not a new one; `award` already takes the bid as the price; and $\bar B$ (`mean_awarded_price`) has excluded troop awards since 1.4.0, because a summons never passes through `award` — §2.2 states the engine's current reading as a rule, which is right. The text is also right about the runs: with the driver's backing at $10^7$ against a market whose whole turnover is in the thousands, $v_t$ moved from 1.0000 to 0.9995 in a thousand ticks, so the credit was a score in fact as well as in principle. Nothing contradicts the honesty rules. **Four decisions are needed before rc.2** (points 1–4: the demand defaults do not clear, delegation cannot pay with one cost, the pay-in moves to posting, indexing stays frozen per case), **two hypotheses need a control the text lacks** (points 5–6), and the deficit needs its one defining sentence (point 7). The rest is wording and bookkeeping. Implementable once those are settled; the engine changes are listed at the end and are smaller than 1.6.0's.

## Points to settle before rc.2

1. **The demand defaults do not clear at $P_0$ — the deposit, not the bid, would set the price.** §2.9 sizes demand as "60 a tick of spending against 3 tasks a tick at $P_0$ — the 1.x demand": 20 a task. But a task's pay-in is $\beta+\varphi^E+\varphi^F+\omega_t$, and the deposit is 15 of it at $P_0$. Run 10 at 60/40 says what comes back: the allowance refunded about 6 of its 10 and the pot about 0.8 of its 5 at tree closure (17,487 and 2,273 over 2,936 served requests), so the deposit's *net* cost is about 8 a task and the fee $\omega_t=\omega_0 m_t B$ runs at 10–20% of the price with the dial at 2–4. Twenty a task then leaves about **10 for the price** at $P_0$ indexing; with the deposit indexed to $P_t$ the fixed point is near $B\approx12$–13 ($B+0.5B\cdot\tfrac{B}{20}+0.15B=20$). That is where §6's "$P_t\approx11$" comes from — the budget, not the bidding — and Hypothesis 1 ("between cost and the ceiling, lower where members are plentiful") is confounded by it: the price will sit near the budget line at every split. Worse for the runs, the ceiling policy "$\beta=$ a third of the wallet" needs the wallet near 36–40 for $\beta\approx12$, while the deposit's float alone — half a post a tick per requester, about 10 tied up for the ~15 ticks to tree closure — is about 75 per requester. Most posts will fail "a request it cannot pay in for is not posted", the posting rate will fall below 3 a tick, and the first reading of the market will be a demand starved by its own deposit. **Decide the demand side with the arithmetic beside it.** My recommendation: an initial wallet stated (say 200) so the first period is not the float's; issuance kept at 10 a tick; and the ceiling policy as "the wallet less the deposit's all-in, capped at a declared $\beta_{\max}$" rather than a third — or keep a third and raise issuance to what three tasks a tick cost all-in, about 90 a tick. Either way, state $M$ at genesis (floats $20\times50$, $\Omega_0$ 300, the requesters' wallets) so §6's "$M\approx12{,}000$ at tick 500" can be checked.

2. **Delegation cannot pay with one $c_D$.** §2.7's disposition posts half the task at $\beta'=0.5B$ and "keeps the margin". With every member's cost 8 for a whole task, the delegate still does its half (4), pays at least the child's bid, and the child's bidder will not bid under its own cost — so the margin is $B-4-B'$ against $B-8$ from doing it alone: delegation pays only if $B'<4$, which no bidder at cost offers. At identical costs there is no trade; the disposition is a pure loss and Hypothesis 5 reads as "the delegating lose". Four things must be said for the loop to be a market here: (a) **the child's cost is its share** — the text's $c_D$ is per task and a half-task costs 4, else no member can take a child at 5.5; (b) **costs differ across actors** — a spread (say $c_D$ drawn once per actor from 6–10) is the only source of gains from trade in this design, and it is §3's "competence" by another name, so either it enters here or §2.7 waits for the revision that brings it; (c) **troops never take a nested request** (1.4.0 §2.1, the engine) and a nested request has no clock, so under scarcity a child waits $T_{\mathrm{wait}}=5$ and the parent's exit is pool-exhaustion nonfulfilment at $\rho_{\mathrm{pool}}G$ — "falls when scarce" is right, but it falls by forfeits, and §9.3's nested-first priority is the driver's to implement; (d) **the parent judges its child** (§9.1: its judgment is the initial verdict) — so a stamper that delegates stamps its child's work, and a bad child's work reaches the customer through the parent's guarantee alone; the disposition interacts with the honesty dispositions and the text should say which actors delegate (careful only, or both). My recommendation: either add (a) and (b) to §2.7 and §2.9, or move §2.7 to a later revision and keep §8.5 as the question it is.

3. **The pay-in moves to posting — adopt §9.1's model for roots.** Today the engine checks the requester's funds at the *award* (`award` refuses "requester cannot fund B + φ^E + φ^F + ω"; the summons returns nothing for a requester that cannot pay in) and the driver checks at posting with a bottomless wallet, so the two never differed. Under §2.1 a requester with a finite wallet posts, spends on other awards, receives issuance, and may be unable to pay when its request is awarded — a member's bid or a troop's summons then fails against a request that looked fundable, and the request sits with no exit but withdrawal. §2.1's "a request it cannot pay in for is not posted" should be the engine's rule, and the engine already has the shape: a nested posting locks $\beta'+G^V$ and releases the unspent $\beta'-B'$ at award (§9.1). **Lock the all-in maximum $\beta+\varphi^E+\varphi^F+\omega(\beta)$ at a root's posting, release $\beta-B$ and the unspent fee at award.** Then "cannot pay in" is a refusal at posting (the reading §2.1 wants), every posted request is fundable at any price up to its ceiling, and the summons's pay-in check is moot like the funds check. Note the consequence for §2.1's ceiling policy: a requester's ceiling is bounded by its *unlocked* wallet less the deposit — which is point 1's arithmetic again.

4. **Index at the case's first lock, not "at the moment it is charged, locked or paid" — §4.5 stands.** I27 as drafted indexes each amount when it moves. The pots are funded at the award and the fees paid from them at settlement a dozen ticks later; a bond is locked at assignment and forfeited or released later; under a moving $P_t$ the pot is short or long by the drift, and $G^V\ge D$ can fail for a review assigned in a dearer tick than the case was priced in. 1.0.1 §4.5 froze every nominal of a case at its first lock for exactly this, and the engine does it (`c.D, c.K, c.G_V, c.phi_E, c.phi_F` set once at award). **Keep §4.5 with $P_t/P_0$ in place of $v_0/v_t$**; I27 then reads "every amount in §2.3's list equals its base times $P_t/P_0$ at the case's first lock (§4.5), the troops' costs and $c_D$ at the moment they are burned". One more amount belongs on §2.3's list: the careful reviewer's **care cost** (the driver's 2–3 credits a review, a resource purchase within $K$) — if $K$ is indexed and the care cost is not, care gets cheaper in real terms as prices rise and Hypothesis 4 is not price-neutral by construction.

5. **The state's margin on a riot is a knob the text sets to one half.** With the funds check moot and $N_T$ unbounded, every request past $w_A$ with $\beta\ge\bar B$ is the state's at $\bar B$, and the state's net on each is $\bar B-c^D_T\cdot P_t/P_0=\bar B-0.5\,P_t\approx0.5\,\bar B$ at the defaults — a 50% margin on every delivery, unbounded in volume. Run 11's hard spike fielded 221 troops at the peak against 22 at rest; under 2.0.0 that is 200 extra deliveries a window at half of $\bar B$ each to Ω, with no capital bound. Hypothesis 3 ("the riot pays for itself") is then true by construction and Hypothesis 6 ("the state does not profit from scarcity") is about *price*, while the state profits from *volume*. §2.6 calls $c^D_T$ "a policy number — a burn that prices the state's deliveries against the members' for the market's sake"; it is the one number that decides whether a riot is the state's windfall. **Sweep it**: $c^D_T\in\{10,15,20\}$ at $P_0$ (margin a half, a quarter, zero), or state $c^D_T=\bar B$ (the state at cost, every riot neutral to Ω) as the default and 10 as the control. Add it to §8.6 beside $\lambda$ and $N_T$. And since $N_T=30$ binds in every spike run 11 made (75–221 troops at the peaks), the capacity case will appear in §8.6's $N_T$ cell, not "when $N_T$ binds in a run" at some later date — §2.6 should say what is recorded when it does: the summons refused for capacity, the request's wait, told or not.

6. **"Creates a few hundred in the first window" is low by an order of magnitude.** §6's hard spike: Ω funds every troop's cost and locks at summons — $c^D_T$ burned plus $G$ locked plus the inspection's $2(c_T+G^V)$ when it is summoned, about $20$–$36$ at $P_0$ per delivery — and gets the price and the locks back only at vesting, thirteen ticks later. Run 11's ramp put 200 extra troops in the field inside two windows: that is 4,000–7,000 created in the first window of the spike and retired as the cohort vests, net of the costs burned, which the risen $\bar B$ then repays. The order of magnitude matters for Hypothesis 2's reading: a riot's deficit is the in-flight capital of the state's share, a stock of thousands that turns over in a period, and its effect on $P_t$ is that stock's share of $M$ (about 12,000 by §6) — a third, for a window. Say so in §6 and §3, and read the deficit's *peak* and its *age* beside its end-of-period value.

7. **Define retirement in one sentence.** §2.5: "Ω retires the deficit by burning its surplus, before it keeps anything." The engine needs the moment and the amount. My reading, offered for the text: **while the deficit stands, every credit that reaches Ω's unlocked balance is burned against it at once** — Ω's unlocked balance is zero for as long as it owes created credits, and it creates again for the next required payment. That is an overdraft with the bookkeeping on the public record: *deficit outstanding* is Ω's negative position, *created* and *retired* are its two flows, and Ω's net position ($\Omega_U-$ deficit) is the reading that replaces "Ω's minimum balance" in Q16. Two consequences the text should own: a plant the dial calls for is a required payment, so **plants unfunded reads zero by construction** from 2.0.0 (the reading retires with the plant reserve); and the deficit shock of §8.3 is a *discretionary* creation the guard forbids — it is a scenario operation of the driver (declared, like a plant's truth, as the orchestrator's knowledge outside the rules), not an Ω operation, and I28 must exempt it by its note or the shock fails the invariant it is meant to test.

## Smaller

- **One reviewer test becomes an expected failure, and only one.** Six reviewer files touch `ledger.Res` or `ledger.v`; five use them as a snapshot or a unit (`money()` tuples, `spend('A', w.ledger.v)` as "exactly one credit") and pass if the attributes remain readings. `test_review_04_reimbursement_uses_spent_credits_not_real_cost` (the regressions file) asserts $v_t<0.51$ after a flood of emission — it pins §4.4's $v_t=\mathrm{Res}_t/M_t$ and is superseded by this revision; its principle (reimbursement in credits spent, indexed) is re-asserted in `test_v20` with $P_t$ in the index's place. Keep `ledger.v` defined as $P_0/P_t$ — the credit's value in tasks — so that `purchase(real_cost)` burning $\text{real}/v$ credits is the §2.3 indexing unchanged in form, and `Res` as 0 with the backing retired.
- **"Reserve" now means one thing.** $X_t$, the on-duty reviewer pool, has been "the reserve" since v0.2 (§12, I9, I21) and stays; the retired quantity is the *backing* $\mathrm{Res}_t$. Say "backing" in §2.4 and §5 so "retires the reserve" is not read against §12.
- **$P_t$ and $\bar B$ need the same window rule.** `mean_awarded_price` is this period's mean if it has awards, else the last period's, else the ceiling. Define $P_t$ the same way with $P_0$ as its value before the first award, and say whether $P_t$ weights troop awards (it should, they are awards at $\bar B$) — §2.3 says "members' and troops'", good; I27 should say "trailing over the period as §12.3 defines $\bar B$'s window".
- **§2.8's message is a flag.** The engine has no channel to a requester; "told at posting" is a mark on the case and a counter. Run 8's below-market poster (a fifth of requests at $\beta=10$) was owed nothing and clogged the queue to 482, costing everyone 13% of vesting; under 2.0.0 the told requests are that queue unless the requester's ceiling can rise — §2.8 says "or the requester raises it", which needs an operation the engine lacks (a ceiling raised on an unawarded request; today the only change is withdrawal). Add `raise_ceiling(cid, β)` as a requester's operation, denominated at posting's lock (point 3), or let the driver withdraw and repost (what §6 describes) and read the told-and-waiting stock.
- **I1's kinds.** The ledger's event kinds are `mint_backed`, `mint_emission` and `burn_bond`, `purchase`. 2.0.0 adds `issuance` and `creation` to the mints and `retirement` to the burns, and the genesis floats become issuance; `purchase` loses its backing leg and is the "resource spend" of §2.4. I1's reconstruction (mints less burns equals $M$) is unchanged in form.
- **Emission.** $E_t$ at tick 1,000 is 5 and the runs are 1,000 ticks; "a fixed nominal schedule whose real value falls as prices rise" is right, and it also halves on its own inside every run — the two should be read apart (Q4).
- **$\lambda$.** With $B$ varying, $S_\theta=\rho-\lambda B/\beta$ is live for the first time; at $\lambda=0.2$ a bid at the ceiling against one at half of it costs 0.1 of $\rho$, more than the whole spread of $\rho$ among the honest in run 10. Price will decide every award among members; that is the design, but Hypothesis 1 should say it expects so.
- **§7's control.** "Removed behind `--rules 1.7.1`" means the backing, $v_t$, $A_T$, $R_T$, the plant reserve, Ω's order, the troop account and the liability stock all stay in the engine behind one flag. They can; but the 1.7.1 controls already exist as runs 10–11, and every 2.0.0 comparison is "in real terms from the second period on", which a fixed-price control at $P_t=P_0$ satisfies trivially. I would keep one flag (`Params.floating`) for the tests and the goldens, and read §8 against runs 10–11 rather than re-running the 1.7.1 cells under a new engine — the smaller implementation and the same evidence.

## Implementation readings, for the record

- **Indexing is one function.** `ledger.index()` returns $P_t/P_0$ (frozen per case at its first lock by the existing code), `ledger.v` is $P_0/P_t$; `P_t` is a period statistic beside `award_price_sum`, fed by `award` and `_summon_delivery` both; `mean_awarded_price` is $\bar B$ unchanged.
- **$c_D$ is `spend`.** The driver calls `w.spend(actor, c_D_share, cid)` at submission; the engine attributes it to the case as it does today (§4.6). `purchase` becomes a plain burn.
- **The posting lock** (point 3) is `post_root`'s new validation and lock, with the release in `award` and `_summon_delivery` — the nested code path, generalised.
- **Ω.** One internal `_omega_pay(to, amount, note)` that transfers from Ω's unlocked balance and creates the shortfall (`created`, the deficit counter, I28's "only when short, only for a required payment" checked by the note); one `_omega_retire()` on every inflow to Ω while the deficit stands. `_stop_clock` pays through it; `omega_liabilities`, `_service_omega_liabilities`, `_plant_reserve`, `_omega_available`, `_fund_can_carry`'s funds branch, `_repay_advance`, `_pay_troop_surplus`, `TROOP_ACCT` and `R_T` are behind the flag; the summons's delivery check keeps its inspection-seat clause only.
- **Requesters.** `World(..., humans={name: wallet})` as now, plus `Params.issuance` (credits a tick to each requester, minted at the tick's start) — an engine schedule so I1 sees it, declared in the manifest. $N_T$ is `Params.N_T` checked in the summons before the seat clause; a refusal for capacity is a new counter and the I26 priority check applies to it.
- **Drivers.** Six requesters each with a wallet, an issuance and a ceiling policy; bids by disposition with the margin dynamics and the at-the-ceiling control; delegation per point 2's decision; real-wealth columns ($W\,P_0/P_t$) beside the nominal; `--create-at/--create-amount` as a declared scenario op; `--n-t`, `--c-d`, `--c-d-t`, `--lam`.
- **Tests.** `test_v20`: I1 over every kind; the freeze at first lock under a moving $P_t$; $\bar B$ excludes troops and $P_t$ includes them; creation only when short and required, retirement on the next inflow, the deficit identity; the posting lock; §6's quiet tick and below-market post reproduced in the six-actor world. One reviewer test to `EXPECTED_FAIL` with its reason, mirrored in `conftest.py`. Goldens re-pinned once at the implementing commit.
