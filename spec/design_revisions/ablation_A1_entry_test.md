## In plain words

1. **A few skippers among many careful members do not prosper.** With 1 slot in 10 skipping, a skipper ends with about half of what a careful slot earns (418–479 against 796–922 real), and almost none of them is still in place at the end. The careful earn what they would earn with no skippers at all.
2. **The more skippers, the better the careful do and the worse the world does.** The careful take over the seats and the awards that the skippers lose. From 1 skipper in 10 to 3 in 4, bad work that gets through rises from 5–7 in a thousand to 9–34, and the system's cost for each task by about a half.
3. **What a skipper gives up is worth seven to eight times what it saves**, at 1 in 10, and ten to fifteen times at the larger shares. This is the reviewer's arithmetic on the note's figures.
4. **A correction.** Careful members are not suspended more often when skippers are among them. The earlier figure had counted arrivals as suspensions. The sentence drawn from it is withdrawn.
5. **The stake's table is complete.** In the scripted table the member's audit seat closes with no bigger deposit in one cell only: tests at 3 in 10 with the seat's fee doubled. Tests and fee cost careful members nothing in real terms, and the system pays: the cost of a task up by two fifths, the cost of truth more than doubled, prices up by a twentieth. A deposit of four times costs the careful 4 to 5 in 100 of their real lifetime income, and three to four times as many reviews that they cannot fully fund.
6. **The reviewer's misses.** This time the arithmetic overstated the deposit's bite at the higher rate of tests, in three cells, and understated the system's cost. One expectation rested on the figure that has now been withdrawn.
7. **Not read:** a member that skips only now and then; a skipper that comes back under a new name; and a learner that counts its own future, which is a build and Baruchi's decision.

## 6. What it means

- **For an agent that counts its own future the record does the work, and now at the margin too.** With tests at 1 in 10 and no added stake, a skipper among careful members ends with half their income and loses its place. Skipping is not a way of life at any share of skippers read.
- **So the skipping in every learned chain is the learner's short sight.** A stake sized on those chains is sized for an agent that cannot see its own future, and the honest pay for it: four times the deposit costs them 4 to 5 in 100 of their real lifetime, and six to eight times cost 8 to 15 in 100 of their surplus.
- **The careful do not pay for skippers among them. The world does**, in bad work that gets through and in the system's cost.
- **The order of the levers holds, and is sharper.** The fee at the seat and more frequent tests first, paid by the system; the deposit last, paid by the honest. One cell of the table closes the seat with no added deposit. The table sizes nothing by itself (§108).
- **For the staged loop.** What stops a skipper is losing its place. That is the lever of Baruchi's note on decision 4: a member that does not review well takes no new jobs. The next attack is therefore on the place itself: a new name.
- **The orchestrator's audit** again leaks less and costs less than the member's audit seat, at every share. It is evidence, as before, for the recommendation Baruchi did not adopt, and it is not reopened here.

## 8. A learner with a horizon (asked in H4; nothing built)

The implementer's answer, in short:

- **Today** a review decision is credited with its own case's money, and with a charge for a suspension it helps to cause. It does not see the seats and awards the slot will not be given afterwards, or its exit.
- **The change**: the decision's target becomes its slot's own return — the discounted sum of its later decisions' rewards and of the slot's own work-side money in between, to a horizon or to the slot's exit.
