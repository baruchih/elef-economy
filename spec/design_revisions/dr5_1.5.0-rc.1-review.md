# Review of 1.5.0-rc.1 — design revision 5, "the reserve is sized to the need"

**Date:** October 17, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `spec-1.5.0-design-revision-5.md` (headed 1.5.0-rc.1, "Proposed") · **Against:** the 1.4.0 text in force, the engine at `f0779ac` (results through `4176ee7`), run 6 and its ruling addendum (`agentic_sim/results/studies/1.4.0-troops/`) · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict.** The rule is the right one and the smallest one: it changes what $R_T$ measures and nothing else, and the engine already has every quantity it needs — capital held per live troop, a refusal branch in each summons, the period clock. The classification (one rule, I23 amended, minor) is right. rc.1 is not implementable as written on one point: its own consequence and worked example describe a reserve that *falls as the backlog is served*, and a peak-of-the-period statistic does not fall until the period ends (1). Three definitions need a word so the engine does one thing (2–4), the §6 figures need revising for how long a delivery's capital is held (5), and the §2.3 reading should attribute the leak to the gate that failed, not only to the auditor's kind (6). On the naming in §1.6 and §9: I read now that "Claude" in these texts is the design reviewer and "the implementer" is me; my two corrections in the run-6 notes stand on substance and the text already says the same, so nothing is owed there.

## What checks out

- Every figure in §1 against run 6 and the ruling addendum (2,376 / 2,024; 3,621 / 1,856; 12 / 27 / 44 / 56%; 900 / 870; 4,800–8,800 and 6,400–12,700; 10,000–22,000; 4,832 → 1,640; 545–2,709; 34 → 9 and 19 → 5; 62 → 52 and 22 → 16; a fifth to a half).
- The diagnosis in §1.3 matches the engine: the batch pays uplift from `fund.U − R_T` with $R_T$ the peak of `cost + lock` over live troops (`_troop_capital_now`), so after each batch the fund sits at $R_T$, and `_fund_can_carry` refuses anything above `fund.U` plus the advance's room. The rule did what it said; the rc's reading that the decision was right and the formula wrong is the one I would give.
- The three rejected alternatives are rejected for the reasons I would have given; the sawtooth of "uplift only when shortfall was zero" is real (the fund would pay out its whole accumulation in the first batch after a served period and be short again at the next).
- The double-count guard in §2.1 — one case, once per seating, never ten times to one stock — is exactly the case-ticks point and it is stated correctly. A case refused at seating $s$ and carried at $s+1$ appears in $D_s$ as refused cost and in $D_{s+1}$ as capital held: no stock counts it twice.
- The engine's "seating step" is one `_summon_troops` pass per tick, after the dial and the rotation; $D_s$ is computable at the end of that pass from the pass's own refusals. No new state crosses a tick boundary except the running maximum.
- §2.2's restatement of Hypothesis 4 says what run 6 showed and names the failure that was not observed (an award taken by a troop from a member who had offered for it). Text only, as classified.
- Hypothesis 2 ($w_A$ begins to matter) is the right thing to expect and the windows sweep already exists to test it.

## Must fix before rc.2

1. **A peak does not fall; §2.1's consequence and §6 say it does.** §2.1 defines $R_T=\max_{s\in\text{period}}D_s$ and then says "as it serves the backlog, refusals stop, the demand stock falls to what is carried, and the money flows back to the reviewers." The *stock* $D_s$ falls; the *reserve* is its maximum over the trailing period and holds until the period ends. §6 makes the same slip: "$D_s\approx68$, $R_T\approx70$, and the uplift resumes" within the same period after $R_T$ had reached 108. Under the rule as written the uplift resumes when the balance exceeds 108, and $R_T$ comes down to the steady-state demand only at the next period boundary. Two honest ways to write it; I recommend the first:
   - (a) *Keep the peak and say so.* The fund retains for the rest of the period in which demand peaked; money returns to the reviewers at the period's end. One period is 200 ticks or 50 plants — the dial's own lag — and it is a feature: a fund that has just been short does not pay out the moment it is not. Amend the consequence and the example.
   - (b) *A reserve that tracks the stock.* $R_T=\max(D_s,\ \text{peak of the trailing period's refused demand})$ or a decayed peak. More responsive, one more definition, and the refused component still cannot fall mid-period without a decay rule the text would have to choose. I would not take this unless the sweep under (a) shows the retention lag costs something that matters.

   **And the engine's reading of "trailing period" must be settled with it.** The 1.4.0 engine computes $R_T$ as the larger of *this* period's peak and the *previous* period's (`troop_capital_peak_prev`), so the reserve does not drop at the boundary to whatever happens to be carried at that instant; the text said "trailing period" and I read it as a window of one period that is always full. That reading is not in Appendix I and should have been; I will record it in the 1.5.0 consolidation either way. Under 1.5.0 it decides the retention lag — one period or up to two. With demand-sizing, the one-period reading suffices: at a boundary the new peak starts at $D_s$ *now*, which includes the capital of every troop still carried, so there is no drop while troops are in the field, and the lag halves. I recommend rc.2 say "the peak over the current period, starting at the period's first seating from the demand then standing" and I drop the previous period. If the text prefers the two-period window, say that.

2. **"Refused a summons" must mean refused for funds.** `_summon_delivery` also returns without a troop when the request is priced below $\bar B$ (not a shortage: it waits) and when the requester cannot pay in; `_summon_review` returns without one when the path is full ($V_{\max}$, I8). None of these is a refusal for funds and none adds to demand. The rule should read "cases whose summons the fund could not carry at $s$" — the branch that already increments the shortfall counters. The requester-cannot-pay case is the market's refusal, the price case is the rule's own exclusion, and the full path is not a need.

3. **A panel seat is two seats.** §2.1 counts refused *cases*; a challenge missing both panel seats is one case needing $2\,(c_T+G^V)$. The engine's panel loop stops at the first refusal, so the shortfall counter sees one. For demand the honest figure is the capital the case *needs* at that seating — both seats — and the text should say "the capital the case's unfilled seats would have drawn", so that "once per seating" is about ticks and not about seats. The same wording covers a case with an audit seat refused (one seat) and a delivery (one).

4. **What a refused summons would have drawn, at indexation.** The text gives $c^D_T+G$ and $c_T+G^V$. In the engine a delivery's $G=\max(1,\lceil G_{frac}\bar B\rceil)$ at the summons and a seat's bond is the case's indexed $G^V$ (`c.G_V`), both computed before the carry check — so the refused cost is exactly the `need` the carry check was given, not the Appendix A default. Say "the amount the carry check refused" or "its costs as they would have been purchased and posted at $s$", and the engine records the number it already has.

5. **§6's capital figures understate how long a delivery is carried, under 1.4.0 and 1.5.0 alike.** A delivery troop's capital (cost $c^D_T$ spent plus $G$ locked, 20 at $\bar B=20$) is held from the summons until the case *vests* — $\tau_T$ to deliver, a tick to review, then the window $W=10$ — about thirteen ticks, or until final N. One extra delivery a tick in steady state is therefore about thirteen in flight and $D_s\approx260$, not "about three deliveries in flight… $D_s\approx68$". Two consequences for the text: the fund accumulates toward a reserve of a few hundred, not 108, before the uplift resumes — at an inflow of 3 of surcharge plus the earnings of what it already carries, that is a few tens of ticks, within the period — and Hypothesis 1's first clause ("the first period's accumulation and then near zero") is still the right expectation, because every carried delivery feeds the fund about +10 realised at vesting and the ramp is self-financing. The 1.4.0 §6 figure of 48 had the same understatement; run 6's steady troop deliveries of 0.8 a tick at 60/40 are consistent with a fund drained to a reserve near 50 carrying about two deliveries and a review at a time, which is what it did. Revise the example's numbers; the argument survives.

6. **§2.3 should attribute the leak to the gate that failed.** A probationer's false approval reaches a customer by one of two routes: the pinned audit *upheld* it (the auditor missed), or the audit *rejected* it and the ruling on the split overturned the rejection (the ruling erred — the 34 → 9 wrong rulings of the sweep, a sixth of the leak at drift). Attributing by auditor kind alone folds the second route into the first. The reading should record, per vested probation false approval: the route (audit upheld / audit rejected, ruling overturned), and for the first route the auditor's kind. On the kinds: a probationer never audits (initial only), but a *readmitted* member does, and a member suspended before the audit and readmitted is neither "in standing throughout" nor "inside its lag"; four kinds — troop; member never suspended in the run; member suspended at a later tick than the audit; member readmitted before the audit — or fold the fourth into the first member kind and say so. All of it is the driver's: it holds the reviewer id of every audit and sees every suspension, and classifies after the run as §7 says. No engine change; no rule.
