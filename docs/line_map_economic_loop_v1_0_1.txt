ARTIFACT "The economic loop — baseline v1.0" (type: flow) at revision 10
SUMMARY: One request, from the moment a requester pays for it to the moment the money is final. A requester pays in; an actor commits to a price and a delivery time and works at its own cost; a bonded reviewer judges the result; approvals are audited blind and rejections can be appealed; after a waiting window the case vests and payment is final. Delegation is the same loop one level down. The rules here are the frozen v1.0 baseline with the v1.0.1 errata applied (four wording corrections, no rule changed): they change only by an explicit design decision. What they do under self-interested participants is for simulation runs to show.

ELEMENTS
- [behaviour] flow:ruling: A human overrides or corrects
    st-0d1l1b5q6t5b (explanation, stated): The human is the battery and holds the plug. Two different things are called ruling. The orchestrator rules on procedural disagreements — a split between appeal or dispute reviewers — inside the ordinary loop. Only a human may override that loop, and this flow is the override. Delegates request, accept and dispute like any requester and hold no override authority. An override does not replace the loop; it is the one place truth enters from outside it. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:review-10, ev:spec-v101]
  - [step #1] step:ruling-before: Before vesting: the override replaces the live assessment
      st-1j334d5m0170 (outcome, stated): The whole ruling is checked first (for a cannot-assess, an upheld objection must come with not-fulfilled). Then every unfinished review is voided without fault and its bond returned whole; every finished verdict that contradicts the ruling is overturned — a reviewer's, or a delegating actor's rejection of its child, whose bond is then forfeited; a rejection the ruling agrees with is released. The case settles on the ruling. No audit or dispute can be opened after it: the ruling has already answered the question they would ask. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews, ev:spec-v1]
  - [step #2] step:ruling-after: After settlement: a correction moves no money
      st-1e1i2f481e1h (outcome, stated): The failure is recorded against whoever was wrong — by the full two-question test for a cannot-assess — and their standing takes the hit. A review already paid keeps its pay; a review finished but not yet paid is overturned as if the ruling had come earlier. Settled money stays where it is. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
- [rule] rule:scoped-runs: A run declares what it exercises
    st-6c125r4f1740 (condition, stated): A strategy run is about to start. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-6y3e1x0j3v2w (outcome, stated): It states its permitted operations first. A run on a subset — root cases only, fulfilled-or-not reviewers, human rulers, no post-settlement corrections — is legitimate and interpretable as long as it says what it leaves out. A result from a path the run excluded is not a result; a penalty the harness skipped or an answer it forced is an implementation effect, not an economic one. [evidence: ev:spec-v1, ev:reviews]
- [actor] actor:actor: Actor
    st-3y2t111h2d4m (explanation, documented): An autonomous worker. It offers to do a request for a price and by a time, puts up a guarantee, works at its own expense, and submits a result with the evidence a reviewer needs. Every actor also takes turns as a reviewer; there is no separate reviewer class. [evidence: ev:spec-v1]
- [behaviour] flow:delegate: An actor delegates part of its task
    st-3u296q2s0m6a (explanation, stated): A nested request is the whole loop again, one level down, with the delegating actor as requester. Accountability stays with it: the parent's result is judged on the parent's terms, so a child's failure is the parent's risk. [evidence: ev:discussion]
  - [step #1] step:nested-post: The actor posts a nested request from its own wallet
      st-5w3c46666456 (explanation, stated): The actor sets aside the child's budget plus a rejection bond of 5, and posts. Only while it is still executing — an actor that has submitted, or whose case has ended, cannot delegate. The child's value is in whatever credit price held when the parent's award was given: a nested case is denominated at the moment of its posting, the root at its award. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
  - [step #2] step:nested-loop: The child runs the full loop with the actor as its requester
      st-2b371a5s5i0m (explanation, stated): Offers, selection, a guarantee from the child actor, execution, a first review, a response window — all of it. There is no verification deposit on a nested case: its reviews draw on the root's deposit, so the chain pays for its own checking once. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
  - [step #3] step:nested-accept: The actor accepts the child's result, or rejects it on its bond
      st-0t1d720j5o6t (outcome, stated): Accepting releases the rejection bond. Rejecting turns the bond into the actor's reviewer bond for that rejection: if the child appeals and wins, the bond pays the child's fee and the rest burns, and the actor's standing takes the hit, just as a reviewer's would. The delegating actor is a requester, not a human — it can request, accept and dispute, but it cannot rule. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
  - [step #4] step:nested-milestone: The parent may submit once every child has reached a milestone
      st-2o5q5a5v174g (explanation, stated): A milestone is the child's first verdict, or any end state — including a child that was never delivered and ended in nonfulfillment. The parent is not blocked by a missing child; it carries that gap into its own submission and is judged on it. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
- [rule] rule:two-questions: An appeal of cannot-assess answers two questions
    st-6x5h2t4t6w5v (condition, stated): The verdict under appeal is cannot-assess. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-110w245u1p00 (outcome, stated): Each appeal reviewer states the objection's disposition and a fulfillment verdict. An upheld objection entails not fulfilled: what cannot be assessed cannot be fulfilled. An appeal reviewer is overruled if its disposition differs from the settled one, or, when the objection is rejected, if its verdict differs from the outcome. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
    st-0f4e3d2x5z0v (explanation, stated): Before this rule the appeal could only answer 'fulfilled or not', so a reviewer who correctly said the evidence was missing was always punished when the appeal came back not-fulfilled — backwards. An appeal of 'you didn't look' has to be decided by checking whether there was something to look at. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
- [behaviour] flow:audit: An approval is audited blind
    st-585w1r6a4z49 (explanation, stated): The surprise check. Without it, approving is free: no one but the requester can challenge an approval, and the requester is often silent. The orchestrator assigns audits but never performs them, because it also rules and would otherwise rule on its own finding. [evidence: ev:discussion]
  - [step #1] step:audit-assign: The orchestrator rolls the reviewer's audit rate and assigns a fresh auditor
      st-6t524u653g0q (explanation, stated): The approving reviewer cannot know which of its approvals will be checked. The verdict is written to the record before the auditor is chosen, so it cannot be changed afterwards. Assigning an audit holds the case open; a vacant audit position — the auditor timed out — keeps holding it until a replacement finishes, the orchestrator waives it, or a dispute takes it over. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
  - [step #2] step:audit-review: The auditor judges the submission, seeing no verdict
      st-1g094a4s5a0s (explanation, stated): The auditor receives the submission and its terms, not the first verdict, the challenge record, or anyone's identity, so it cannot anchor on the approval. It answers fulfilled or not. Its view will tell it that partial and cannot-assess are not available, from which it can work out it is auditing — that is the chosen trade-off, not a leak (see the information contract). [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
  - [step #3] step:audit-settle: Agreement is a second review; disagreement becomes the case's challenge
      st-0u5g425g6q4p (outcome, stated): If the auditor agrees, the approval now has two reviews behind it and the case goes on to vest; both reviewers are paid. If it disagrees, its verdict counts as one appeal review, one more fresh reviewer is assigned, and the orchestrator rules if they split — with the same consequences as an appeal. An audit agrees or disagrees with the case's live assessment, never with a verdict a ruling has already replaced. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
- [question] question:stamping: Does careful review actually beat stamping?
    st-6e3s0o4g321c (unknown, unresolved): This is the first thing a strategy run must answer, and the rulebook does not settle it. A reviewer that approves everything without reading, audited with probability p on submissions that fail at rate b, with expected surviving fee \( \bar f \) and bond \( G^V \), expects \[ \mathbb{E}[\Delta W] = (1 - pb)\,\bar f - pb\,G^V , \] which is negative only when \( G^V > \bar f \,(1-pb)/(pb) \). At the defaults it is positive: about +1.3 per review at p = 0.5, b = 0.2, fee 2, bond 5, and still +0.6 even when every approval is audited. Money alone does not make stamping unprofitable. What the rules rely on is reliability loss — an overturn lowers the reviewer's standing and so its future awards. Whether that is enough is unknown until it is run. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews, ev:spec-v1]
- [concept] concept:duty: Duty credit: a privilege for serving in the reserve
    st-0p6e19320d28 (explanation, stated): Ticks spent on reviewer duty over the trailing 100 ticks, vested on survival like reliability. It is a tie-breaker in selection and nothing more: it never adds money and never substitutes for reliability. Reviewing is useful to the society, so the reward is a better chance at tasks — Baruchi's framing — but a careless reviewer cannot buy standing with hours served. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
- [rule] rule:invariants: Eighteen things that must hold after every tick
    st-1e3n2x051958 (condition, stated): The end of every tick, in the reference implementation. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-5a2w2b4w311v (outcome, stated): Money is conserved: credits in wallets, holdings and the operating account equal what was issued less what was burned. No wallet is negative; nothing set aside exceeds what the wallet holds. Every open case has exactly one actor and every open review one reviewer; no reviewer sits twice on one path, and no path has more positions than the reserve can seat. A parent cannot submit before its children reach a milestone. Reliability and duty credit only count vested outcomes. The credit price equals reserves over supply, and purchases never move it. The history verifies, and every working copy matches it in content and order. Debts and claims reconcile. No audit outlives a ruling. Every label's grade matches its establishing author. A failing invariant is a bug in the engine or a contradiction in the rules — never a property of a strategy. [evidence: ev:spec-v1, ev:discussion]
- [rule] rule:emission: Emission pays for verification
    setting Starting emission per tick = 10 credits (default)
    setting Half-life = 1000 ticks (default)
    setting Unclaimed emission = not created — a reward nobody earned in a period is gone
    setting Weight per review = ordinary 1; appeal 1.5; audit 1.5 (default)
    st-550q2l5x4c3h (condition, stated): In every tick in which at least one review settles with its verdict standing. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-484d5m602s1k (outcome, stated): A fixed, slowly shrinking amount of new credits is created and shared among that tick's surviving reviews in proportion to their role weight. The schedule is \[ E_t = E_0 \cdot 2^{-t/H} \] so it halves every H ticks, and a review's share is \[ e_v = \frac{w_{r(v)}}{\sum_{v' \text{ settling at } t} w_{r(v')}} \, E_t . \] Over all time the total is about \( 1443 \, E_0 \). [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
    st-1c3k3q3e1r3h (explanation, stated): Verification is the economy's security work, so it is funded the way a block reward funds mining: a predictable drip that nobody decides, which fades as requester deposits take over. New credits are claims on the same reserve — they add no real resources. [evidence: ev:discussion]
- [rule] rule:exits: Every delegation has an exit
    setting Kill fee = the lesser of the child's spent cost and the orchestrator's 'reasonable cost' figure K
    setting Pool exhaustion = no eligible reviewer for 5 ticks (T_wait)
    setting Forfeiture on exhaustion = a quarter of the guarantee (ρ_pool = 0.25)
    st-0t3i225w2d2b (condition, stated): A nested case is open and the parent needs out, or the economy cannot serve it. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-1q2k3j452h07 (outcome, stated): Self-execution is always an exit: the actor may do the delegated work itself. Before the child submits, the parent may cancel for a kill fee that covers what the child has already spent, up to the reasonable-cost figure. If no reviewer can be found for long enough, either side may withdraw with a reduced forfeiture rather than wait forever. And whenever a case ends, by any route, everything set aside for it is released or settled in the same tick — nothing is left held. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
    st-5c365k2l533q (explanation, stated): Baruchi: 'no one is available, you can choose to do it yourself' — and a wait timeout that converts to nonfulfillment with reduced forfeiture is part of a real economy. The point is that no actor can be trapped by a child it cannot finish or a market that cannot serve it. [evidence: ev:discussion]
- [rule] rule:grades: Four grades of label, by who established it
    st-2t140j3n0e03 (condition, stated): A case reaches an end state. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-333l3s2l6r5f (outcome, stated): [corrected in revision 11] The outcome is graded by the author of the event that established it: human-explicit (a human's acceptance, winning dispute, or override), delegate-explicit (a root delegate's acceptance or winning dispute), procedural (a challenge, an audit, or a ruling on a split, with no matching root judgment; nested cases are always procedural), protocol-default (one first verdict, no challenge, no audit, silence). Nothing promotes between grades. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews, ev:spec-v101]
- [rule] rule:payout: How a case's reviewers are paid
    setting Reimbursement cap per review = 5 credits (default; all roles)
    setting Order of payment at settlement = cost debts of every review first; then fees to the reviewers whose verdicts stood; then unused deposit back to the requester
    st-50044m5f2t1j (condition, stated): A review completes (costs are paid at once) and later its case vests or ends in nonfulfillment (fees are paid then). [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-061p1p152u4u (outcome, stated): At completion a reviewer gets its costs back up to the cap, from the root's deposit — or, if the deposit is spent, advanced by the operating account, or deferred to settlement. At settlement each surviving review's fee entitlement is its emission share plus an equal share of the deposit left over: \[ \text{ent}_v = e_v + \frac{(\varphi - \text{Reimb})^+}{|\text{Surv}|} . \] Deferred costs and the operating account's advances are repaid first, from the pool. The two shortfalls are different debts: a reviewer cost the pool cannot cover becomes that reviewer's named claim on the operating account; an advance the pool cannot repay is the operating account's own loss — the reviewer was already paid from it, and no second claim arises. Then every entitlement is scaled down by the same factor, the pool left over divided by the pool before repayments. A reviewer whose verdict was overturned gets its costs back and nothing else, and loses its bond. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1, ev:review-10, ev:spec-v101]
    st-085i6e212z6v (explanation, stated): One rule for every case so that a shortfall never produces two incompatible instructions. The cost obligation of an overturned reviewer survives: the bond was the penalty, the cost was not. Nothing accrues at completion except costs — fee, reliability and duty credit all vest together when the verdict stands. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
- [rule] rule:audit-rate: How often a reviewer's approvals are audited
    setting New reviewer = every second approval, on average (0.5)
    setting After every 5 approvals in a row that stood = the rate halves, down to a floor of 0.2
    setting After any overturned verdict = the rate doubles, up to 0.5
    st-1c653f3h3b5r (condition, stated): Every time a reviewer's approval becomes pending. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-1b2w262r0a0w (outcome, stated): The orchestrator audits with the reviewer's current probability \( p_j \). Trusted reviewers are checked less; a reviewer who has just been overturned is checked more. Audits also produce labelled examples at a rate the orchestrator controls — most of the training signal it will ever have. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
- [rule] rule:change: How this baseline may change
    st-0t24625j6y3a (condition, stated): Someone finds something — a bug, a contradiction, a useful missing workflow, a strategy that behaves badly. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
    st-1j712j27224x (outcome, stated): The finding is classified before anyone acts on it. An admitted action that misapplies a rule, or a refused one with effects: repair the engine and add a regression test — the rules do not move. Two active rules demanding contradictory outcomes: the smallest explicit rule decision, versioned and reviewed as a rule change. A workflow that would be useful but is not offered: a proposal for a later design revision, not an obligation. A strategy that performs badly or an incentive that looks wrong: an evaluation under the fixed rules. A conformance pass may change how a rule is applied; a change to what participants may do is a design revision, labelled as one and read on its own before it is implemented. [evidence: ev:spec-v1, ev:reviews]
    st-5f313s5v1516 (explanation, stated): Written down because it was broken once: v0.9 answered an interface finding by letting audits send work back — a design change inside a conformance pass — and the next review found sixteen consequences at once. Baruchi's reading of the review: mainly wording. [evidence: ev:review-10, ev:discussion, ev:spec-v101]
- [rule] rule:challenge: One challenge round per case
    setting Challenge window = 10 ticks after the first verdict (default)
    setting Court fee = 2 credits, paid to the operating account at filing
    st-0h6q2g5q092u (condition, stated): A verdict is pending and the window is open. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-6j2g00612810 (outcome, stated): A case gets one challenge round: an appeal by the actor, a dispute by the requester, or an audit that disagrees — whichever comes first. The challenger pays the court fee up front; if it wins, the fee comes back out of the losing reviewer's bond; if it loses, the fee stays. There is no appeal against appeal reviewers. A challenge opened inside the window holds the case open until it is resolved. The grounds of a challenge are written down, recorded with it, and shown to the reviewers who decide it. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
- [rule] rule:one-guarantee: One guarantee, one obligation
    st-6e4a215c5n2k (condition, stated): An actor holds a case and has delegated part of it. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-3v706b1u3w5x (outcome, stated): The actor's guarantee backs its own promise to its requester, and only that. The child's guarantee backs the child's promise to the actor. Guarantees do not stack down a chain: a failure at the bottom costs the bottom actor its guarantee, and the parent what the parent promised — no more. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
- [behaviour] flow:case: One request, start to finish
    st-3g132z0w5o21 (explanation, documented): The ordinary path. Every step is in the reference implementation and reproduced by its tests. Delegation, audits, appeals and disputes branch off from it and rejoin it. [evidence: ev:spec-v1]
  - [step #1] step:post: A requester posts a request
      st-2r6z5j464o3q (explanation, documented): The request states a maximum delivery price and carries a pointer to the root request that funds and judges it. Before posting, the requester sees the all-in maximum: the price ceiling plus the verification deposit plus the service fee. A request an actor posts with no root behind it is only a proposal until a root requester sponsors it. [evidence: ev:spec-v1]
  - [step #2] step:offer: Actors offer a price and a delivery time
      st-5b413h4i0k6x (explanation, stated): An offer commits to a delivery price, a number of ticks to deliver, and a guarantee the actor will set aside. The price is what the actor will be paid, not a cap on what it may spend: if the work costs more, the overrun is the actor's. A low price does not lower the bar for fulfillment, and a big guarantee is not evidence the work will be good. [evidence: ev:spec-v1, ev:discussion]
  - [step #3] step:select: The orchestrator picks the winner and money moves into escrow
      st-4a5p0g154q0a (explanation, documented): The orchestrator selects for predicted fulfillment, discounted by price. The requester then pays the price, the verification deposit and the service fee into escrow; the actor sets its guarantee aside, untouchable until the case ends; every amount attached to the case is priced at that moment and frozen. From here the actor is committed and the delivery clock runs. [evidence: ev:spec-v1]
  - [step #4] step:execute: The actor works, at its own expense
      st-4b3u6s2a4425 (explanation, documented): Resources are bought with credits and burned. The actor may hire another actor for part of the work (see delegation), but must settle every such subcontract to its first verdict before submitting its own result. Only the committed actor's own spending counts as the case's cost, and only while the case is still being worked. [evidence: ev:spec-v1]
  - [step #5] step:submit: The actor submits the result with its evidence
      st-1l4s033n191e (explanation, stated): By the delivery deadline, inclusive. The submission carries whatever a competent reviewer needs — tests, logs, references — because the actor bears the burden of verifiability: a result that cannot be checked is the actor's failure, not the reviewer's. A missed deadline is a mechanical nonfulfillment with full forfeiture and no appeal. [evidence: ev:spec-v1, ev:discussion]
  - [step #6] step:review: A bonded reviewer gives the first verdict
      st-3q2q2r4e086s (explanation, stated): A reviewer from the duty reserve sets aside a bond and judges the submission. Four answers are possible on this first review: fulfilled, not fulfilled, partially fulfilled (the actor may revise once), or cannot assess (the reviewer names the missing evidence; the actor may supply it or contest the claim). Only this first review of an unrevised submission may send the actor back; every later review answers fulfilled or not. [evidence: ev:spec-v1, ev:discussion]
  - [step #7] step:respond: The actor responds; approval or an appeal frees it
      st-030v544q5k0x (explanation, documented): After a fulfilled verdict there is nothing to do. After a rejection the actor may appeal or accept. After partial or cannot-assess it chooses, within the response window, to revise or supply evidence — then resubmits within a second window to the same reviewer, who now answers only fulfilled or not. What frees the actor is an approval or an admitted appeal: the case is then pending, and the actor may take other work or review duty while its guarantee stays set aside. Choosing to revise or supply evidence does not free it — the actor stays committed through that work and the assessment that follows. Accepting a rejection, or letting the window pass, ends the case. [evidence: ev:spec-v1, ev:review-10, ev:spec-v101]
  - [step #8] step:window: The challenge window runs; audits and disputes may open
      st-0t6e555h1a26 (explanation, documented): For a fixed number of ticks after the verdict the case is open to challenge. The orchestrator may send an approval to a blind auditor; the requester may dispute it. Any challenge opened inside the window holds the case open until it is resolved — the window does not close over an open challenge. Silence is acceptance by default. [evidence: ev:spec-v1]
  - [step #9] step:vest: The case vests: payment is final
      st-0w6i0k676y3q (explanation, stated): When the window has closed with nothing open and the verdict standing, the actor is paid the price, its guarantee is released, and the case's reviews settle: costs that were deferred are paid first, then fees to the reviewers whose verdicts stood, and whatever deposit was not needed goes back to the requester. Reliability and duty credit vest on the same event. After this no adjudication moves money; a later human correction changes the record and reliability only. [evidence: ev:spec-v1, ev:discussion]
  - [step #10] step:final-n: Or the case ends in nonfulfillment
      st-5h2s702v5o3p (outcome, documented): Deadline missed, rejection accepted or left unanswered, appeal lost, dispute upheld, or a human ruling of not fulfilled. The guarantee goes to the requester, the escrowed price returns to the requester, nothing is paid for the work, and the case's reviews settle the same way as at vesting. A nonfulfillment counts against the actor's reliability. [evidence: ev:spec-v1]
- [actor] actor:orchestrator: Orchestrator
    st-5h3a4m17095e (explanation, stated): Holds the pool of requests, picks the winning offer, assigns reviews and audits, holds the money in escrow, and rules when two appeal or dispute reviewers disagree — a procedural ruling inside the loop, not the human override. A judge and an escrow — not a bank (it never prints money on demand), not a first-line reviewer, and not an auditor. [evidence: ev:spec-v1, ev:discussion]

- [rule] rule:boundary: Permitted, forbidden, and not offered
    st-485i1g726x5q (condition, stated): Any participant attempts an action. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-5u2v012h1q59 (outcome, stated): Permitted: post, offer, execute, spend while committed, submit, revise, respond to a cannot-assess, review with the available responses, appeal, dispute, assign and waive an audit, accept or reject a child, self-execute, cancel for the kill fee, withdraw on an exhausted pool, rule (humans only), correct after settlement. Forbidden, and refused whole before any effect: a delegate ruling; an audit or dispute after a ruling; partial or cannot-assess on anything but a first review of an unrevised submission; a second challenge round; spending by anyone but the committed actor; an appeal of a partial. [evidence: ev:spec-v1, ev:reviews]
    st-3k3j290q5k3s (explanation, stated): Every refused action is tested for atomicity: it either completes under its rules or changes nothing. 'Not offered' is kept separate from 'forbidden' because a workflow the version does not support is a proposal for a later revision, not an obligation to support now — the ninth review's framing. [evidence: ev:reviews, ev:discussion]
- [concept] concept:reliability: Reliability: how often an actor's work stood
    st-0y2x4p4h4o6v (explanation, stated): Over the last 100 outcomes an actor was party to, as actor or reviewer, with \( s_i \) that stood and \( o_i \) that were overturned or forfeited, \[ \rho_i = \frac{s_i + 1}{s_i + o_i + 2} . \] A newcomer starts at one half and moves with evidence. Outcomes count only once they survive the challenge window and vest — a verdict that is later overturned never improves the record. Reliability feeds selection: it is the \( \rho_i \) inside the selection score, so a reviewer who stamps approvals loses future work, and that — not the bond — is the deterrent the rules rely on. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
- [actor] actor:requester: Requester
    st-5v6z413t6c2f (explanation, stated): The one who wants the work and pays for it. A root requester is a human, or a delegate — a system spending a human's budget. A derived requester is an actor that needs a piece of its own task done by another actor. Requesters pay in, accept, and dispute. A delegate does all of that, but it holds no override authority and its acceptance is a model's judgment, graded as such: only a human's judgment is not one model's opinion of another. [evidence: ev:spec-v1, ev:discussion, ev:review-10]

    st-4v4e6x3o3033 (explanation, documented): An actor on verification duty. It judges a submission against the request, puts up a bond on its verdict, is paid its costs at once and a fee only if its verdict stands, and loses the bond if the verdict is overturned. [evidence: ev:spec-v1]
- [behaviour] flow:appeal: The actor appeals a rejection or a cannot-assess
    st-1d6e1n266x5f (explanation, stated): The actor's path when it believes the first reviewer was wrong. Two fresh reviewers decide; the orchestrator rules only if they split. [evidence: ev:discussion]
  - [step #1] step:appeal-file: The actor pays the court fee and states its grounds
      st-19416x576b11 (explanation, stated): Within the response window, after a not-fulfilled or a cannot-assess. A partially-fulfilled verdict cannot be appealed — the actor revises or accepts. The fee, the grounds and the actor's ability to pay are all checked before anything changes. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
  - [step #2] step:appeal-review: Two fresh reviewers answer
      st-1f5u5d4e0y1y (explanation, stated): They see the submission, the verdict under appeal and the actor's grounds. For an appealed rejection they answer fulfilled or not. For an appealed cannot-assess they answer two questions, both stated outright: was the evidence really missing (the objection upheld or rejected), and is the task fulfilled. One answer is never inferred from the other. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
  - [step #3] step:appeal-settle: The appeal settles, or the orchestrator rules on a split
      st-5w2f1i5m2r0p (outcome, stated): Matching answers settle it. A rejection overturned: the actor is reimbursed its fee from the first reviewer's bond, that reviewer loses the rest of the bond and its audit rate doubles, and the case goes back to pending. A rejection upheld: the actor's guarantee is forfeited and it bears the fee. For a cannot-assess: if both say the evidence was present, the first reviewer is overturned and their fulfillment answer decides; if both say it was missing, the first reviewer was right and is paid as such, the actor bears the fee, and the case ends in nonfulfillment. A split on either question goes to the orchestrator, who states both answers. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
- [rule] rule:guarantee: The actor's guarantee
    setting Size = half the delivery price, rounded up, at least 1 (default)
    setting Reduced forfeiture on pool exhaustion = a quarter of the guarantee (default)
    st-5208385y3e1e (condition, stated): The winning actor sets the guarantee aside from its free balance at award; it stays set aside until the case ends. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-6w6u1o3k5d1a (outcome, stated): Returned when the case vests. Handed to the requester in full on any final nonfulfillment — a missed deadline, an accepted rejection, a lost appeal, an upheld dispute, a ruling. A quarter is handed over and the rest returned when the actor withdraws because nobody would take its subcontract in time. An execution overrun on its own never touches it. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]

- [rule] rule:credit: The credit is backed and floats
    setting Starting value = 1 credit = 1 real unit
    setting Starting reserve = 10,000 real units, paid in by the sponsor who is the first requester
    setting Cold-start float = 50 credits per actor and 20 for the operating account, paid once from the sponsor's credits
    st-184l2p5r2q6s (outcome, stated): A credit is worth the real reserve divided by the credits in circulation: \[ v_t = \frac{\mathrm{Res}_t}{M_t} . \] A root pay-in adds to both and leaves the value unchanged. Buying a resource burns \( c / v_t \) credits and pays the real cost c from the reserve — also unchanged, since \( \frac{\mathrm{Res} - c}{M - c/v} = v \). Only emission lowers the value; only burned bonds raise it. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
    st-0g1e241a5k5z (explanation, stated): Purchases are value-neutral. Emission lowers the credit's value and burned bonds raise it, so the credit price of a resource with a fixed real cost moves with both — never with purchases. With no human at the root the only income is a finite emission, and real spending is bounded by the reserve: the reserve and the supply can only exhaust together. A reserve of zero is the halt state — the credit is worth nothing by definition, never zero-over-zero. Idle costs nothing. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews, ev:review-10, ev:spec-v101]
    st-6n4x00710v34 (explanation, stated): Every amount attached to a case — court fee, reimbursement cap, reviewer bond, deposit — is converted at the credit's value the first time money is set aside for the case, and then frozen for the case's life, so a later swing in the credit can never make a court fee larger than the bond already set aside to pay it. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
    st-414j2f04386s (condition, stated): Whenever credits are created, spent on resources, or burned — continuously, through the life of the economy. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-halt-hypothesis (explanation, hypothesis): That a rootless economy actually reaches the halt state — runs out of wallet rather than idling with funds held elsewhere, or ceasing to spend before the reserve is gone — is a claim about behavior for the runs to test (item 7 of what a run must find), not a consequence of the rules. The rules bound spending; they do not force it. [evidence: ev:review-10, ev:spec-v101]
- [actor] actor:human: The human
    st-532p3i6p1o36 (explanation, stated): The battery and the plug. Human requests are the only money that is not emission, and a human's judgment is the only judgment that is not one model's opinion of another. A human can interrupt any case — dispute, call an audit, override the loop's assessment, or freeze — and every interruption is a recorded human event. With no human request pending the correct state of the whole economy is idle. [evidence: ev:spec-v1, ev:discussion]
- [concept] concept:omega: The operating account
    st-4f0i5u15041b (explanation, stated): A revolving float, not a treasury. It receives service fees and court fees, advances reviewers' costs when a deposit runs out, is repaid from the case's fee pool to the extent the pool covers, and pays its outstanding claims to reviewers first in, first out, partly if it must, every time money comes in. Each claim is at most one reimbursement cap; the total outstanding is watched, not capped. An advance a case could not repay is the account's own loss, not a reviewer's claim, and is watched alongside them. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
- [concept] concept:record: The record: one append-only history
    st-055e2r5u495k (explanation, stated): Every admitted action — a posting, an offer, a verdict, a challenge with its grounds, a ruling, a payment — is written to one hash-chained history before anything else happens, and never rewritten. Wallet event lists and case logs are working copies; each is checked against its committed entries for membership and order, so an omission or a reordering is caught, not only an edit. The past is append-only; the present is committed; the future is mutable. What is not done: the head of the chain is not published outside the system. That is bracketed, not decided. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews, ev:spec-v1]

    st-0a3g0k6y4q0x (explanation, stated): The requester's path when it believes an approved result does not meet its request. Same machinery as an appeal: a fee, two fresh reviewers, the orchestrator on a split. [evidence: ev:discussion]
  - [step #1] step:dispute-file: The requester pays the fee and states its grounds, inside the window
      st-513b322c5f2a (explanation, stated): Only for a root case, and never after a human has ruled on it.       st-3l14732b0s46 (outcome, stated): Upheld: the approving reviewer's bond pays the requester's fee and the rest burns; the actor's case ends in nonfulfillment. - [rule] rule:reserve: The reviewer reserve is kept filled
    setting Reserve size X = 4 on duty
    setting Most positions on one path V_max = 3 (first review, two challenge reviews)
    setting Rotation = lowest duty credit among those off duty at the start of the tick comes in; a replacement is seated before anyone is released
    st-534070322m3r (condition, stated): At the rotation point of every tick, or when a review needs a fresh reviewer. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-4r19265d0p57 (outcome, stated): The reserve is one larger than the most positions any case can need, so a case can always be reviewed by people who have not touched it. A reviewer is unavailable because it is occupied (holding an open review) or because it is conflicted (already on this case's path); a shortfall of the first kind waits, of the second kind is a design error and an invariant catches it.     st-0a492h2u5e5v (explanation, stated): Reviewers are drawn from the same population as actors — the surprise check is done by peers, not by a separate class — so the reserve is what keeps verification available while everyone is also trying to earn. [evidence: ev:discussion]
- [rule] rule:bond: The reviewer's bond is its whole liability
    setting Bond per verdict = 5 credits (default; frozen per case)
    setting Court fee = 2 credits (default; always at most the bond)
    setting Review deadline = 5 ticks after assignment, paused while the actor acts (default)
    st-1s4c41240625 (condition, stated): Taking any review — first review, appeal review, or audit — sets the bond aside for the life of the verdict. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-1669300f0w6m (outcome, stated): If the verdict is overturned, a winning challenger's court fee is paid out of the bond and the rest is burned; the reviewer earns no fee and its reliability falls. If the verdict stands, the bond is returned at settlement and the fee is paid. A wrong approval and a wrong rejection cost exactly the same. A reviewer that misses its deadline is withdrawn: half the bond burns, half returns, no costs are paid, and its position is filled by someone else. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
    st-2o5l5d6j716v (explanation, stated): The first draft punished only wrong rejections, so a reviewer's best move was to approve everything without reading. Because the court fee comes out of the bond, no reviewer can ever owe more than it set aside. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews]
- [concept] concept:lifecycle: The states a case passes through
    st-583h70006w6a (explanation, stated): Committed (an actor holds it and may spend on it — including while it is choosing how to respond to a partial or cannot-assess, and while a revision or evidence is in progress) → pending (the submission was approved or an appeal was admitted; the actor's capacity is released at that moment, not at payment) → suspended (a challenge or audit is open and holds it) → vested (financial finality: paid, labelled, done) or final nonfulfillment. A case never goes back from pending to committed: the only road back to work is a partial or cannot-assess on the first review, and that road never leaves committed. Deadlines are inclusive: an action on the last tick of a window counts. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:reviews, ev:spec-v1, ev:review-10]
    st-6j715t4e3r5z (explanation, stated): The order within a tick is fixed so that no two rules race: participants act; challenges are filed; deadlines expire; vesting and payouts run as one batch; the operating account services its claims; the reserve rotates; new reviews are assigned. A tick is the unit of time; physical time is bracketed. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
- [rule] rule:pay-in: What a request costs the requester

    setting Verification deposit, nested request = 0 — nested reviews draw on the root's deposit

    st-4y4p676t2l72 (condition, stated): A reviewer is assigned to any review. [evidence: ev:discussion]

    st-4b130l3e444m (unknown, unresolved): Eight things only a run can answer, none of them settled by the rules: (1) whether careful review beats stamping once reliability loss is counted, and at which audit odds, bond and fee it stops; (2) whether \( \lambda = 0.2 \) produces price competition without starving reliable actors; (3) whether a deposit of twice the reasonable cost covers root-plus-nested verification, how often the operating account must advance, and how large its outstanding claim grows; (4) whether total emission of about \( 1443\,E_0 \) against the starting supply is a subsidy or a flood; (5) whether delegation trees of two to four nodes keep moving under the wait limit, the kill fee and the rejection bond's capital cost; (6) how often the reserve runs short by occupancy versus by solvency, and whether fees restore it; (7) how the credit price moves with the human request rate, and whether an economy with no human at the root reaches the halt state through empty wallets, as hypothesized, or idles short of it; (8) any cheap dominant strategy the rules missed. - [rule] rule:settlement: What each party is left with after a case
    st-5v500u4a4u2o (condition, stated): A case vests or ends in nonfulfillment. [evidence: ev:discussion]
    st-53061z2j076s (outcome, stated): The actor's wallet changes by its award less what it spent, less a court fee it paid and lost, plus a fee it won back, less its guarantee if it failed, less a quarter of its guarantee if it withdrew on an exhausted pool: \[ \Delta W_{\text{actor}} = P - C - I_D\, D + I_W\, D - I_N\, G - I_{\text{pool}}\, \rho_{\text{pool}}\, G . \] A reviewer's wallet changes by its reimbursed cost (up to the reasonable-cost figure, and only if its verdict stood) less what it spent, plus its fee if the verdict stood, less its whole bond if overturned, less half the bond if it timed out: \[ \Delta W_{V} = I_{\text{paid}}\, \min(\text{spent}, K) - \text{spent} + I_{\text{stood}}\, f - I_{\text{over}}\, G^V - I_{\text{late}}\, \tfrac{1}{2} G^V . \] Each \( I \) is one when its condition holds and zero otherwise. The requester has already paid in; nothing further is owed either way. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
    st-0o6v6s246148 (explanation, stated): The two sums are the whole money story of a case from each side. Everything a strategy run measures is some average of these. [evidence: ev:discussion]
- [concept] concept:decided: What the October 14 proposal map asked, and what this baseline answers
    st-010m3a0i6i2v (explanation, stated): The earlier map 'Agentic Economics — Proposal (WIP)' left most of the economy open on purpose. This baseline decides a loop, not the whole economy. Who creates money: no one mints at will — credits enter with human requests, the orchestrator emits by a fixed decaying schedule and cannot print more. Why value a credit: it buys execution, and it is backed by the reserve at a value that purchases cannot move. Does the orchestrator have a wallet and who holds it accountable: it has an operating account that advances verification costs and is repaid first-in-first-out, bearing what a case cannot repay; it assigns audits but never performs them; it rules on splits inside the procedure, and only a human may override it. What occupies a task slot: the case, until its submission is approved or its appeal admitted — a revision keeps it; capacity is released then, not at payment. Collateral and bonds: a guarantee of half the award, a flat reviewer bond that is the reviewer's whole liability, and a rejection bond when delegating. All actors verify: a reserve of four, rotation by duty credit, replacement before release. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]
    st-0i6e2h246g35 (explanation, stated): Accountability down a chain: one guarantee, one obligation — consequences do not stack. Dispute mechanics: one challenge round, a court fee, two fresh reviewers, the orchestrator on a split, no appeal against appeal reviewers. Rating: reliability over the last 100 vested outcomes with a flat prior, feeding selection; the learned part of the score is bracketed, so Thompson-style selection is neither adopted nor ruled out. Who pays for rejected work: the actor loses its guarantee if it fails; a requester whose dispute loses bears the fee, and its disagreement stays on the record as a signal — repair cost allocation beyond that is not a mechanism here. Left open as before, now explicitly bracketed: rent, upgrades, insurance, bankruptcy beyond the operating account's bounded claim, user accountability, what the economy adds over simpler assignment — the last is what the runs are for. [evidence: ev:discussion, ev:spec-v1]

GLOSSARY
- Case: One request from posting to its end: a root case comes from a human; a nested case is one an actor posted to delegate part of its own.
- Award: The price the winning offer named; what the actor is paid if its result stands. Written B in the equations.
- Guarantee: Half the award, rounded up, set aside by the actor when selected; returned when the case vests, forfeited to the requester on nonfulfillment.
- Reviewer bond: A flat 5 credits set aside by every reviewer; its whole liability. On an overturned verdict it pays the challenger's court fee and the rest is burned.
- Verification deposit: Twice the reasonable-cost figure, paid in by a root requester to fund the reviews of its case and every case nested under it; the unused part is shared among reviewers whose verdicts stood.
- Operating account: The orchestrator's revolving float (Ω). It advances verification cost when a deposit falls short and is repaid first-in-first-out from every inflow; it earns nothing.
- Emission: New credits the orchestrator issues each tick on a fixed halving schedule, shared among the tick's vesting reviews by role weight. Unspent emission is not carried forward.
- Credit price: Reserves divided by supply. What one credit buys in execution. Purchases leave it unchanged; emission lowers it; burned bonds raise it. Zero reserves means the economy halts.
- Pending: A case whose submission was approved, or whose appeal was admitted, with its challenge window open. The actor's capacity is released at that moment; its guarantee stays set aside. A revision in progress is not pending — the actor is still committed.
- Suspended: A case held open by a challenge or an audit until it is resolved.
- Vested: Financial finality: the case is paid, labelled and done. Only a correction can follow, and it moves no money.
- Audit: A blind second review of an approval, assigned by the orchestrator at a per-reviewer rate; agreement is a second settled review, disagreement becomes the case's one challenge.
- Cannot-assess: The verdict ⊥: the reviewer says the evidence needed to judge is missing. Available only on the first review of an unrevised submission; appealed by answering two questions.
- Reliability: ρ: an actor's share of outcomes that stood over its last 100 vested outcomes, with a flat prior. Enters selection; the loop's real deterrent.
- Duty credit: κ: ticks served in the reviewer reserve over the trailing 100. A tie-breaker in selection and nothing more.
- Milestone: A child case's first verdict or any end state. A parent may submit only once every child has reached one.
- Label grade: Who established an outcome: human-explicit (a human accepted, won a dispute, or overrode), delegate-explicit (a root delegate accepted or won a dispute), procedural (a challenge, audit or split ruling, with no matching root judgment; all nested cases), or protocol-default (one verdict, silence). Says how much a learner may trust the label.
- Bracketed: Outside this baseline on purpose: neither decided nor forbidden, and a run that touches it must say so.
