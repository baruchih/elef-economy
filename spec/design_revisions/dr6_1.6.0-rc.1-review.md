# Review of 1.6.0-rc.1 — design revision 6, "auto-scale"

**Date:** October 17, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_1.6.0-rc.1.md` · **Against:** the 1.5.0 text in force, the engine at `01b75e2`, run 7 (`agentic_sim/results/studies/1.5.0-reserve/`) · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict.** The diagnosis is run 7's and the five rules answer it in the right places: the seat is sized on what waits (§2.3), the state can reach it (§2.2), the customer's wait has a price and an owner (§2.1), the auditor's record is required before it judges (§2.4), and the reset that saw-toothed is withdrawn (§2.5). Each is implementable; the engine has the state each one needs. rc.1 is not implementable as written on three points: §2.4 as stated refuses every panel assignment in every world that has not planted, which is every reviewer-authored test and the first period of every run (1); §2.2's order of payment names a plant cap the engine does not have, so under stress the scaling it authorises will spend the honesty clock's capital (2); and Hypothesis 1 asks for a vesting rate above the arithmetic ceiling, which misreads what the seat costs — the queue, not the rate (3). Four more need a word (4–7), and one thing about the shape of the revision (8).

## What checks out

- Every run-7 figure in §1 (4.5–5 on duty; 7,300–11,400; 2.2–2.4; 290–530; ~300 open; 3,568 against 270; 94 against 13; 3,135 and 3,146; 2,112 / 2,186 / 2,063; 85–90% upheld; 55–75% and 73–80%; 2–3, under 1, 10–15%; 901–2,040; 2,479–3,958).
- **Priority is already the engine's order.** The summons passes iterate the live cases in creation order, which for root requests is posting order; the stamping driver awards in case-id order. "Oldest clock first" is a statement of what happens, so the rule costs nothing and can be checked (I26).
- **The clock has its anchors.** `Case.t_post` exists; the first verdict is one place (`_initial_verdict`, F or N; P and ⊥ are responses); the per-tick loop over open requests exists in the summons pass, so a tick with $\beta<\bar B$ can be excluded by a counter. A rebate is one transfer, Ω → requester, at the verdict; a liability is a counter on Ω serviced where Ω already services its claims on cases.
- **Seat the queue is a per-seating maximum.** $X_t$ is sized once a period today (`max(V_max+1, ⌈r_t d̄⌉)`); the rotation fills to it in the 1.3.0 order, probationers to initial seats first. Taking `max(X_t, waiting + expected)` at each rotation is a few lines and I9's amendment is the predicate.
- **Inspection first and the delivery check** are a reordering of one pass and one `need` figure; the demand stock keeping the delivery's own 20 is right (a check, not a hold).
- **The two-period reserve** is the 1.4.0 engine's reading over 1.5.0's $D_s$; the code for both halves exists.
- **The duty-cap sweep** is a fraction in `_member_cap`, which already floors at $V_{\max}+1$.
- **The red team's cases** are each one driver flag or none: a below-market poster (`--beta` on a share of requests), a flood (`--tasks`), a late withdrawer (`withdraw_unawarded` exists; see 6).
- The classification as minor is defensible on the text's own ground — the one change to what a member may do is §2.4, and the newcomer pin already said an undetermined approval is not trusted. See 8 for what it costs.

## Must fix before rc.2

1. **§2.4 as written refuses every panel assignment in a world without plants.** `panel_eligible` today is duty-eligible, not on probation, and `record_passes(θ_panel)` — and an *undetermined* record passes. Requiring determination (`len(plant_bad) ≥ n_min`) makes every member of every reviewer-authored test world panel-ineligible, since those worlds never plant: every `assign_audit` and `assign_appeal` in them is refused, and some sixty tests fail. The text's fallback — "undetermined members are panel-eligible in a world with plants off" — cannot be read by the engine: those worlds have the default $\pi_0=0.1$ and simply never receive a plant; "plants off" is not a state the engine can see. The same applies to the first period of *every* run: at tick 0 nobody is determined, so every panel seat is a troop's until records form, which §2.4 and Hypothesis 5 accept but which §1–3 do not reckon with (see 3). **The reading I would implement:** the requirement is dormant until the world's first plant has been adjudicated — a market whose clock has not started cannot have asked anyone to prove a record — and from then on it is a Rule the engine applies at assignment and at the summons. Reviewer-authored worlds never plant and are untouched; the implementer's own tests plant and will be revised. Alternatively a `Params.panel_determined` flag defaulting on, with the same dormancy. Either way the text must say which, and must say the cold-start consequence in §2.4's own words: *in every run the first period's panels are the state's.*

   **And the arithmetic of determination should be in the text.** A record determines on $n_{\min}=4$ bad plants *seen on duty*; an actor off duty sees none. Bad plants arrive at about $\pi_t\times$ initial reviews a tick $\times\beta_P$ — 0.3 a tick at $m=1$, 0.6 at $m=2$ — shared among the five or so on duty, and the duty turn is three reviews or thirty ticks. A member is determined after roughly 70 on-duty ticks at $m=1$, spread over several turns; eight careful members at 60/40 need 32 bad plants between them, 100–200 ticks. So the "cold start" of Hypothesis 5 is the first period, sometimes two, of every run at every split, and the §8 comparisons against run 7 must be read from the second period on, or the first period's all-troop panels will be mistaken for the rule's steady effect. Note also what §2.4 does *not* change: a careful member that slips once on its first four bad plants has $\hat a=2/6>0.2$ and is already panel-ineligible under 1.3.0 until its record dilutes ($n\ge8$); about a fifth of careful members pass through that state at determination.

2. **§2.2's order of payment names a cap the engine does not have, and without one the scaling spends the honesty clock.** "Plant capital under its cap (the honesty clock first)" — there is no plant cap: `create_plant` takes 15 from Ω's unlocked balance and is refused when Ω is short (`plants_unfunded`, run 4's finding). Within a tick the driver supplies plants in the action phase and the engine advances at step 5, so plants come first *within* a tick by accident of order; but the advances of tick $t$ are what the plants of tick $t+1$ find, and with $A_T$ removed they can take Ω to zero. Then the dial goes blind exactly under capture — the stress that summons the troops is the stress that stops the plants that would detect it. The rule must hold back a **plant reserve**: Ω advances only from its balance above the next period's expected plant capital (`plants_due` per tick $\times$ 15 $\times$ the period's remaining ticks, or a simpler $\pi_t\cdot$ initial rate $\cdot$ 15 $\cdot$ period length); rebate liabilities likewise. Say it as a rule and as a reading ("plants unfunded for want of Ω" must read zero in every run, or the finding is that the float could not carry both the clock and the army). On the order itself: ranking a debt to a customer (the accrued rebate) *below* new discretionary advances is a choice I would not make silently — a liability is owed; an advance is optional. If the text keeps advances first, it should say why (scaling reduces future lateness) and bound the liability's age; otherwise liabilities before advances.

3. **Hypothesis 1 asks for a vesting rate above the ceiling, and so misstates what the seat costs.** Three tasks a tick at $b=0.2$ gives at most $3\times0.8=2.4$ vestings a tick plus the false approvals (about 0.05): a ceiling near 2.45. The 1.2.0 control at 50/50 is *at* the ceiling (2,399), and run 7's 2,175–2,375 is at or within a few percent of it; "toward 2.7" cannot happen, and "above the 1.2.0 control" is a rounding error at best. What the seat binds is **the queue** — the ~300 open cases and the latency of each — not the long-run rate: at 60/40 posted 3,000, vested 2,186, final N about 500, open about 300. Restate Hypothesis 1 as: reviews waiting fall from 7,300–11,400 case-ticks to a few hundred; the open-case queue falls from ~300 to the in-flight count (a few tens); vesting rises by the backlog cleared (a few percent) toward the ceiling; and the state's share rises to the unmet delivery demand. §18.1 Q11's "measured against the posting rate" is right; its yardstick is the queue and the on-time share, not vestings a tick. (This also sharpens §2.1's case: the harm the seat does is lateness, which is exactly what the clock prices.)

4. **The delivery check and the advance it draws.** `_fund_can_carry(need)` draws Ω's advance for `need − fund.U`. With `need = 36` the advance would be drawn for 36 while 20 is spent, and the 16 would sit in the fund until the batch repays it — not wrong, but it inflates "advance outstanding" and the fund's balance by a figure that means nothing. Say: the check is against the fund's unlocked balance *plus Ω's available advance*; the advance drawn is the delivery's own shortfall. §6's "20 + 16 = 36 against the fund's unlocked balance; the fund is short, Ω advances the difference" should read "the difference on 20".

5. **A request with no first verdict.** A delivery that misses its deadline is final N without a review (`_finalize_N`); a troop's delivery likewise. Its clock never meets a verdict. Settle the clock at the first verdict *or at the case's final settlement without one*, whichever comes first — the customer waited either way — and say which figure the rebate uses when there is no verdict (lateness to the final, I would say).

6. **"Withdrawn before its first verdict."** The only withdrawal is `withdraw_unawarded`: a request can be withdrawn while AVAILABLE and not after award. So the clause is "withdrawn before award" — say that, and note the consequence: a requester cannot escape the clock's benefit after award and cannot farm it before, which is the point. The red team's "requester that withdraws late requests" is then a requester that withdraws *unawarded* ones, which costs it nothing and gains it nothing.

7. **§8.8's cold start needs no arrivals process.** The engine has no operation to add an actor to a running world, and adding one is an interface change the drivers' view would have to carry. It is also unnecessary: every run *is* a cold start for records — all twenty actors are undetermined at tick 0 (see 1) — so Hypothesis 5 is measured in the first period of every §8 run, by period. Drop the arrivals process, or make §8.8 "the first two periods of the tipping runs, read by period" and keep the budget for the sweeps. If an arrivals process is wanted later, it is a new public operation and a design question of its own.

## Smaller

- **Five rules in one revision, and what the runs can then say.** Run 7 could attribute because 1.5.0 changed one thing. 1.6.0 changes five; a tipping result against run 7 will not say which rule did what. The engine should carry each as a `Params` flag — `service_clock`, `omega_backs_fund`, `seat_the_queue`, `panel_determined`, `reserve_periods` — all on by default, and §8 should include one-factor-at-a-time cells against the 1.5.0 engine (`--rules 1.5.0` as the control, each flag toggled alone at 60/40). It costs 100 runs and buys attribution. On the text's own classification: §2.3 and §2.5 are conformance-shaped fixes to sizing and a reading; §2.1, §2.2 and §2.4 are the design. If the OFAT cells show §2.3 alone unbinds the seat, that is worth knowing before Ω's backing is judged.
- **§2.1's clock exclusion** ($\beta<\bar B$) is dead in the drivers — every offer is at $\beta$, so $\bar B=\beta$ — as 1.4.0 already noted of the price rule. Say so, as 1.4.0 did; the red team's below-market poster is the first driver to exercise it and needs `--beta` to vary per request.
- **The rebate's cap at $B$** means a request late by more than $B/r_s=20$ ticks is owed nothing further; at 80/20 with Ω₀ = 300 the text expects lateness "near ten", so the cap rarely binds — but a flood would, and the cap is what keeps I26's liability bounded by $B\times$ open requests. Worth one sentence.
- **I26's conservation clause** should name the wallets: rebates paid are Ω → requester transfers; liabilities are a counter on Ω, not credits; so "rebates paid plus liabilities accrued equal rebates owed" is a bookkeeping identity over the counter, not a money conservation — I1 covers the transfers.
- **§2.3's "expected this tick"**: the engine knows the period's initial reviews so far (`period["initial"]`) and its ticks; the expected count is that rate. Say which; or drop it and size on the waiting count alone, which is the number that bit.
- **§2.6's cap sweep:** at $E_t$ ("all") no member is ever free to deliver, so every award is the state's and $\bar B=\beta$ by construction; at ⌈2E_t/3⌉ at 60/40 that is six of eight. The text says it is a product decision; the sweep should also read members' income by source at each, which the driver has.
- **Goldens:** re-pinned once, as always; the equivalence scenario plants, so §2.4 will reseat its panels.
- **`A_T` kept as the 1.5.0 control**: `Params.A_T` stays with the 1.5.0 default and `omega_backs_fund=False` means "A_T binds"; `--rules 1.5.0` sets it.
