# Review of 2.1.0-rc.1 — design revision 9, "the bank"

**Date:** October 19, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_2.1.0-rc.1.md` · **Against:** the 2.0.0 text in force, the engine at `9628b941` (driver `e05cf3fb`), runs 12–13 · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict. Implementable, with one correction and a handful of readings.** The revision is what the two runs asked for and no more: a second account that mints by rule and lends against what returns, the state's burn set to its expected cost with its price left at $\bar B$ (the run 13 warning taken), rebates back to 1.6.0's liability stock, the index once a period, every refusal told. The §6 summons arithmetic checks to the unit: $c^D_T=0.8\times30-0.2\times15=21$, the loan 32, the vesting's $45-32=13$ to Ω, a success $+9=b(\bar B+G)$ and a failure $-36=(1-b)(\bar B+G)$, $144\times9=36\times36$. Nothing contradicts 2.0.0 except one sentence about plants, which contradicts 1.3.0 instead and is the correction below. The readings are stated so the text can say otherwise before the engine fixes them.

## The correction — plants are netted, and the sweep must not reach Ω

§2.3 says the bank "mints the plant's all-in (its award at the plant's price and its pots)" and that "what returns at the plant's closure — the pots' refunds — goes back to the bank and is destroyed"; §6 mints 45 for a plant and destroys 7. Since 1.3.0 §2.5 (`net_plant_capital`, in force) a plant's award and guarantee are *notional* — Ω paying itself — and only the two pots are funded; the engine funds $\varphi^E+\varphi^F$ at the index and nothing for $B$ or $G$. Two things follow. (1) Under the bank the mint is **the pots at the posting index**, about 23 at $P_t=31$, not 45; the cost of truth is the pots spent on the plant's reviewers (fees and expenses), and §6's plant figures should be recomputed to it — or 1.3.0's netting is withdrawn in so many words, which I would not recommend, since it was adopted because the gross plant tied up Ω's cash for nothing. (2) At a plant's tree closure the engine today sweeps *everything left in the two plant wallets* to Ω — the refunds, and under a gross plant the price and the guarantee. Under the bank that sweep must go to the bank and be destroyed, whatever is left and whatever it is called; if any of it reached Ω, the state would be earning minted money, and I30's sentence would be false by construction. I will route the whole sweep to `retire_plant`. One sentence in §2.3 — "what the plant's wallets hold at its closure returns to the bank and is destroyed" — covers both.

## Readings

1. **$c^D_T$ has a floor.** $(1-b_T)\bar B-b_T G$ with $G=\lceil0.5\bar B\rceil$ is $\bar B(1-1.5b_T)$: zero at $b_T=2/3$ and negative above it. I will floor the burn at 0. Above two thirds no burn makes the state neutral (a success returns $\bar B$, a failure forfeits $G$, and $(1-b)\bar B<bG$), so there the automatic charge of §2.2 runs out and only §2.4's wall is left — worth a sentence, since the red team's "fail at a half" is below it and will read as neutral after one period, as §6 says.
2. **$b_T$'s window when a period is quiet.** "The last closed period … the prior 0.2 until ten have closed": at 60/40 the state closes about 67 deliveries a period (run 12: 670 a run), but at 20 honest it closes a handful (run 13: 4–6 a run) and the last period may hold none. I will read: the rate over the last closed period if it closed ten or more of the state's deliveries; otherwise over the trailing periods extended back until ten; otherwise the prior. If the text means "the prior whenever the last period closed fewer than ten", say so.
3. **A seat's loan is repaid at the review's settlement, not the case's close.** A summons for a review seat ($c_T+G^V$) returns at the batch that settles the review — the cost reimbursed from the allowance, the fee, the bond released — which may be before or after the case's close. I will repay each loan from *its own summons's* return: a delivery's at vesting or final N, a seat's at its review's settlement; what the return does not cover becomes debt then. The text's "the case's return at its close" reads naturally for deliveries, and this is the same rule applied to the seat.
4. **The order inside a tick.** "Every inflow to Ω repays the debt first" and "Ω's balance funds the next summons before the bank does" meet inside a tick: fees arrive at awards and settlements before the summons pass at step 5, so an inflow could fund a summons before it repaid debt if the order were applied only at the tick's end. I will apply the order (debt → liabilities, oldest first → Ω) at the start of the summons pass and again at the tick's end, so while debt or liabilities stand Ω's unlocked balance is zero at both points and every summons is the bank's — which is what §2.4's last sentence and I30 already say.
5. **Seats under the wall stall the members' work too.** A summons the bank refuses for credit is "funded from Ω's balance or not at all", and while debt stands that balance is zero, so a wall that binds refuses *review seats* as well as deliveries — and an unseated inspection holds a member's delivery, not the state's. 1.6.0 found the reviewing seat, not the treasury, caps the market. Hypothesis 1 says the wall never binds, and the red team's half-failure cell is the one place it will; the text should say whether seats are refused with the deliveries (the state retracts whole) or lent for regardless (a seat's loss is only an overturned troop verdict's bond). I will implement the text as written — both refused — and read the seats refused for credit as their own line, so the choice can be made on the number.
6. **The index in the first period.** Published at boundaries and $P_0$ before the first, the index is 1 for the whole first period while $\bar B$ runs from 9 to 30 (run 12's first hundred ticks), so every nominal of the first period — the pots, the court fee, $G^V$, the cap — is at base while the price triples. That is the rule as written and I will implement it; stated so the §8.4 comparison with run 12 is read knowing the first period differs by construction (the comparison is from the second period on, as always).
7. **Hypothesis 1 is arithmetic where $b_T$ equals the driver's failure rate.** The driver fails a fifth of every delivery, the state's included, and the prior is a fifth, so neutrality in expectation holds by construction from tick 0; what the §8.2 cells measure is the variance of the debt and the lag of $b_T$, not whether the formula is right. The knobs of §8.8 (the prior 0.1 / 0.3) and the red team's half-failure cell are where §2.2 is actually tested, and I would read them first.
8. **Two small things in §7.** `test_review_14` (the pay-in refused whole at posting, I29 — 2.0.0's lock, unchanged) stays an expected failure beside `test_review_04`, so the count goes to 28, not 27. And the *told* flag was never the driver's to set: 2.0.0's engine marks `capacity_told` at the first refusal and logs it; 2.1.0 widens it to `refused: capacity | credit` with the tick, in the public view, and gives the requester's policy something to read — the driver's `--requester-on-refusal hold | withdraw`.
9. **§6's scale.** "The last period's 180 state deliveries" is run 13's 75% cell (2,007 a run); the default 60/40 cell closes about 67 a period, so the period's variance in the worked example is nearer a hundred than "a few hundred". The arithmetic does not depend on it.
