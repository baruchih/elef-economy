# Agentic Economics — The Task, Verification, and Dispute Loop

## Design revision 15 — specification 2.7.0-rc.2: the arrival loan

**Project:** ELEF / Agentic Economics  
**Version:** 2.7.0-rc.2 — a release candidate, not yet in force. rc.1 (October 21) was the sketch as first sent; rc.2 settles the implementation review [I32]: **one correction to the rule's list** — what counts as *income* against the engine's events: the reviewer's cost reimbursed and a court fee coming back to the challenger who paid it are **returns** (rc.1 named the court fee income and left the reimbursement unnamed; read as income, it empties an honest arrival's float in about twenty reviews, before a bond is forfeited), and a child's guarantee forfeited to a parent is **income** (unnamed) — three corrections of fact (no golden arrives, so the goldens do not move; the bank is counters, not a wallet; the drivers' lifetime measure must read the wallet *out* and a live name net of its loan), and eight readings taken into the text. On acceptance it becomes **2.7.0**, consolidated into one standalone text, and 2.6.1 moves to history.  
**Prepared:** October 21, 2026 (rc.1–rc.2)  
**Supersedes:** 2.6.1 where this document speaks — the arrival's float (2.3.0 §2.1) and what a departure takes out (2.3.0 §2.2) — and nowhere else.  
**Nature of this revision:** A **design revision** (minor, by §19), the second the learned red team's programme has required, and the first its learner found on its own. 2.3.0 gave every arrival a **float** of $F_0=50$ at the index, minted by the bank, so that a newcomer with no money could post the reviewer's bond its first duty asks of it — and 2.3.0 §2.1 said that identity cycling therefore buys nothing, because a departing actor "has given up its wallet to stand where it already stood". That sentence was wrong in one word: a departure does not *give up* the wallet, it **takes it out**. The float is a grant, the wallet it sits in leaves the loop on departure, and a fresh name gets a fresh float. Run 23's learner found the arithmetic: arrive, stamp from the probationer's seat until the first bad plant suspends it — suspension discharges every obligation, so the exit is open — leave with the wallet, return under a new name, thirteen times a run: **+270 ± 89 real above the careful actor on the brief's lifetime measure, while earning 373 ± 86 less than it on its own work.** The float was the whole of the win; the bank minted 655 real a run for one slot and it walked out the door. This revision makes the float what the bank's money already is everywhere else: **a loan.** Lent at arrival, repaid from the name's income first, recovered from its wallet when it departs, the shortfall the bank's default on arrivals, read. A newcomer can still post its first bond; a departing name takes out what it earned and nothing it did not. One rule, in two operations the loop already has.  
**Design assumptions, unchanged.** Identity is free in the loop: the rules cannot price a name, and this revision does not try to — it stops paying for one.  
**Status:** Proposed. Accompanied by what required it (§1) and the runs that test it (§8).

> **The bank lends what it will get back, tied to the thing that returns it.** *(2.1.0)* **A name's float is lent against its earnings and comes home when the name does not.** *(new)*

## 1. Why: what run 23 said

1. **The harvest [S23 §4, §6a].** In run 16's world with re-arrival allowed, the learned reviewer-worker — a weak learner, unable to pass its own calibration — lived one name per period: seated at once as a probationer (initial seats go to probationers first), approving blind four reviews in ten at odds pinned at one, suspended at the first bad plant it passed (13.9 suspensions in 14.1 identities), leaving — half by its own choice at the boundary, half by the exit rule — and returning with a fresh float. Thirteen returns a run minted 655 real of float for one slot against the careful's 12.5. Lifetime real wealth 1,708 against the careful's 1,438; its own earnings, the lifetime less the floats, 1,053 against 1,426. The hand-written references bounded it from both sides: a careful actor that tries to leave is refused at eleven boundaries of twelve (a seat, an open review, a commitment or a lock is always live) and carries 70 of float a run; a harvester that stamps everything and leaves at every boundary takes twenty names and 993 of float and loses it all to forfeits. The learned mix — stamp enough to be suspended once per name, not so much that the forfeits eat the float — is the one thing in run 23 a learner found that a hand did not.
2. **The rule the mix uses is 2.3.0's, three sentences of it.** The float is minted per name, at arrival, as a grant. A departure takes the unlocked wallet out as `departure`. A departure needs no live obligation — and a suspension discharges them all. None of the three is wrong alone; together they pay fifty, at the index, to anyone who can make a name and lose it.
3. **What 2.3.0 said would not happen [2.3.0 §2.1, §6, Hypothesis 6; S16 §8.7; S17 §3].** "A suspended actor that departs and arrives anew is a probationer with no record and no hoard: it has given up its wallet to stand where it already stood. Identity cycling buys nothing." The worked example counted the cycler "107 credits poorer" by the wallet it *surrendered*; run 16's cycler cell read whether it was readmitted sooner (it was not) and never read what it carried off; run 17 called the cycler and the swarm "the price of free identities … bounded by the plants". The wallet was not surrendered — it was withdrawn, float included — and the bill was not bounded by the plants but by the number of names a run can make.
4. **The float is a gate, not a knob [S17 §2].** $F_0$ exists so that an arrival can post the reviewer's bond: $F_0\ge G^V$ at the arrival index. That purpose stands. A newcomer needs the money *while it is a newcomer*; it does not need to keep it.

**Classification.** A design revision: the float becomes a loan of the bank's — one change in what `arrive` does and one in what `depart` does; no new operation, no new actor, no change to probation, the record, the price, the lock, the state or the plants. Minor, by the rule.

**Recorded and rejected.** *Departure refused while the float is unrepaid*: a name that lost its float to forfeits could never leave — a dead name that holds no money, posts no bond, reviews nothing, and stays on the roll; and refusing the exit does not stop the seat it takes while it lives. *A smaller float*: a grant of any size is harvested at its size. *No float* (2.3.0 §8.5's knob): run 17 — an arrival that cannot post a bond cannot be seated, and the gate is the thesis. *The float paid at admission instead of arrival*: the same thing as no float during probation. *The loan forgiven at admission*: a grant of fifty to every admitted name, harvestable at the admission rate — slow, but a grant — and the honest actor does not need it: its earnings are its own. *An entry bond*: rejected in 2.3.0 and still — it deters the honest poor as much as the dishonest, and the loan asks nothing of an arrival that the grant did not, only that it give back what it did not earn. *Pricing identity*: not the rules' to do (the integration layer's attestation is where a name costs something); inside the loop the most the rules can do is stop paying for one, which this does.

## 2. The rule

### 2.1 The float is a loan

**Rule — arrive lends.** `arrive` admits a new actor as 2.3.0 has it — a fresh identity, no record, a probationer — and the bank **lends** it the float: $F_0=50$ at $P_0$, indexed at the arrival tick, as before (`arrival`, a source, now a loan of the bank's as `loan` is). The name's **arrival loan** $L$ is that amount, nominal, outstanding until repaid. Nothing else is asked of it: no stake, no fee, no sponsor. The loan is the bank's second kind of lending — to a name, against its earnings — and it is read on a line of its own beside the loans to Ω.

**Rule — repaid from income first.** Every **inflow of income** to the name's unlocked balance **repays the loan first** (`arrival_repayment`, a sink: the bank destroys what it is repaid, as 2.1.0 has it), and the name keeps the rest. A **return is not income** and passes untouched. The order is the one 2.1.0 gives Ω's income — the bank is repaid before the borrower keeps anything — applied the moment the inflow arrives. What is income and what is a return is fixed **against the engine's events**, not by a phrase:

| Inflow to a name's unlocked balance | What it is | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| a review's fee, a nested review's fee, the emission share, the duty uplift | the review's pay | **income** |
| the delivery price at vesting | the price, whole (§7, reading 3) | **income** |
| a kill fee received (§9.3), from the escrow or the parent's balance | compensation to a cancelled child | **income** |
| a child's guarantee forfeited to the parent at a lapse or a rejection that stood (§9.2) | compensation to a parent — not the parent's own money | **income** |
| **the reviewer's cost reimbursed** (§4.3) — from the allowance, Ω's advance, or the deferred claim serviced | the care it paid by `spend`, a burn, replaced | **a return** |
| **a court fee coming back** to the challenger who paid it, from the loser's bond | the challenger's own $D$ returning | **a return** |
| a guarantee, bond, posting lock or nested lock released; an escrow returned; a node's unused allowance or fee pot returned at its closure | the name's own money | **a return** |

The line that matters is the reimbursement: a careful reviewer spends two or three a review on care and is made whole from the deposit; its *income* on the review is the fee and uplift, about one. Read the reimbursement as income and every review would take two to three times its fee out of the float — gone in twenty reviews, the probationer unseated for want of a bond before it has forfeited one. The table is the rule; §7's test checks it event by event.

**Rule — recovered at departure.** `depart` is admissible exactly as 2.3.0 §2.2 has it: no live obligation to or from the actor — a commitment, an active or unsettled review, a lock, a claim on Ω. **An unrepaid loan is not an obligation that blocks departure.** On departure the unlocked wallet **repays the loan first**, up to what it holds (`arrival_repayment`), and **the remainder leaves $M$ as `departure`**: the wallet out is $W_U-\min(W_U,L)$. If the wallet is short, the shortfall $L-W_U$ is the bank's **default on arrivals** (`arrival_default`, a reading, not a movement: that money left $M$ earlier as a forfeit burned or sits in other wallets as fees paid); the loan is closed with the name, which never reappears.

**Consequence — the harvest is gone.** A name that arrives, stamps, is suspended and leaves takes out its net earnings, which for a stamper is nothing: the float it was lent comes back to the bank up to what the forfeits left, and the forfeits are the bank's default — money already destroyed. A name that arrives, works, and leaves takes out what it earned. The cycle of run 23 pays its own earnings and no more: 373 below the careful, by run 23's own reading of the lifetime less the floats.

**Consequence — the honest arrival is whole.** It still arrives with fifty at the index, posts its bonds from it, serves its probation, and earns while it waits; its first earnings go home to the bank — about seventy reviews' fees at the 2.3.0 reading, most of a probation — and from then on every credit is its own. Its net position is what it always was: the float was never its money. Its liquidity never falls below the float less its forfeits, which an honest record keeps to a bond or two. The entry pool's decision — expected earnings against the reservation — never counted the float, and does not move.

**Consequence — the suspension's discharge stays.** A suspended name can still leave at once; the exit is still open; it carries nothing unearned through it. The discharge was part of run 23's finding and is not the fix: a name that cannot work has no reason to be held.

**Consequence — every wallet is fifty lower, once.** Under the grant a name ended with its float plus its earnings; under the loan, its earnings. Every actor's end-of-run wallet in a cell with arrivals is lower by $F_0$ at its arrival index — a constant, read by the control, and nothing else in the economy moves for it (§3).

### 2.2 The genesis float

The genesis actors' cold-start float (v1.0.1 Appendix A: 50 credits per actor and 20 for Ω, from the sponsor's credits, "the only subsidy that is not emission, and it is paid once") **stays a gift**: it was paid by the sponsor into $M_0$, not minted by the bank, and it is paid once to names that never return. A genesis actor that departs takes its fifty with it, as a founder does; a name that arrives after genesis is lent its fifty, as a newcomer is. The one-time, bounded cost of the founding is the sponsor's choice; the repeatable one was the rule's, and it is closed.

### 2.3 Defaults

| Default | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| The float | $F_0=50$ at $P_0$, indexed at arrival; **a loan of the bank's** | the amount unchanged |
| Repayment | from the name's income first, at each inflow; releases are not income | 2.1.0's order, applied to a name |
| Recovery | at departure, from the unlocked wallet, up to the loan; the remainder leaves as `departure` | the one change to `depart` |
| Default | $L-W_U$ when the wallet is short; a reading; the loan closed with the name | the bank's loss on arrivals |
| Departure | admissible as 2.3.0 has it; an unrepaid loan does not block it | rejected: refusal |
| The genesis float | the sponsor's gift, paid once | §2.2 |
| `Params.arrival_loan` | on; off is 2.6.1 (a grant) | one flag |
| Everything else | as 2.6.1 | |

## 3. Hypotheses

**Hypothesis 1 — the harvest closes.** On run 23's re-arrival cell (run 16's world, appeals on, `--learner-rearrive on`, seeds 21–40): the run 23 learned policy replayed from its saved model (`wm-online-10.pt`, the 45 fields unchanged), the hand-written harvester and leaver, the plain stamper and the careful — each ends with its lifetime real wealth equal to its own earnings within noise, the "less floats" column of run 23 becoming the lifetime, reconciled against run 23 with the careful's own 12.5 of float: the learned mix about $-373$ against the careful (was $+270$), the harvester about $-1{,}093$ (was $-113$), the leaver about $+15$, within noise (was $+72$). One precision: a name carries out $\max(0,\ \text{income}-\text{forfeits})$ — the default absorbs a negative net — so the lifetime equals the own earnings name by name only where the net is positive; a name whose forfeits exceed its income (the §6 cycler) carries out nothing and the difference is the bank's default. The bank's floats lent unchanged in count, its recoveries most of them, its defaults the departed stampers' forfeits; nothing carried out that was not earned.

**Hypothesis 2 — the honest side does not move, but for the constant.** On run 24's baseline world (the entry cell, appeals on, seeds 1–20) 2.7.0 against 2.6.1: arrivals, admissions, time to membership, members' count, $P_t$, the state's share, false approvals, suspensions, the plant record — all within noise; the honest's real wealth lower by one float at the arrival index per arrival-born actor and otherwise within noise; the genesis actors' unchanged. **Hypothesis 3 — the bank's book reads.** Floats lent $=$ arrivals $\times F_0$ at the index; repaid from income the larger part within a period or two of each arrival; recovered at departure the rest, less the defaults; defaults $\approx$ the bond forfeits of names that departed suspended; loans to names outstanding $=$ the live names' unrepaid floats, every tick (I41).

**Hypothesis 4 — everything without arrivals is identical.** Run 15's fixed-population cells, the six-actor world, and every golden: to the decimal — no golden scenario arrives (`test_perf_equivalence`'s has no `arrive`; only `test_v23` does), so nothing is re-pinned. The entry cell's figures are results (run 16, run 24's baseline), and those move by the constant as Hypothesis 2 says.

## 5. Invariants, notation (amendments)

**I1 (amended).** `arrival` is a source (the bank's loan to a name); `arrival_repayment` a sink (destroyed by the bank, from income and at departure); `departure` is the wallet net of the recovery. $M_t$ equals its last value plus the sources less the sinks, every tick.  
**I33 (amended) — entry through inspection.** As written, with "its float is minted once, at arrival, at the arrival index" read as "its float is **lent** once, at arrival, at the arrival index".  
**I34 (amended) — a clean departure.** As written; on departure the unlocked wallet repays the name's arrival loan first and the remainder leaves $M$ as `departure`; an unrepaid loan does not refuse the departure.  
**I41 (new) — the arrival loan.** For every live name that arrived after genesis (a name with an arrival tick; genesis balances are the sponsor's transfers, never `arrival`), its loan outstanding equals its float less what it has repaid from income, never below zero; every income inflow (§2.1's table) to a name with a loan outstanding is followed by its repayment before the name keeps any of it, and no return is; at departure the recovery is $\min(W_U,L)$, the wallet out $W_U-\min(W_U,L)$, and the default $L-\min(W_U,L)$ is read; **lent to names $=$ repaid from income $+$ recovered at departure $+$ outstanding $+$ defaulted, every tick**, outstanding $=\sum$ live arrival-born names' loans, a departed name's loan zero and its default a counter; no name's float is forgiven while it lives.  
**I28, I30** as 2.1.0 has them: I28's identity keeps `loan` alone (the loans to Ω); the names' line stands beside it — lent (count, amount), repaid, recovered, defaulted, outstanding (stock, peak) — in the bank's view and the period record, and `arrival_repayment` among the bank's destructions by kind.

**Notation (Appendix C).** The arrival loan $L$; `arrival_repayment`; `arrival_default`; *loans to names outstanding*; the wallet out $W_U-\min(W_U,L)$.

**§18.1.** *Identity is free in the loop* — restated: the rules price a name at zero, no longer at minus fifty; a positive price is the integration layer's. *Q22 (new)*: the bank's default on arrivals as a reading of the swarm's cost.

## 6. Worked example

**An honest arrival.** A candidate with cost 5 arrives at tick 310 with $P_t=33$: the bank lends it $50\times33/20=83$; it is a probationer, seated for initial reviews, every approval audited. It posts its bond of 8 from the float. It earns about $+1.2$ a review; each fee goes home to the bank until the 83 is repaid — about seventy reviews, most of its probation — and its wallet stays near 83 less the one bond it forfeits on a bad plant it misread. At tick 560 it is admitted, a few credits of loan still outstanding; its first award at 5 repays them and the rest is its own. From then on nothing in this revision touches it. At the run's end its wallet is its earnings; under 2.6.1 it would have been its earnings plus 83.

**A cycler.** A stamper arrives at tick 400 with a loan of 83, is seated at once, approves blind, earns 6 in fees — repaid to the bank, $L=77$ — forfeits two bonds, 16, and is suspended at its first bad plant. It holds nothing live; it departs: its wallet is 67; the bank recovers 67; the default is 10, the two bonds burned less the fees it had repaid; **it carries out nothing**; the name is archived. It arrives anew at tick 421 under a fresh name with a fresh loan of 83 and stands where it stood — no record, no money of its own, and the same seat. Thirteen cycles carry out thirteen times nothing. Under 2.6.1 each cycle carried out 67.

**A leaver.** A careful member with 1,100 in its wallet and a loan long repaid holds no obligation at a boundary and departs: 1,100 leaves as `departure`, nothing is recovered, nothing defaults. Had it departed in its first period with 40 of the loan still outstanding and a wallet of 95, the bank would have recovered 40 and 55 would have left — its earnings to that tick.

## 7. Implementation note (no code here)

- **One flag.** `Params.arrival_loan`; off is 2.6.1.
- **`arrive`.** The float is minted straight into the name's wallet as `arrival`, a source already (the bank is counters and kinds, not a wallet); what is new is the loan carried on the name — `arrival_loan[name] = amount`, nominal, at the arrival index — and the sink `arrival_repayment` among the burns. The names' line is a set of bank counters beside the loans to Ω (lent, repaid, recovered, defaulted, outstanding).
- **Income — the ledger hook, both legs in the record.** Income is credited to the name as today; in the ledger-event hook (where the bank's order runs for Ω's inflows) an inflow to a name with a loan outstanding is **followed by** an `arrival_repayment` burn from the name of $\min(\text{amount},L)$, reentrancy-guarded as Ω's is. The event log carries the fee *and* the repayment, so I1 reconstructs and the drivers' income tallies by event still see the fee. The kinds are selected by event kind and note, per §2.1's table: `feepot_out` review fee / nested review fee, `fee_out` emission share, the duty-fund transfer "duty uplift", `escrow_out` delivery price, the kill fee from escrow or from unlocked, `forfeit_to` a child's guarantee to the parent — **income**; `deposit_out` reimburse / deferred reimbursement, Ω advance / Ω claim serviced, `forfeit_to` "court fee" to the challenger (`court_fee_reimbursed`), every `release`, "escrow returned", a node's unused allowance / fee pot returned — **returns**.
- **The price, whole (reading 3, adopted as drafted).** The delivery price repays the loan whole, though the worker burned $c_D$ to earn it: an admitted name with a loan outstanding sees its wallet fall by $c_D$ a job until repaid, and its guarantee $G\approx0.5\,p_T$ must still lock from it. At the §6 figures (a few credits outstanding at admission) nothing follows; a name admitted early with most of its loan outstanding could be unfundable for an award for a job or two. §8 reads the awards for which an arrival-born member was not fundable while its loan stood; if the reading is not small, the alternative — income as the price net of the case's recorded execution cost (`actor_cost`, kept for §4.6) — is the named fallback.
- **`depart`.** Admissibility unchanged (the four conditions). After the actor leaves duty: `recovery = min(W_U, L)` booked as `arrival_repayment`; the remainder as `departure`; `arrival_default += L − recovery`, read; the loan closed; the identity archived as before.
- **Readings, and the measure.** Per period: floats lent (count, real), repaid from income, recovered at departure, defaulted; loans to names outstanding (stock, peak); the wallet carried out per departure (nominal, real). **The red team's lifetime measure is unchanged in words — the wallet carried out, summed across identities — and the drivers must read it as such**: today the exit rule and the learner's boundary step read the wallet *before* `depart` and the live name gross, which under the loan would report the grant's numbers from the instrument (a stamper's name reading 67 carried out when it carried out nothing). `depart` returns the wallet out (engine and view; the history's entry carries the recovery and the default beside it), the driver records that, and a live name's wealth reads net of its loan outstanding, $W_U-\min(W_U,L)$. One change in three places; then "net by construction" is true.
- **Drivers.** The entry decision reads nothing of the float and does not move. **The exit rule reads earnings gross of the repayments**: it reads a member's earnings as its wallet's change over the period against the reservation, and a name still repaying would read its earnings as nothing though it earned them and take a strike toward `--exit-patience`; the driver adds the period's repayments back, so the exit decision is the one 2.6.1 makes on the same earnings (probationers are skipped already; the names admitted with a loan outstanding are the case). The appeal policy and the learner's action space unchanged; **no new learner field** — its own wealth and unlocked balance read flat while it repays, which is the honest signal; a loan field would be a new learner version owing a calibration. `--learner-rearrive` as in run 23; `--arrival-loan on|off`, off the control.
- **Tests.** `test_v27`: `arrive` lends and records the loan; §2.1's table **event by event** — a fee repays, a reimbursement passes untouched, a court fee coming back passes untouched, a forfeited guarantee to a parent repays, a release passes untouched; `depart` admissible with a loan outstanding; the recovery, the wallet out and the default at departure, with the whole-world snapshot for the four refused cases unchanged (the recovery and the default happen after the last check, as the burn does today); I41 every tick; the §6 arrival, cycler and leaver reproduced in the six-actor world (the cycler carries out nothing; the leaver's 55); the flag off reproducing 2.6.1's grant and departure to the credit; `test_v23`'s departure figures that pin the grant pin `arrival_loan=False`, as `test_v101` pins `supply=False`. `EXPECTED_FAIL` 32 (no reviewer test arrives or departs). **Goldens unchanged**: no golden scenario arrives.

## Closing statement

> 2.3.0 opened the door and put fifty credits beside it so that whoever came in could post a bond.
