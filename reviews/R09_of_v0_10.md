# Agentic Economics v0.10 — Rulebook stabilization review

**Review date:** October 15, 2026  
**Inputs:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_v0_10.md`, its applicable inherited rules, and the newly uploaded `agentic_sim_v0_10.zip`.  
**Disposition:** Stop open-ended rulebook redesign. Freeze a versioned research baseline after accurately describing the reviewer information boundary. Continue implementation conformance maintenance separately from rule design and from strategy research.

## 1. The decision this review addresses

The user explicitly separates the rulebook from choosing strategies within the economy. This review preserves that distinction. Its question is whether v0.10 needs another round of rule creation, not which actor or orchestrator strategy will be successful.

The basic task/payment structure and its worked examples were already present in v0.4. That does not mean all regular paths, accounting boundaries, or admitted operation sequences were correct in that implementation. Earlier repairs to atomicity, reimbursement, occupancy and liabilities were substantive. However, discovering an implementation defect need not reopen the economic design.

Three activities should now have different change criteria:

| Activity | Question | Appropriate action |
|---|---|---|
| Rulebook design | What is allowed, owed, observed, and final? | Change only for an explicit design decision or unresolved rule contradiction |
| Implementation conformance | Does the engine perform those rules, including refusals and timing? | Patch the engine and add a regression without introducing a different permitted behavior |
| Strategy research | Which allowed choices work well for participants or the system? | Separate investigation against a fixed rulebook and specified settings |

A freeze is not a claim of optimal incentives, complete production assurance, or the absence of undiscovered bugs. It means the selected rules are a stable object to reason about and implement.

## 2. Scope and method

I extracted the uploaded v0.10 ZIP into a clean review directory, inspected the v0.9-to-v0.10 engine changes and the relevant inherited rules, ran all supplied test entry points, independently inspected the actual exceptions behind the historical expected-failure markers, and added 24 targeted checks of the selected boundary.

The additional checks were deliberately bounded to withdrawal, response admissibility, adjacent continuing workflows, and the public reviewer information contract. This was not an unrestricted new search for every possible unusual sequence. No bidding policy, evaluator, learning model or economic strategy was added. All protocol mutations in the additional tests use public operations; read-only snapshots are used to check atomic refusal.

All 23 supplied files are unchanged, verified against SHA-256 hashes taken immediately after extraction. Generated caches are not part of the source manifest or review bundle. The previous review's independent audit-reopening file is copied into the supplied suite byte-for-byte unchanged; its expectations are now classified by external test configuration.

## 4. Assessment of the reversal

The revision explicitly withdraws v0.9 section 8.3 rather than claiming its reopening paths were repaired. The code removes `suspended_verdict`, `_audit_reopens`, audit-aware lookup of the current initial assessor, and the associated position-count exceptions.

The selected boundary is now:

- An unrevised initial review may request revision or missing evidence while the actor still holds the task commitment.
- An audit and a challenge review return fulfilled or not fulfilled. An audit does not send the actor back into execution.
- Resubmission remains F/N-only, including when a different reviewer replaces the original one.
- The reviewer is told its allowed response contract.

This is a reasonable simplification for the current baseline. A question about a finished submission can still change its settlement through the existing challenge or ruling procedure. What it no longer does is reclaim an actor's released execution slot, invalidate a parent's earlier milestone, or create a second response cycle through the audit channel.

It would be too strong to say that reopening is impossible in principle. It is a different contract with additional capacity, timing and adjudication decisions. It was not required merely to fix a mismatch between the evaluator interface and the allowed answers. Withdrawing it is a defensible scope decision.

### Additional conformance checks

The new file covers:

- P and unknown audit answers refused with a nonzero proposed review cost, using a whole-world snapshot, across ordinary, nested, merged-dispute and already-busy actor contexts;
- initial F/N/P/unknown remaining permitted;
- resubmission across elapsed time and replacement retaining the F/N-only stage;
- definitive audits across elapsed time, with agreement or disagreement followed by a split ruling;
- audit timeout after the original window, followed by replacement and settlement;
- a human ruling affecting the old task without reclaiming the actor from its new task;
- the existing two-question appeal of an initial missing-evidence claim.

These checks all pass. They found no additional execution failure within this bounded review. They do not certify every admitted sequence or production condition.

## 5. One material clarification: the interface is honest, but not role-indistinguishable

The v0.10 specification says that the role is not supplied and that “blindness stands.” The first part is true in the narrow field-level sense: there is no explicit `role` attribute in `ReviewView`. The stronger indistinguishability interpretation is not true.

For a currently assigned, open, unrevised fulfillment review, the public view has:

| Assignment | `question` | `resumed` | `responses_available` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial review | `fulfillment` | `False` | `True` |
| Audit of an unrevised submission | `fulfillment` | `False` | `False` |

No hidden-world access is needed to distinguish these cases. The code defines `responses_available` to be true only for an open initial review before resubmission. For these inputs, false identifies an audit. Since audits are assigned only to pending fulfilled outcomes, recognizing the audit also reveals that a prior approval exists. This does not reveal every original assessment detail or the original reviewer's identity.

Audits of resubmitted work are not covered by the simple `resumed=False` inference. This observation does not claim all assignments are distinguishable in every state. The exhibited common pair is sufficient to reject a universal role-indistinguishability guarantee.

Relevant source locations in the supplied package:

- `sim/engine.py:100–121`: reviewer view fields;
- `sim/engine.py:486–508`: permitted-response predicate and view construction;
- `sim/engine.py:590–611`: answer validation;
- `sim/strategies.py`: the documented response contract.

### Recommended treatment

Keep the interface and the non-reopening rule. Describe the information boundary accurately rather than expanding the state machine again to preserve a stronger secrecy claim.

Suggested wording, not an edit made by this review:

> Reviewers receive the information required for their question and the permitted response set. Other reviewers' identities and unrelated judgments are not directly supplied. The question and permitted responses may reveal the stage or kind of assignment; audit-role indistinguishability is not guaranteed.

For appeals and disputes, the explicitly supplied challenged assessment and objection remain part of the required input, as already selected.

Whether perfect audit-role secrecy is actually required is a design question. My recommendation for this baseline is to accept the weaker, explicit information contract. The tests establish the leak; they do not demonstrate any particular incentive effect or exploitation strategy.

A smaller wording point is similar: an audit can be assigned immediately after F. The actor's capacity has been released, but it is not necessary that a separate response window has elapsed. The stable reason for not reopening is the released commitment, not a claim that every audit starts after such a window closes.

## 6. Should refinement continue?

### Recommendation: stop open-ended rule design

The reviewed change is a good place to draw the line. State the actual information boundary and freeze the rulebook as a versioned research baseline. Nothing in this review requires a new actor role, another appeal level, a scoring formula, a rework mechanism, or a choice of learning strategy.

Future changes should be classified before implementation:

1. **Implementation defect:** an admitted operation applies the existing rule incorrectly, money is attributed to the wrong obligation, or a refused operation has effects. Correct code, preserve rules, add a regression.
2. **Rule contradiction or undefined required outcome:** two active rules demand incompatible outcomes, or an in-scope case has no selected disposition. Make the smallest explicit design decision needed, version it and review it as a rule change.
3. **Alternative policy or additional capability:** potentially interesting, but not a reason to keep the present rulebook indefinitely open. Defer to a separate design revision.
4. **Strategy or incentive question:** separate from conformance. A current rule can be well-defined without being optimal or immune to gaming.

The rulebook's permitted boundary is allowed to exclude a workflow. A test that deliberately attempts excluded behavior should test the refusal; it is not an obligation to add support for that workflow.

This is not “stop fixing bugs.” It is “stop equating every bug or imaginable alternative with a reason to redesign the economy.” No particular strategy must be chosen to freeze that boundary.

## 7. Consolidation instead of another refinement cycle

The current specification is a short amendment that inherits v0.9 and earlier versions while withdrawing a particular subsection. The sequence also contains older overridden formulas and stronger statements that later revisions corrected.

A useful rulebook-only next artifact would be a consolidated baseline containing the effective current rules, with superseded alternatives in a separate historical appendix and implementation limitations listed once. This is consolidation, not an invitation to add rules or tune parameters. A reader should not need to resolve precedence across ten files to determine what F/N, finality, or reimbursement currently mean.

I have not produced that consolidated specification here, nor changed any source or map. This review only recommends that direction.

## Conclusion

The original examples remain executable; the richer fixes were not all cosmetic; v0.10 deliberately stops adding audit-reopening obligations. The supplied suite and the additional focused checks support that narrower boundary.

**Freeze the rulebook after acknowledging the visibility trade-off. Maintain conformance separately. Strategy choice remains a different question, and this review does not require choosing one before the rulebook can stabilize.**
