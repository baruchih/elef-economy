# Review of 2.6.0-rc.1 — design revision 14, "the plant's disguise"

**Date:** October 20, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_2.6.0-rc.1.md` · **Against:** the 2.5.0 text in force, the engine at `44ef6e47`, drivers `f55a234d`, run 20 (`results/studies/rt-Q2/`, the hand-written tell-reader), the red team's brief · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict. Implementable with one redraft, three corrections of fact and five readings.** The content — a plant lives the life of a real request, posted, available, awarded and submitted at the real population's pace under names that recur nowhere — is the right repair for the leak run 20 found, and it is a design revision by §19 (the construction of a plant, 1.2.0 §2.1, changes what a member observes). The redraft is on the scope of the widened test (§2.2): as written it names "the public record's fields", and a fresh identity in a public record is itself a complete tell; the test must be scoped to what a *member* observes, or the identities must not be fresh in the record. Everything else is implementation readings, taken as written unless ruled otherwise.

## The redraft — the fresh name is a tell in the record

§2.1 makes the plant actor and the plant requester fresh identities per plant "in the public record alike", and §2.2 widens the test to "the public record's fields". The record's money events carry wallet names (`Event.src`, `Event.dst`; public through `Public.events_since`, which is how the hand-written driver tallies every actor's income). A real root is awarded to a member whose name recurs, or — after $w_A$, at the state's price — to a troop's single-use name. A plant under this rule is awarded, before $w_A$, at a market price, to a name that appears once. A `freshname` reader — *a plant when the request was awarded before $w_A$ to a name never seen before* — passes every plant by construction, as `idage` did, and the consequence "a member that reads the record sees a request posted, awarded and submitted at ordinary intervals by names it has never seen" is exactly its rule. Two ways to close it, for the reviewer's choice:

1. **Scope the test to a member's observations** — the view, the roots it saw available and when, its own bids and their outcomes, its own assignment ticks, and the record's fields *about its own cases and its own wallet*. The record's other names are the orchestrator's instrument (the drivers' harvest), not a member's reading; the brief's learner is bound to the view already. §2.2's list loses "the public record's fields" and gains "its own"; the design assumption the rc states ("the drivers' convention — the view only — is what keeps the public record's actor names from leaking plants") then stands as the rule's boundary rather than as something the rule claims to have replaced. **Recommended**: it is the smaller change and the honest one — a record that names awardees cannot hide a one-time awardee.
2. **Make the plant's identities recur** — a standing pool of plant actors and plant requesters that look like members and customers over a run. A plant actor that never bids on real work, or a plant requester that never posts real work, is told apart by its history within a period or two; the pool would have to bid and lose on real roots to disguise itself, which puts the orchestrator in the market. Not recommended.

Either way, the plant's identities should be **aliases** of the two plant wallets, as a troop's `T<n>` aliases the state's wallet (`Ledger.aliases`): fresh names in the view and the case log, two wallets in the ledger, I15/I21/I33 and the plant sweep unchanged.

## Corrections of fact

1. **"Excluded from $P_t$ — new."** A plant is excluded today: `create_plant` never passes through `award`, and `_record_price` runs only at a member's award and a troop's delivery, so no plant's price has ever entered $P_t$ or $\bar B$. Under the new life the plant *will* pass through the award step, so the exclusion becomes an explicit skip there — "as before", not new.
2. **The identifier's order in the engine.** The record layer pages settled cases by the counter (`record.py`, `int(key[1:])`), the History chains the ids, and the drivers sort their working set by it in twelve places. The rc's own words — "the ledger keeps the creation order privately for the History's chain" — are right and should be read as: **opaque identifiers are the public surface's**, a translation both ways in `drivers.view.Public` (and in the case log the view returns), with the engine, the record and the goldens on `q<n>` as today. Then the goldens move only for the plant's life, not for a renaming of every case.
3. **"I17 (1.2.0, amended)."** I17 is *debts and claims reconcile* (§17's table). 1.2.0's indistinguishability rule is a **test** (`test_v12`: identical views for a plant and a case) with I20 carrying the sealed marker; the amendment belongs to §7's test and to I20's text, and the new invariants are I39 and I40 as the rc numbers them.

## Readings

1. **The award is the market's report, not a new operation.** The engine has no bids and no selection by design (§18.2); the driver's selection runs on the plant as on any root and calls `award(cid, member, B, τ)`. On a plant the engine takes the call as the selection's report: it awards the plant actor at $B$, commits no member, and logs it; the driver reads the awarded actor from the case afterwards, as it must anyway. **No refusal** — a request whose award is refused and then goes to someone else would be a tell at the orchestrator's level; a bidder in the market learns only that it was not awarded, as today. If no bid has come by $w_A$, see reading 2.
2. **Time-to-award should be the market's own, not a draw.** A drawn time-to-award means the engine must hold a priced, available request off the award until the drawn tick — refusing or ignoring bids a real root would have taken — and that waiting is a tell no real root has (a real root with bids under its ceiling is awarded the tick they come). A plant whose ceiling is drawn from the real ceilings gets bids as a real root does and waits as a real root waits; its time in the market is then the real distribution by construction, not by sampling it. Past $w_A$ a real root is the state's: the plant must then be awarded to its plant actor at $p_T$ at the summons pass's step, and counted apart from the state's deliveries (it is not one). The same reasoning keeps the **marks**: a plant whose drawn ceiling lands below the floor or below market is marked as any root is (the mark is the engine's reading of a ceiling, not of who posted); the rc's "never below market or below the reservation" would make the unmarked request the tell. What stays excluded is what has a customer in it: $K_{\text{open}}$, the clock and its rebate, the summons' funding and the state's counts.
3. **The view's counts must include the plant.** `market()` counts the available roots with `c.plant is None`; a member that counts the roots it sees and reads the view's count would find them differ by the plants. The dial's `plants_created` / `plants_due` are published live and move at the plant's posting — a member reading `dial()` the tick a root appears can match them; the plant counts should be **published at the period's close**, as $P_t$ is, with the live counters kept for the orchestrator's instrument only. Both are the view's, one line each.
4. **The time-to-submission and the deadline are the plant actor's.** A real delivery's `t_submit − t_award` is the member's work; the plant actor's is a draw from the trailing real deliveries', and its deadline a draw from the real roots' $τ$ — as the rc has it. Before the first closed period, the drivers' defaults: $β = P_t$, $τ = $ the work ticks, submission after $τ − 1$ at the earliest (a real honest child submits at its deadline, a root after its work ticks). The engine keeps the trailing records it already has (`t_post`, `t_award`, `t_submit`, `beta`, `tau` on every root).
5. **`create_plant` stays as the control; `post_plant` is the 2.6.0 entry.** Nine test files build plants in the submitted state through `create_plant`; the 1.2.0 construction remains behind `plant_market=False` and those tests stand. The equivalence scenario switches to `post_plant` under the flag, so the goldens re-pin once for the plant's life — the rc's expectation. `EXPECTED_FAIL` 32 on my reading: no reviewer test reads a case id's order (`test_v171` reads a troop's).
