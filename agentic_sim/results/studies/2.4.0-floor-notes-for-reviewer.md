## 7. For the decision

1. **The floor works as a backstop and also as a price.** In the patient cells the state delivers at 8 what the cheap members leave, and
   the cheap members — median cost 5.4 — bid up toward it (margin 0.36 against 0.07). The floor holds the price up for them. §2.1's
   "it does not let the state set the price" is true of $\bar B$ (7.1–7.7, under the floor) and not of $P_t$ (7.2–7.7, pulled up to the
   state's 8). A reading for §2.1's consequence paragraph, not a contradiction.
2. **The requesters still hoard** (7,600–9,100 a wallet). The patient policy's posting rate, not the floor, sets what they spend; a hoard is
   the demand side's choice under issuance 10 a tick. Nothing for the rules; Hypothesis 1's clause was the sketch's, not the rule's.
3. **The sweep's 12** admits more members (ten of twenty) and prices the state out; **4** is the control. The default 8 is the anchor the
   reviewer chose and the runs do not move it.
4. **The kill fee's test is the driver's, and this driver's rule is too late.** The parent cancels when its own deadline leaves room; the
   lowballer's deadline is earlier. A parent that cancels a child at the child's deadline minus one tick (before the lapse) would meet the
   case; the engine allows it (cancellation before submission). I will run that variant with the red team in the next batch unless the
   reviewer prefers the rule's answer first (a nested guarantee not released on a non-performance cancellation — named in rc.2, not in this
   revision).
5. **The award-pass correction is confirmed small outside the patient cells** (three posts in 2,554; the riots within a percent) and large
   inside them — it is what makes the floor work: the raised requests go to the members first, the state after.
