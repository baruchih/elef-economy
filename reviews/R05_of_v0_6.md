# Agentic Economics v0.6 — independent conformance review

**Review date:** October 15, 2026  
**Basis:** Uploaded `agentic_economics_loop_spec_v0_6.md`, its explicitly inherited v0.5/v0.4 rules, and the implementation actually contained in `agentic_sim_v0_6.zip`.  
**Scope:** Source inspection, executing the supplied suites, and targeted checks of adjacent protocol paths. This is not a strategy-driven economic experiment, a production security audit, or a proof of full conformance.

## Executive assessment

v0.6 demonstrably repairs the cases from the preceding review. Both custom-runner modes and raw pytest now agree: **81 passed and one expected failure**. The previous 14-run interaction suite is present unchanged and passes. The rejected historical test still uses the intentionally superseded, single-answer missing-evidence appeal API; it is not a new failure.

The new checks expose six further interaction/payload findings, plus a narrower working-copy completeness issue. The independent suite reports **4 passing controls and 10 failed checks**: eight checks across findings A–F, and two optional record-hardening checks in G. These are selected counterexamples, not an estimated failure rate. Findings B and E are caught by the implementation's invariants after the action; several others pass every existing invariant.

The most important remaining defects are:

- An unfunded dispute can clear a vacated audit and allow the case to vest without either replacement or waiver.
- A direct human acceptance of a submitted child, before the parent judges it, leaves the rejection bond locked and fails I17 after vesting.
- Audits admitted after an override use the old, overruled verdict: an opposing assessment can be rewarded, while an agreeing assessment can fail during settlement.

Other checks concern preserving the resubmission stage across reviewer replacement, reconciling parent-submission guards with terminal child timeouts, and committing the actual grounds of an appeal.

**The direction of the economic model is not being reopened.** The existing rules need consistent transitions and complete payload capture. The remaining brackets—strategies, learned selection, evaluator behavior, external head anchoring and real-world resource exchange—are not counted as defects here.

## 2. Findings

### A. A refused dispute clears the vacated-audit obligation

**Priority:** High — changes admission and financial-finality behavior.  
**Rule:** v0.6 §2.2 requires atomic admission; §7.5 requires a vacated audit to hold the case until replacement completion, explicit waiver, or an admitted requester dispute.  
**Code:** `sim/engine.py:680–703`, specifically clearing `audit_vacated` and decrementing `open_items` at 698–700 before `_open_challenge()` collects the fee at 667–671.  
**Test:** `test_r5_a_unfunded_dispute_keeps_vacated_audit_obligation`.

Reproduction under full monetary defaults:

1. H starts with 20.5 credits and awards a ten-credit task. Delivery escrow, the ten-credit deposit and the 0.5-credit service fee leave H with zero.
2. The initial reviewer approves. A timely audit is assigned and subsequently times out.
3. At tick 6, `audit_vacated=True` and `open_items=1`. The original window closes at tick 10.
4. H attempts to dispute but cannot pay the frozen two-credit court fee.
5. The call raises `LedgerError`, yet `audit_vacated=False` and `open_items=0`. No challenge exists.
6. The case vests at tick 10. All seventeen checks pass.

The same result occurs with the zero-service/zero-emission fixture. The refused filing has silently done what only an explicit waiver or valid replacement procedure should do.

**Correction direction:** Validate fee collection and all other admission requirements before replacing the existing obligation, or make the whole transition rollback-safe. On failure, the previous suspension must remain. The existing funded-dispute path is a passing control.

### B. Direct acceptance of a child by human override leaves the parent's rejection bond locked

**Priority:** High — causes an invariant failure after financial settlement.  
**Rule:** v0.6 §15.2 admits a human ruling on an existing submitted result; nested acceptance releases its rejection bond under inherited v0.4 §9.2 / v0.5 §9.2. I17 prohibits a terminal nested case retaining that bond.  
**Code:** `sim/engine.py:912–982`, especially the rejection-only reconciliation at 971–972 and positive branch at 977–982; normal acceptance releases the bond at 607–609.  
**Test:** `test_r5_b_direct_nested_acceptance_override_releases_rejection_bond`.

Sequence:

- A posts a child; B is awarded it and submits a result.
- Before A issues an initial judgment, H overrides the child to F.
- The child becomes PENDING but A's five-credit `rej:<child>` lock remains.
- Advancing time vests and pays the child, then raises `InvariantError: I17 ... VESTED but still carries a rejection bond`.

The newly added path for **contradicting an existing N judgment** is working. This is the neighboring path where **no initial judgment has yet been made**. There is no rejection to overturn, but there is collateral to release upon acceptance.

**Correction direction:** Reconcile the bond by the resulting obligation, not solely by whether an existing verdict was contradicted. If a confirmed/accepted result ends the rejection obligation, release it. The test does not ask to penalize a parent who had not yet judged.

### C. A permitted audit after a human override uses the obsolete original verdict

**Priority:** High — can reward disagreement as agreement, or leave a partly settled case stuck.  
**Rules:** Human override is the ruling before vesting (v0.6 §15.2). Audit agreement/disagreement controls the challenge path (inherited §8.3).  
**Code:** `sim/engine.py:483–501`, `705–714`, `766–798`, and the lookup at `655–660`.  
**Tests:** Both parameters of `test_r5_c_follow_on_audit_uses_live_ruling_or_is_refused`.

Common setup:

1. C says N.
2. H overrides to F before vesting. C is correctly marked overturned.
3. The engine permits a fresh audit because `outcome_or_verdict()` is F.
4. However, `_audit_result()` compares the auditor against `c.verdict`, still the old N, not the live F outcome.

Two consequences:

| Auditor says | Observed behavior |
|---|---|
| N | Treated as agreeing. No challenge opens. The case vests F; the auditor receives the ten-credit fee in the zero-cost, zero-emission fixture and one positive reliability outcome. |
| F | Treated as disagreeing. Another reviewer also says F. Completion raises `RuleError: no completed initial review` because C was already overturned. Before raising, the second review has been marked done and the challenge has been marked resolved, while `open_items` remains 1. Existing checks pass this stuck state. |

This review does **not** select whether an additional audit should be permitted after a human ruling. The independent test permits an atomic refusal at audit assignment as a valid repair. If the action is admitted, it must operate on a coherent live assessment and liability basis. It cannot compare against an obsolete verdict while settling against the new one.

**Correction direction:** Define or enforce the follow-on-action boundary after a human ruling, and use a consistent judgment being reviewed through assignment, comparison and settlement. Avoid attempting to forfeit an already-forfeited original review.

### D. Replacement of a timed-out reviewer loses the resubmission stage

**Priority:** Medium — changes the allowed assessment/response path.  
**Rule:** v0.5 §6.1, inherited by v0.6, makes a revision/evidence response a resubmission assessed as F or N, and limits revisions.  
**Code:** `sim/engine.py:415–426`, `443–455`, `470–481`, `556–568`; `Review.resumed` defaults to False.  
**Tests:** Two parameters of `test_r5_d_replacement_retains_resubmitted_result_constraints`.

Sequence:

1. C returns P; A chooses revise and resubmits.
2. C times out during the resumed review.
3. D replaces C on the same submitted result.
4. D's new review object has `resumed=False`, so D may return P again.

The corresponding BOT → supply evidence → timeout → replacement path also permits another BOT. In the P case the actor has already consumed its one revision, while the evaluator can again create a partial-response state. In the BOT case the API permits another evidence-supply round.

The current F/N-only restriction is carried by the reviewer assignment, not the stage of the submitted case. Replacing the reviewer implicitly resets it.

**Correction direction:** State explicitly whether the replacement inherits the existing resubmission stage. If the inherited rule remains F/N-only for that result, preserve it across replacement. If fresh evidence objections should be allowed, that is a stated rule change, not a side effect of allocating a new dataclass. The normal P on an original submission is a passing control.

### E. Parent submission after an unsubmitted child's timeout is admitted, then rejected by an invariant

**Priority:** Medium — an admitted operation produces a state the engine itself prohibits.  
**Rule:** Parent submission requires a defined child milestone (inherited §9.1); missed child delivery reaches final N.  
**Code:** `sim/engine.py:402–413`, `880–903`; `sim/invariants.py:100–109`.  
**Test:** `test_r5_e_parent_submission_after_child_timeout_is_consistent_or_refused`.

B's child task misses its delivery deadline before submitting anything. It becomes `FINAL_N` but has no `t_verdict`. A then submits the parent:

- `submit()` accepts, since FINAL_N is not in its blocking-state list.
- I7 rejects: the child never reached an initial verdict.

The test does not dictate how the model should interpret this terminal child. Either it counts as an allowed terminal milestone, or parent submission is refused atomically under the existing rule. The current implementation does both: accepts the transition and declares it invalid afterward.

**Correction direction:** Make the admission guard and child-milestone invariant use the same definition, including non-delivery terminal outcomes.

### F. The appeal explanation is delivered to reviewers but not committed to the authoritative history

**Priority:** Medium — important admitted decision data is not protected by the recorded commitment.  
**Rule:** v0.6 §14 says participant actions are committed and the appeal view contains the challenger explanation.  
**Code:** `sim/engine.py:663–678` stores `d` in the mutable challenge but logs only challenge kind and challenger; `457–468` supplies `d` to the reviewer.  
**Test:** `test_r5_f_challenger_explanation_is_bound_to_history`.

Two otherwise identical worlds admitted these different grounds:

- `The required log is attached.`
- `The log was never requested.`

Their reviewer views contain different text. Their History heads are exactly equal:

```
b48d0fa55fb163cd3328746a5447687ad885760cce073a3034710d6044f33f57
```

This is **not a hash collision**. The explanation was not included in the hashed payload. There is no deliberate mutation of the trusted head or external-memory attack in this test; it compares two legitimate input sequences.

Consequently the chain proves that an appeal was opened by a named challenger, but does not bind the actual grounds that its reviewers examined. This is separate from the intentionally bracketed production evaluator and request body: the implementation already accepts this string and uses it in an implemented view.

**Correction direction:** Commit the material admitted parameters, including the explanation or a commitment to it, rather than only a human-readable event summary. Make the record used by subsequent views refer to that admitted payload.

### G. Working-copy checking detects modifications, but not omitted entries

**Classification:** Optional integrity hardening / claim-scope qualification. Not the same severity as a valid operation producing an incorrect financial settlement.  
**Rule/claim:** v0.6 §14 and I16 describe checking working copies against authoritative entries.  
**Code:** `sim/invariants.py:246–258`; it iterates over entries still present in each copy.  
**Tests:** Two parameters of `test_r5_g_working_copy_omissions_are_detected`.

The following each pass all checks:

- Clear the case's entire working log.
- Remove transfer events from the ledger's working event list, while leaving the transfers' effects and all canonical History entries unchanged.

The hash head is unchanged and the canonical history verifies. Transfer deletion does not affect I1's mints-minus-burns calculation. I16 checks remaining entries but does not establish that the copies contain every corresponding canonical entry exactly once and in order.

**Limit of this finding:** The authoritative history is not lost; it can still contain the missing events. The current check verifies integrity of present entries, not completeness of the copies. Whether completeness is required should be stated honestly. A driver or analysis that uses the canonical history directly is less affected than one using these copies as complete histories.

**Correction direction:** Either describe that narrower guarantee, or reconcile copy membership/order to the relevant canonical entries. No external trust anchor or new cryptographic protocol is required. Editing an existing copied event remains a passing detection control.

## 3. What the test results establish

The regressions are not all independent failure modes and were not sampled to estimate reliability. The parameterized cases deliberately show paired outcomes of the same issue. The two G tests are separately labeled hardening expectations and should not be presented as evidence that the canonical hash chain was broken.

Existing invariants do provide useful detection in B and E. The problem there is timing: the action has already mutated the case and, in B, settlement has paid before the next check raises. Other new counterexamples satisfy all current checks. In particular:

- I5 checks the stored `open_items` but does not reconstruct the obligation lost by an unfunded dispute.
- A live `outcome` and an original `verdict` can differ without a check ensuring that later audits use the intended one.
- I17 catches an ended rejection bond but does not release it itself.
- I16 does not establish that every material admitted action parameter was put in the chain, nor that working copies are complete.

These are gaps in conformance/coverage. They are not emergent economic incentives. A policy might discover them in an unrestricted run, but income from them would be an implementation artifact unless the model explicitly intended the behavior.

## 4. Recommended scope of the next pass

Do not introduce a new score, fee or role in response to these findings. Keep the current economics and finish the transition boundaries:

1. Make audit takeover and overrides preserve obligations under refusal, direct acceptance and follow-on actions.
2. Keep the submission stage independent of which reviewer happens to hold the current position; reconcile terminal child milestones.
3. Commit the action parameters actually supplied to evaluators. Describe or strengthen copy completeness separately.

The existing passing traces remain useful, and strategy work can be developed against an explicitly constrained set of traces. Before interpreting unrestricted strategy-driven results, repair these cases or explicitly exclude them from the experiment's permitted operations. This review makes no claim that the list is exhaustive or that a fully passing test suite would prove the incentives sound.
