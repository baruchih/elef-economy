## What I would put to the next decision, in order

1. **The troops' working capital at 50/50, or the cannot-serve protocol.** The two accounts work wherever the cap keeps lateness small and fail where it does not, because the state's capacity now sits behind Ω's old debts. Three candidates, one of which is the one already held: (a) reverse the order under stress — advances before liabilities older than a period while the queue is above some length, since the debts are paid by the deliveries the advances buy; (b) a working-capital target for the troop account above R_T — a period's expected summons capital — before any surplus flows; (c) the cannot-serve protocol, which bounds the debt at the source: the withdrawer's cell now shows a solvent Ω at 60/40, and at 50/50 the queue of 609 is the customers who should be told the line is busy. The take does not do it (15% at 50/50 still leaves 7,600 owed).
2. **The take at 60/40 is 10%**, if the reviewer and Baruchi want plants unfunded exactly zero in every seed there (3 of 20 seeds carried 2–8 at 5%); a default, with the honest richer than under 1.6.0 at that rate because the surcharge doubles too.
3. **The stock never clears once the population is honest** (adaptive: 12,500 at the end, 5 of 20 seeds at zero). A liability with no income to pay it is a bookkeeping fact, not a mechanism; whether it is written down, paid from the fee at a higher take, or avoided by cannot-serve is a design question.
4. **The honest are 12–25% poorer than under 1.6.0** at 60–80%, by the troops' share of their former uplift, and five to six times richer than a stamper. Baruchi's call, with the numbers beside it.
5. **Drift at m_max 2: false approvals 80 against 64.** More state deliveries mean more pinned approvals in the window of the auditor's lag; read with Q14 before anything is moved.

What held: Hypothesis 1 (solvent at 60/40; plants unfunded 0.7, not 0), 3 (the state's share unchanged where refusals fall), 4 (poorer and far ahead), 6 at 60/40. What did not: 2 (50/50 collapses rather than resolving), 5 (the stock halves, does not clear), 6 at 50/50, and the accounts alone under the half cap, which the text did not predict.

Untouched: §18.1 questions 2, 4, 5, 7, 10.

**The two splits are the same through the second period** (on time, the panels the state's, Ω near 1,800 at both) and **part at the third**, when the records determine and the stampers are suspended: ten members remain at 50/50 against eight at 60/40, and that is the difference. With the cap at two thirds, about five members are on duty at both splits; the other five at 50/50 (three at 60/40) deliver — three committed at any moment against two — and **one or two of them are free at the seating step on most ticks**. The award summons of 1.4.0 §12.3 is read by the engine per tick: a request is a troop's only when **no eligible member is free to commit**, so one free member at step 5 blocks every award summons that tick however long the queue. Across the 10-tick samples from tick 180 on, 50/50 spends **79% of its ticks with five or more requests waiting (289 on average) and a member free (2.0 on average) — no summons**; 60/40 spends 54% so, with 14 waiting and 1.5 free, but the members at 60/40 are all committed often enough that the state summons 21 deliveries per 10 ticks (the median) and the queue never forms. At 50/50 the median is 0: the summonses come in **bursts** on the ticks when no member happens to be free — the first at tick 288 on average, 38 deliveries at once (the p90 window is 23, the largest 82); 70% of all troop deliveries at 50/50 are summoned in 10-tick windows of twenty or more. The queue grows between bursts at about a request a tick (three posted, about 1.8 vested), and it is the queue's age that is the lateness and the liability.

**Where the money enters.** The two 50/50 cells — 1.7.0 and the 1.6.0 control — share this stall exactly (74% of the control's ticks). Their Ω paths differ from tick 110 (the first troop seat's fee is Ω's under 1.7.0, the fund's under 1.6.0) and their **service paths diverge at tick 504 on average (range 320–690)**, when the control's cumulative troop deliveries pull 20% ahead. What differs there is the size of the bursts and who pays for the stall: (a) a burst is bounded by what can be advanced at that tick — under 1.7.0 Ω's balance above the plant reserve, under 1.6.0 the surcharge-fed fund plus Ω — so the control's bursts are larger (p90 43, largest 189) and the refusals for funds in a 1.7.0 burst are its tail (462 case-ticks in period 3 with Ω at 1,193, as the first forty deliveries take 800); (b) the stall's lateness is paid at the first verdict — in cash by Ω under 1.7.0 (rebates paid 602 by the end against the control's liability), as a liability under the control; (c) each burst sets R_T as the peak capital held (805 twenty ticks after the first burst), so the troop account retains the burst's earnings for two periods instead of passing them to Ω; and (d) from tick 396 on average (period 5) **Ω's balance above the plant reserve is consumed by liabilities older than a period before any advance**, after which the bursts are small (805–3,845 case-ticks refused per period) and the control, whose fund still has the surcharge, pulls ahead — 110–141 deliveries a period against 24–39. The collapse is the stall; the two accounts decide how it is financed and lose to the merged fund once the debts rank first.

**For the record, a reading, not a change:** the engine's test for "no member free to commit" is one free member at the seating step. The other reading — a request has waited $w_A$ unawarded and the free fundable members are fewer than the requests waiting, so the excess is the state's — would summon for the queue beyond the free members. Whether that is the rule's meaning or a new rule is the reviewer's to say; the trace says what the per-tick reading costs at 50/50, and that the same test is why Hypothesis 2 of 1.6.0 came out inverted in run 8 (at 80/20 the four members are never free, so the state is never blocked).

**T_susp moves who is free, and the stall follows.** At T_susp = 0 a suspended stamper is a probationer at the next rotation, takes initial seats (3–4 probationers on duty), is re-suspended at its next failed plant and is back on duty the tick after — 460–570 suspensions a run. The probationers carry the initial seats, so fewer members sit on duty (3–4 against 5–6) and more are free to deliver: careful awards 195 against 162 at 50/50. **At 50/50 that helps** — the queue 173 against 609, on-time 0.70, the liability 5,963, plants unfunded zero — and **at 60/40 it breaks the market** (queue 519 against 42, on-time 0.43, the liability 13,497), for the reason of (1): with more members free at the seating step the state is blocked nearly always (78 troop deliveries against 1,719), and eight careful members cannot deliver three tasks a tick alone. **Honesty pays for it at both splits:** false approvals 26 and 23 against 11 and 14, the probationers' 17 and 15 against 3 — the stampers review on probation almost continuously, every approval pinned and audited, and the audit's 5% miss on a thousand more pinned approvals is the leak; the stampers end two and a half times richer (1,590 / 1,376 against 611 / 651) on probation fees. **The audit load on members** rises with the probationers' seats: audits by members 1,127 / 869 at T_susp 0 against 858 / 1,160 at 100 — at 50/50 up by a third, at 60/40 down because the members are no longer on duty to take them and the troops take 715. T_susp = 50 is between on everything: better than 100 at 50/50 (queue 165, liability 9,493, false approvals 14), worse at 60/40 (queue 61, liability 2,765, plants unfunded 12). **No value of T_susp is right at both splits**, and none repairs 50/50 on its own: the lever it pulls is how many members are free to deliver, which is the same lever the stall reads the other way.

**What I would put to the decision now, in place of item 1 above:** the summons test. The 50/50 collapse is the per-tick reading of "no member free to commit" — the state is blocked while a queue of hundreds waits and two members are free — and the two accounts only decide how the bursts between blocks are financed. A ruling on the reading (summon for the queue beyond the free members) would be tested first, since it touches the mechanism itself; the cannot-serve protocol remains the bound on the debt whatever the reading; T_susp is not the lever.
