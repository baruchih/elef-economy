## Verdict

Buildable as four flags on the engine plus the driver's seating, with the corrections below. Eight places where the words meet the code and need changing; three questions whose answers change what is measured; one conformance detail that has to be settled or every arm over-sizes the duty roster. ## The deciding verdict — four corrections

**D1. The seats are Reviews on the troop seat's road, and the path's positions must admit them.** The engine allows `V_max` = 3 review positions on a challenge path (I8, `_positions` l.1572; `_new_review` l.1578). A real case's path today is at most initial + audit + third seat, or initial + two panel seats — three. A deciding verdict of two or three seats on top of an audit or a panel reaches five or six. Two roads exist in the engine: (a) the orchestrator's own verdict as a History-head coin with no Review, no fee, no loan and no place — what `_state_verdict_on_plant` (l.3775) does on a plant past w_R; (b) a troop's Review through `_summon_review` (l.4366), which purchases c_T from Ω at the summons (a bank loan when Ω is short, never refused for a seat), locks G_V as the seat's bond, takes a place in N_T, is reimbursed from the root's allowance up to K and earns the role's fee from the fee pot. The brief wants the deciding verdict "paid as a troop's seat is paid" and read "at cost", so it is (b): under the flag I8 reads `V_max` + the deciding verdict's seats on the case, and `_summon_review` is called for them directly, bypassing the w_R wait and the free-member test. Road (a) would hide the cost the brief wants to measure.

**D2. The verdict is the driver's, as every troop's is; "at once" is the next tick.** The engine has no strategies: a troop's review is completed by the driver (`complete_review`, l.1733, with cost 0), the truth with probability 1 − ε_T being the driver's disposition (`eps_T` is declared in `Params` l.90 as the default the driver reads). So the deciding verdict is seated by the engine in the tick's summons pass and answered in the driver's next reviewing pass, one tick later, exactly as a troop's audit seat is today and as the planted verdict is (`plant_verdict` the tick after `plant_seat`). The brief's "taken at once" should read "seated in the tick's pass, answered at the next tick's reviews". The third seat on a split is seated the tick after the two answer, so a split costs one more tick. Step 5's shared difficulty reaches these seats through the driver's verdict, where the brief puts it.

**D3. Its accuracy.** Two seats at ε_T = 0.05, independent, and a third on a split: wrong with probability ε² + 2ε(1 − ε)·ε ≈ 0.0073. The brief's "under one in a hundred" is right; hypothesis 3's figure is this.

**D4. Its money, stated.** Each seat purchases c_T = 3 (indexed) from Ω and is reimbursed from the root's expense allowance up to K = 5 while the allowance lasts (`_reimburse` l.1693: target min(cost, K)); it claims the role's fee w·f = 3 from the fee pot at settlement, pro rata with every surviving review when the pot is short (`_settle_reviews` l.2385). The root's pots are φ^E = 10 and φ^F = 5 — sized for one review and one audit (`Params` l.26–27). A deciding verdict of three seats on a case already carrying an initial review and an audit asks the allowance for up to 9 more and the pot for 9 more against 5. So, as the engine stands, the customer's pots are drawn further than today (the "cost beyond the price" rises by what is left in them, at most) and the rest is the state's: unreimbursed cost stays with Ω, the fees scale down for members and state alike. The brief must say which it wants: the pots as they are (the customer pays up to the pots' remainder, the state the rest — my recommendation: nothing minted, nothing topped up, the state's shortfall read), or the pots topped up to the seats' need as a plant's are (`_plant_top_up`, bank-minted — which would put the state's seats on the bank's account and change the money reading). The "overhead to the system" reading then carries the state's c_T burns in full and its unreimbursed part separately.

**The state's own seats carry no bond risk.** Nothing overturns a deciding verdict's seat; a split's minority seat stands and is paid like a troop's seat. Today a troop's seat that is overturned burns its bond and closes its loan as debt (`_overturn_review` l.2105, l.2122). Under the flag no state seat on the deciding verdict is ever overturned. Say so.

## Arm 1 — the trusted ruling: three corrections and one question

**A1.1. The audit's disagreement keeps the challenge record.** Today a disagreeing audit opens a challenge of kind `"audit"` with the audit review as its first panel review (`_audit_result` l.1998 → `_open_challenge` l.1920, `is_audit_as_appeal`), a second seat is a member's through `assign_appeal` (or a troop's after w_R), agreement settles it, a split goes to the driver's `rule` (l.2042). Under the flag the second seat and the ruling are replaced by the deciding verdict — but the challenge record should still be opened, with the state's seats in `challenge["state_verdicts"]` as the plant path already records them (l.3812), and settled through `_settle_challenge(c, outcome, by="deciding verdict")`. Then the channel reader (`fa_channels.py` reads `c.challenge["kind"]` and the panel's composition), the stage tables and I25's dispute branch keep working unchanged, and the court fee moves as today (`pay_fee_to` the challenger from the overturned initial review's bond; an audit challenge has no challenger and no fee).

**A1.2. A member panel can confirm but no longer overturn on its own — spell it out.** Today two panelists who agree settle the challenge either way (`_maybe_settle_challenge` l.2038): two N's on an appealed F overturn the initial review and burn its bond on the panel's word. The brief's rule ("if both confirm the standing verdict it stands; otherwise the deciding verdict") makes a member panel's agreement final only when it confirms. That is what "bonds burn only against trusted truth" requires, and it is a change from today's rule, so the flag's text should say it plainly: a member panel confirms or escalates; it never overturns.

**A1.3. The state's own work.** Under 1.4.0 §2.3 / I25 only a member audits the state's delivery, and Appendix V's second reading admits a member's audit after a dispute whose panel was the state's alone — the principle being that the state does not judge its own work. Under arm 1 a member's audit that rejects the state's delivery escalates to the state's deciding verdict, which is the state judging its own work. Either exempt `market_audit_only` cases from the flag (the member third seat and the driver's ruling as today — my recommendation, I25's purpose kept), or accept it and say so; the brief's "I25 unchanged" does not decide this.

**Q1 (a question, not a correction).** Under arm 1 a dispute panel that confirms a false approval is final and unchecked — the same seat the own-money population at 2.7.0 stamped 0.90–0.94 and that the hand's requesters won 97% of where the learned ones won 69–75%. The deciding verdict only reaches the panel when it does not confirm. So hypothesis 1's "the leak does not rise" depends on the plants reaching the dispute panel (2.8.0, still on under arm 1) and on nothing else; the channel "disputed, panel confirmed (a member sat)" is where it would show. Does the reviewer want a confirming panel sampled to the deciding verdict at a stated rate, as an arm 1b? Without it, arm 1 tests the bond's incentive at the dissenting seat only.

**Out of scope, stated.** The ⊥ appeal's objection path (`rule` with an objection, l.2058) is left as today under the flag; no population has ever answered P or ⊥, so nothing is measured there. `override` (the human) is untouched.

## Arm 2 — the state's audit: two corrections and the conformance detail

**A2.1. Where the seat is taken.** The roll is the driver's (`roll_audit` l.4153 records the obligation at `audit_odds`); today the summons pass takes an unseated obligation after w_R with no free eligible member (`_summon_troops`, the audit branch). Under `state_audit` the pass takes every obligation on a member's approval of a member's delivery in the same tick's pass, no wait, no free-member test (D2's latency applies), and `assign_audit` refuses a member on such a case. The pinned reviewers' approvals (odds 1) are included: every audit of members' work is the state's. On agreement the approval stands; on disagreement the seat is the deciding verdict's first and one more seat is taken (a third on a split), through A1.1's record.

**A2.2. The plants' audit seat goes with it, and the base of π^A is zero by construction.** With no member at the audit seat, a plant's audit obligation "discharged at once with no seat" is right — the audit seat leaves the plants' reach, which is consistent, and should be said: under arm 2 the plants test the first review and the panels only. `planted_approval` off follows (its audit seat was a member's or nobody's, and `real_member_audits` — its base — is zero).

**A2.3. The accounting, so hypothesis 2's income line reads right.** In the hand's world a run has about 960 member audits. Each becomes a state seat: c_T = 3 burned by Ω and reimbursed from the allowance (the allowance holds it: the initial review's care ≤ 5 plus 3 is within 10), the audit's fee 3 taken from the pot that was sized for exactly that audit — so the members' income falls by their audit fees, as the hypothesis says, the state's fee income rises by the same, and the customer pays nothing more. The state's cost is the c_T burn, about 2,900 a run at the index; that is the number "the orchestrator's seats counted at cost" means, and the reading must carry it beside the fees.

**C1 — the conformance detail every arm shares.** The reserve target and the member seats are sized on audit and panel demand (`_reserve_target`, `_rotate_membership`: waiting initial + waiting panel + the period's rate; member seats on applied audit odds plus the challenge rate). Under `state_audit` and `state_bench` those seats are the state's, but the roster would still be sized for them: members held on duty for seats they can never take, paying the duty tax for nothing. Under the flags the sizing must exclude the demand the state now carries, or the arms' member-income and overhead readings carry an artefact. This has to be settled before building; my proposal is to exclude it, and to read the duty roster's size in every arm.

## Arm 3 — the state's bench: one correction

**A3.1. Plant challenges stop; the truthful ones too.** Under `plants_every_seat` the plant actor appeals and the plant requester disputes at the real rates (2.8.0 §2.2), adjudicated by truth. Under arm 3 the deciding verdict decides every challenge, so a plant's challenge would be decided by the state on a case whose truth it already knows — pointless; the brief's "plants file no challenge" is right and must cover the truthful plant challenges as well as the lies (`planted_lie` off covers only the lies). The driver's `plant_respond` and challenge pass skip plants under the flag. The audit seat on a plant stays a member's (arm 1), so the plants test the first review and the audit seat under arm 3.

**The dispute's discharge.** `_discharge_obligation_at_dispute` (l.3662) discharges a standing audit obligation when the dispute settles; under arm 3 the settlement is the deciding verdict's, which is stronger than an audit, so the discharge stands. For the state's own work disputed, Appendix V's rule (a member's audit after a state-alone panel) applies to every dispute under arm 3, since every panel is the state's; I25 then holds as today. State it.

## Arm 4 — the small core: two corrections

**A4.1. "Plants as 2.7.0 had them" must not turn off 2.8.0's audit roll.** `plants_every_seat` carries two things: the plants' later seats, and (D) — the roll at the odds of the verdict (`audit_odds` returns `audit_odds_at_verdict` under the flag; `_live_audit_odds` is 2.7.0's read). Turning the flag off for arm 4 would also revert the real cases' roll to the live odds, a second change bundled into the arm. Keep `plants_every_seat` on; arms 2 and 3 already make a plant's later seats vacuous (no member audit seat, no plant challenge), so the plant closes at its first verdict as the brief wants and (D) stays. If the reviewer wants arm 4 read against 2.7.0's roll too, that is a separate cell.

**A4.2. Resumed reviews.** "Members hold initial reviews and resumed reviews": a resumed review follows a P or ⊥ answer and a revision, and no population in the record has ever answered P or ⊥. True by construction; nothing to build.

**On hypothesis 4's last clause.** The state's share of review seats: in the hand's world about 330 of 3,600 review seats a run are the state's (a third of the audits); under arm 4 every audit and panel seat is the state's, about 1,200 — the share rises from roughly a tenth to a third. "Roughly doubles" understates it; the reading will say.

## Arm 5 — no plants: three corrections to the sketch (second wave)

**A5.1. The record's entries.** The record 1.2.0 keeps is two windows per actor, `plant_bad` (bad plants: approved?) and `plant_good` (good plants: rejected?) (`Actor` l.98–99), with the Laplace rates `a_hat`, `r_hat` against θ_duty and θ_panel on at least n_min entries of a kind. A state adjudication of a member's verdict on a real case maps onto them with the deciding verdict standing in for the truth: a member's F found N → an entry in `plant_bad` (approved); a member's F confirmed F → `plant_good` (not rejected); a member's N overturned to F on appeal → `plant_good` (rejected); a member's N confirmed → `plant_bad` (not approved). The state only sees a member's N when it is appealed, so the `plant_good` window fills from appeals alone — slowly; and every entry carries the deciding verdict's 0.7% error (or a single state audit seat's 5% where it agreed and no verdict was completed — the sketch should say the agreeing audit seat is one seat at ε_T, not the deciding verdict).

**A5.2. Admission.** An arrival is admitted on n_min bad plants adjudicated with a passing record (`_check_record` l.3835). With no plants an arrival is never admitted. The sketch must restate admission on n_min state adjudications of bad work, and expect it to take longer: a probationer's approvals are all audited, but bad work is about a fifth of deliveries, so n_min = 4 bad-work entries need about twenty audited approvals.

**A5.3. The dial's input.** F̂_t today is the bad plants' approval rate at the first review, against F* = 0.10. Under arm 5 the state audits only approvals, so the statistic available is the share of audited approvals found wrong — the leak before the audit, not the approval rate of bad work. The sketch says the dial "moves the roll's floor in place of the plant rate"; m_t already scales every member's odds (`p_eff` = min(1, m_t p_j), l.177) and the floor (p_floor m_t for a troop's approval), so the mechanism exists — but the threshold F* needs restating for the new statistic, and I21's step bounds read m_t the same.

## The readings — four notes

1. **The leak, both figures:** the driver's standing false approvals over vested roots; over all bad deliveries (submitted tasks with `fulfilled` False). Both from columns the driver has. By channel: `fa_channels.py` as corrected November 6, with one new channel for arms 1–4, "escalated, deciding verdict" (confirmed / overturned), read from `challenge["by"]`.
2. **The plants' shadow cost:** a plant's own awarded price B is recorded at its award (the market's report); use it, not the trailing B̄ — exact, per plant, and the plant actor burns no effort, so nothing is double-counted. The cost of truth stays as defined (minted pots less what returned, with emission).
3. **The customer's cost beyond the price:** the pots consumed (φ^E + φ^F less the closure refund), the fee ω₀ m B, court fees paid less reimbursed, less rebates received — per case from the ledger's events on the requester, as `seat_income.py` reads the fees. Under D4 as recommended this moves only where the deciding verdict draws the pots further.
4. **False penalties:** a bond burned (`burn_locked`, note "bond burned", keyed by review id) on a review whose verdict equals the task's truth, by seat kind. A plant's seat adjudicated by truth is never a false penalty by construction; a deciding verdict's 0.7% error is the floor under arm 1.

**Hypothesis 1's "one time in five" is a ceiling.** Today a correct auditor's rejection is burned only on a split the ruling gets wrong: the third seat must stamp F (the own-money population's panel stamp 0.41–0.56 at 2.9.0, 0.04–0.26 in the liar chain) and the ruling err (0.2) — about one in ten in the careful basin, one in five only where the third seat always stamps. The comparison under arm 1 is against that, not 0.2.
