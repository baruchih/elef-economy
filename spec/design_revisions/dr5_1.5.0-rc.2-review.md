# Review of 1.5.0-rc.2 — design revision 5, "the reserve is sized to the need"

**Date:** October 17, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_1.5.0-rc.2.md` · **Against:** the rc.1 review (`dr5_1.5.0-rc.1-review.md`), the 1.4.0 text in force, the engine at `f0779ac` · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict.** rc.2 adopts the six points of the rc.1 review and the smaller ones, each in the proposed form. The rule is now one statement the engine can carry out in one place — the end of the summons pass — with one running maximum reset once a period, and the text, the example, the invariant and the tests agree with each other on what falls, what holds, and when. I find nothing that contradicts the baseline or itself. The text is implementable as it stands.

## The six points, checked

1. **A peak holds; the period is one.** §2.1 now says it: $D_s$ falls as the backlog is served, $R_T$ stands at the period's peak until the boundary, and restarts there at the standing demand. The engine's order inside a tick fits without rearrangement: the batch (step 3) pays uplift against the old peak, the period ends (step 5) and the peak is reset to the capital still held, and the same tick's summons pass takes the maximum with that seating's $D_s$ — which is "the demand then standing" of §2.1. The previous period's peak is dropped; Appendix I records the 1.4.0 two-period reading as superseded (§7). The recorded-and-rejected "reserve that tracks the stock" is the (b) of my review, kept as a question for Q13.
2. **Refused means refused for funds.** §2.1 names the three returns that are not refusals (below $\bar B$, cannot pay in, path full), and §7 and the tests pin them to add nothing. That is exactly the branch that already increments `troop_shortfall_*`.
3. **A case's demand is its unfilled seats.** Two for a panel short of both, one otherwise; "once per seating" is about ticks. The engine's panel loop stops at the first refusal and the demand adds both seats' need, as §7 says; a panel with one seat carried and one refused adds one, since the carried seat is in capital held.
4. **The refused cost is the carry check's own `need`.** At indexation — the delivery's $c^D_T+G$ with $G=\max(1,\lceil G_{frac}\bar B\rceil)$, the seat's $c_T$ plus the case's indexed bond — computed before the check as now.
5. **§6 revised.** A delivery's capital held to vesting, about thirteen ticks; steady demand $13\times20+8\approx270$; the ramp cash-negative on each new cohort (20 out, 30 back thirteen ticks later) with the uplift stopped until the unlocked balance crosses the peak; the uplift resuming inside the period at about 16 a tick. The arithmetic checks: a vested troop delivery returns $B=20$ plus the released $G=10$ against the 10 purchased, $+10$ realised; the surcharge at $m=2$ is 3 a tick for three awards; $33-20=+13$. The 1.4.0 §6 figure of 48 is acknowledged as the same understatement.
6. **§2.3 by route, then by kind.** Upheld (auditor missed) or rejected-and-overturned (ruling erred), and four auditor kinds on the upheld route including the readmitted member. All the driver's, attributed after the run; it holds every audit's reviewer id and sees every suspension and every ruling. Q14 restated to match.

The smaller points: I23 made checkable ($R_T\ge$ capital held, $R_T\ge D_s$ at the last seating, over explicit readings); the period record carries peak demand and its refused component; the carried-at-the-next-seating test; the goldens re-pinned once; `--decline-awards` and the requester flood named for §8.6. Naming settled in §1.6 and §9.

## For the record, not changes

- **What the uplift check compares.** As in 1.4.0, the check is the fund's *unlocked* balance against $R_T$, and $R_T$ counts capital *held* — costs purchased plus bonds and guarantees locked. The locked part already sits in the wallet as a lock and is not payable as uplift; the check asks on top of it for unlocked money enough to carry the demand again. That is the right reading — the reserve is the capacity to summon the demand now, and a summons draws from unlocked balance — but it means the fund's total at the moment uplift resumes is about $R_T$ plus the locks then held, not $R_T$ alone. §6's "unlocked balance crosses the peak of about 280–300" is consistent with this; I note it so the §6 reproduction is not read against the wrong figure.
- **"At rest, $D_s=0$ for a whole period, $R_T=0$."** The reserve reaches zero at the first boundary after demand has fallen to zero and stays there; a whole period of zeros is sufficient, not necessary. Hypothesis 3 says "one period after the last troop", which is the upper bound. No change needed.
- **Partial advances on a refusal.** `_fund_can_carry` may draw part of Ω's advance and still refuse; the advance sits in the fund until the batch repays it. Under 1.5.0 the refusal adds the full `need` to demand, as §2.1 says, and the unused advance is repaid as now. No change.
