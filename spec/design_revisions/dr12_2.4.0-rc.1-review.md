# Review of 2.4.0-rc.1 — design revision 12, "the state's reservation"

**Date:** October 19, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_2.4.0-rc.1.md` · **Against:** the 2.3.0 text in force, the engine at `4812ee36` (drivers `95081c19`), run 17 · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict. Implementable as it stands, with two corrections and eight readings.** The rule is one line in one place: `_summon_delivery` prices at $p_T=\max(\bar B, R)$ and declines below it, the burn and the guarantee follow $p_T$, the clock's running condition becomes $\beta\ge p_T$ (today it is $\beta\ge\bar B$), and a flag with a tick sits beside `below_market`. Every quiet cell of runs 12–17 has $\bar B\ge 20$, so the floor at 8 is dormant there and the control is the default off. The two corrections are to the text, not the design: the kill fee is the child's *recorded* cost, not a declared one, which changes what §2.4 and Hypothesis 5 should expect; and the symbol $R_T$ is already the fund's troop reserve in Appendix C, §12.3 and the engine.

## The corrections

**1. The kill fee is what the child has spent, and a child that never worked has spent nothing.** §9.3 (v1.0) sets the fee at $\min(C_B, K)$ with $C_B$ "B's execution cost on $q'$ as §4.6 defines it" — §4.6's $C_{iq}$ is the credits the actor *burned* on the case: resource purchases, nested prices paid, kill fees paid. The engine reads it so (`cancel_nested`: `fee = min(c.actor_cost, c.K)`, `actor_cost` accumulated from the case's own spend). A lowballer that bids half its cost and never submits has `actor_cost` 0; the fee is 0; on cancellation its guarantee is released (§9.3, "subcontractor guarantee released") and the parent's escrow returns. So §6's "the lowballer's declared cost on a job it never did, small" and Hypothesis 5's "what remains — the fee paid for nothing — is read" should expect **nothing paid** — the cancellation costs the parent only the delay, and the lowballer neither gains nor loses a credit. The driver change is right and should go in; the hypothesis should say that the kill fee closes the parent's loss entirely and leaves the child's miss free, which is the asymmetry the rc names as the next candidate (a nested guarantee floored to the parent's exposure, or a guarantee not released on a cancellation the child's non-performance forced). Nothing in the rule moves; the expectation does.

**2. $R_T$ is taken.** Since 1.4.0 $R_T$ is the fund's troop reserve (§12.3, Appendix C, I23, I24; retired by 2.0.0 but kept in the text as the controls' rule and in the engine as `World.R_T`, the view's `fund_reserve`, the period record's `R_T`). A second $R_T$ in §2.1 and I37 would make Appendix C say two things and the engine's readings ambiguous. Propose $\underline{B}$ — "the state's floor", read with $\bar B$ the market's level and $p_T=\max(\bar B,\underline{B})$ the state's price — and `Params.state_reservation` for the flag as the rc has it (not `p_floor`, which is the audit odds' floor). The driver's `--state-reservation 0|4|8|12`.

## Readings

1. **The clock is one condition.** Today `_clock_runs` is "a live root, not a plant, $\beta \ge \bar B$ while unawarded" (the below-market stop). §2.2's "stopped while below $R_T$, as a below-market request's is" is the same test at $p_T$: the clock runs while $\beta\ge p_T$. I will implement it as that one condition and read I37 so; the below-market flag keeps its 2.0.0 condition (set only once a member has been awarded), the reservation flag needs none — $\underline{B}$ is a constant, so a request posted at $\beta<\underline{B}$ is marked at tick 0.

2. **Requests below the state's price should leave the award pass's count.** The 1.7.1 award pass gathers every root request older than $w_A$, counts the free fundable members at the market price, leaves that many of the *oldest* to the market and summons for the rest — and `_summon_delivery` then declines the ones priced below $\bar B$. A below-market request older than $w_A$ therefore holds a member's slot it will not use, and a fundable request younger than it is summoned for only if it falls beyond the count. Under the floor every patient request sits below $p_T$ for $T$ ticks and the queue is mostly such requests, so the misallocation would be the common case rather than the edge. The rule's words — "a request unawarded for $w_A$ with no free fundable member … and $\beta\ge\bar B$" — read as a condition on the request, so the gathered list should hold only requests at $\beta\ge p_T$; this is a conformance reading of 1.7.1 that 2.4.0 makes matter, and I will make it with a regression test and say so in Appendix Q. It changes no run of 12–17 where $\bar B\le\beta$ held for nearly every request.

3. **Above $\bar B$ the state sets the price it counts.** When $\bar B<\underline{B}$ the state's awards at $\underline{B}$ enter $P_t$ while $\bar B$ (members only) follows the cheap members' bids, so in a patient cell the two readings diverge for good: $\bar B$ at 4–6, $p_T$ at 8, $P_t$ a blend weighted by the state's share. §2.1's "price-taker above the floor" is right and this is the intended consequence of the floor binding; the notes should read $P_t$, $\bar B$ and $p_T$ side by side there, and Hypothesis 1's "$P_t$ 8–10" is a statement about the blend.

4. **The burn and the guarantee at $p_T$.** `_summon_delivery` today computes $G=\lceil G_{\text{frac}}\bar B\rceil$, the base fee $\omega_0\bar B$ and the `expected` burn at $\bar B$, and the troop delivers at $\bar B$ from the requester's posting lock (which covers $\beta(1+\omega_0 m)+\varphi$, and $\beta\ge p_T$ so the pay-in fits). Every one of these moves to $p_T$ with one substitution; `half` stays at the index and `at_cost` becomes $p_T$. The posting lock is enough because the floor only ever raises the state's price toward $\beta$, never above it.

5. **I33's bound is a parameter constraint.** $F_0\times$ index at arrival against $G^V\times$ index at a case's lock: the two indices differ by the period's move, so the per-tick statement is $F_0\ge G^V$ nominally with the index cancelling — a check on `Params` at construction (`F0 >= G_V`), and I33 keeps asserting the mint was $F_0$ at the arrival index. I will put it there and in Appendix A's note. It says what run 17 found (a float of 0 admits nobody) as a rule and costs nothing.

6. **Goldens and the expected failures.** `test_perf_equivalence`'s scenario has $\bar B\ge 20$ throughout, so the floor at 8 is never met and the goldens need no re-pin unless reading 2's conformance change touches a summons there (it should not; I will check and re-pin only if it does). `EXPECTED_FAIL` is **32**, not 28 — four were added under 2.2.0 and 2.3.0 (Appendices O and P); the rc's §7 should say 32, unchanged.

7. **The organism knob (§8.8) is not a driver sweep as written.** "$w_A=2$ where $P_t\le 2R_T$ and 1 above" is a rule that reads the price level inside the engine; $w_A$ is a `Params` constant and a driver has no public operation to move it mid-run. Run it as the static sweep $w_A$ 1 against 2 on the high-price cells (run 13's margin cells and the 80/20 cell), which answers the question the knob asks — does a shorter wait take the price toward the floor and no further with the honest's real wealth unchanged. If the answer wants the dynamic rule, that is an rc of its own.

8. **Names.** Delegation lives in `drivers/stamping.py` (`delegation_step`), not `drivers/delegate.py` (proposed in the dr11 review, not made); the clock is `_clock_runs` / `_stop_clock`, not `_clock_tick`. The run 17 patient policy becomes `--ceiling-policy patient-blind` and the new one `patient` (raising to $\max(\bar B,\underline{B})$ read from the view, which will expose the floor). The parent's cancellation is `--parent-cancel on|off` on the delegation driver; the view already carries `cancel_nested`.
