# Review of 2.0.0-rc.2 — design revision 8, "the price of work"

**Date:** October 18, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_2.0.0-rc.2.md` · **Against:** rc.1 and its review, the 1.7.1 text in force, the engine at `381b9ed`, runs 10–11 · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict.** rc.2 adopts the seven points and the smaller ones, and adds three things of its own that are right: effort costs nominal as the anchor (the deflation argument in §1 is the one I had not made), the state at cost, and $N_T$ finite by default so the capacity case is run rather than deferred. The posting lock, the one-sentence retirement, the frozen index and the one flag are as the rc.1 review proposed and are implementable as written. **One definition is missing** (point 1: the riots cannot be posted by six requesters), **two defaults are off against run 10's figures** (points 2–3: $N_T=30$ binds at rest at 80/20, and issuance 15 puts the quiet price near 16, not §6's 11), and four implementation readings follow from the text that I will take unless told otherwise (points 4–7). With point 1 defined and points 2–3 decided — one line each — rc.2 is implementable; nothing in it contradicts 1.7.1 or itself.

## To settle before acceptance

1. **The riot's shape under finite wallets.** §6 and §8.4 keep run 11's hard spike as "fifteen posts a tick for 100 ticks" and the flood as six a tick sustained. Under §2.1 a requester posts at most one request a tick, so six requesters post at most six a tick; and the all-in lock (about 50 at $\beta=40$) against a wallet of 200 carries four posts each before issuance alone — 15 a tick — carries 0.3 a tick each. Fifteen a tick is not a policy six requesters can run, and six a tick lasts four ticks. The riot has to be defined as one of three things, and they measure different things: **a crowd** — more requesters arrive with wallets of their own (demand from outside, the shape run 11 had); **a burst of issuance** — the same six requesters given money (which is the deficit shock of §8.3 by another route); or **a change of policy** — posts a tick and $K_{\text{open}}$ raised, which the wallets then bound. My recommendation is the crowd: $n$ requesters added at the spike's start with the default wallet and issuance and removed at its end (their open requests served out), with $n$ chosen so the posting rate at $P_0$ is run 11's (fifteen a tick needs about thirty requesters at the default policy, six at six a tick); it keeps the spike a demand event and the shock a money event, so Hypotheses 2 and 3 stay apart. The flood is then twelve requesters sustained. State the choice in §2.1 or §8 and the driver carries it (`--spike-requesters`, `--spike-from`, `--spike-ticks`).

2. **$N_T=30$ binds at rest at 80/20, not only in riots.** §2.6 sets 30 on run 11's riot peaks (75–221). Run 10's quiet field, ticks 600–1,000, deliveries and review seats together: **18 at 50/50, 23 at 60/40 (peaks 28), 26 at 70/30 (peaks 30.5), 29 at 80/20 (peaks 34)**. At 30 the 80/20 cell is capacity-bound every tick and 70/30 at its peaks, so the tipping comparison against run 10 would carry a capacity refusal the 1.7.1 cells never had, and Hypothesis 4's "unchanged from run 10 at every split" could fail for a reason that is not honesty's. Either **$N_T=40$** — clear of the quiet field at every split, inside every riot peak — or keep 30 and say that 80/20 is capacity-bound by design and is read as such (its refusals for capacity a reading, not a surprise). I recommend 40.

3. **The quiet price under issuance 15 is about 16; §6's 11 is issuance 10's figure.** Supply at 60/40 under $N_T$ is members' 1.24 a tick (run 10's awards) plus the troops' $N_T/13.4$ (a troop delivery holds its place 13.4 ticks in run 10) — about 3.5 a tick at $N_T=30$, 4.2 at 40. A task's all-in, net of refunds, is about $1.6B$ at the defaults ($B$, the deposit's net 8 at $P_t/P_0$, the fee $\omega_0 m_t B$ with the dial at 4). Issuance 90 a tick clears at $B\approx90/(1.6\times3.5)\approx16$ with $N_T=30$ (13 at 40); issuance 60 clears at about 11 (9 at 40). So the §6 example holds at issuance 10 and the text's default is 15. Decide which: 10 as the default and 15 the sweep keeps §6's figures and leaves 60/40 just at capacity; 15 as the default makes every quiet cell mildly capacity-bound and the price about 16. Either is a coherent economy; the worked example and the default should agree. (Under unbounded $N_T$ the same arithmetic gives the state about three quarters of the market at 60/40 — the field grows to about 50 — which is what §2.4's caution predicts and the control will show.)

## Implementation readings, for the record

4. **A root's first lock is its posting.** §4.5 says a root's first lock is its award; under the posting lock it is the posting, as a nested case's already is, and the case's nominals ($D,K,G^V,\varphi$) freeze there. One more thing freezes with them: **the fee rate** — $\omega=\omega_0 m_t B$ is locked at posting at that tick's $m_t$, and if the dial rises at a period boundary before the award the fee at award would exceed the lock. The case carries its $m$ from posting; the release at award is $\beta-B$ plus $\omega_0 m(\beta-B)$. I29 then holds exactly.

5. **Effort costs nominal means the cost API is in credits.** `spend(actor, cost)` and `complete_review(rid, verdict, cost)` take a *real* cost and burn $\text{cost}/v$ credits; with `ledger.v` redefined as $P_0/P_t$ that would index the effort — the opposite of §2.3. Under the flag the engine takes the cost in credits and burns exactly that; `ledger.v` is a reading only; the troops' indexed $c_T$ is indexed by the engine before the burn. The reviewer tests run at $v=1$ and see no difference; `test_review_04` is the named expected failure either way.

6. **Issuance defaults to zero in `Params`.** Every reviewer test asserts a human's wallet after a sequence; an engine default above zero would move it each tick. The driver declares 15 (or 10, point 3) in the manifest; the engine mints it at the tick's start as the `issuance` kind, and the genesis floats and budgets are the same kind.

7. **The posting lock against the reviewer tests.** Six reviewer files fund their humans at 1,000 and post through helpers one request at a time; one test funds at 42 and one through a variable. I expect none to trip the lock, at most one; any that does becomes an expected failure with I29 as the reason. The `--rules` controls are not re-run (§7), so nothing else moves.

## Smaller

- **The delivery check's surviving clause.** 1.6.0's "a delivery is summoned only when its inspection can be carried too" was about funds for the inspection's seats; with funds moot, what it means under $N_T$ is that a delivery's summons needs room in the field for itself **and** its inspection seat (two of $N_T$), else the delivery would be fielded and its pinned audit refused for capacity — the 1.6.0 hazard in capacity terms. I will read it so; say it in §2.6 if the reading is wrong.
- **At cost, exactly.** $c^D_T=\bar B$ burned at summons and $\bar B$ received at vesting is neutral when the delivery vests; a final $\mathsf N$ forfeits $G$ and the burned $\bar B$ is not recovered — the state's loss on a failed delivery is $\bar B+G$, not "a small loss", about 1.5 $\bar B$ at the driver's fail rate times the state's volume. It is still nobody's windfall; the figure should be right.
- **`raise_ceiling` and the clock.** A below-market request's clock is stopped while $\beta<\bar B$ and runs again once raised — the 1.6.0 rule evaluated per tick, no new code; the time spent below market is not late.
- **The ledger.** `purchase` loses the backing check and the halt state; `mint_backed` at genesis becomes `issuance`; I2's "bad reserve" clause retires with $\text{Res}_t$ held at 0.
- **The deficit shock's note.** The scenario operation pays requesters from Ω through `_omega_pay` with a note I28 recognises as the declared exemption; it is a rebate in name only and the record should say "scenario: deficit shock", not "rebate", so Q15's rebate readings are not inflated by it.
- **Hypothesis 1's "near cost where members are plentiful."** At 50/50 ten members and troops at $\bar B$ share a market of three a tick; members are never plentiful in any 1.x cell (the state delivers 46–67%), so "near cost" will be read only in the adaptive run's honest tail. Fine as a hypothesis; the run that tests it is §8.7.
