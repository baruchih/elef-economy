# Review of 2.7.0-rc.1 — design revision 15, "the arrival loan"

**Date:** October 21, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_2.7.0-rc.1.md` · **Against:** the 2.6.1 text in force, the engine at `da77795c`, run 23 (`results/redteam/Q1/`, `results/studies/rt-Q1-notes-for-reviewer.md`), run 24's baseline (`results/studies/2.6.0-appeals/`) · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict. Implementable with one correction to the rule's list, three corrections of fact and eight readings.** The content — the float lent as the bank lends, repaid from the name's income first, recovered at departure, the shortfall the bank's default and read — closes run 23's finding at its cause and leaves the gate (an arrival can post its first bond) where 2.3.0 put it. It is a design revision by §19: what a departing name may carry out changes. Minor, as the rc says: two operations the loop has, one flag. The correction is to §2.1's list of what counts as income; as written it names one return as income and leaves the largest return unnamed, and either reading empties an honest arrival's float before it has forfeited a bond. Everything else is implementation readings, taken as written unless ruled otherwise.

## The correction — what is income

The rule turns on one classification: an inflow to a name's unlocked balance is **income** (repays the loan first) or a **return** (the name's own money, untouched). §2.1 lists the income kinds — "a review's fee and uplift, a delivery's price at vesting, a kill fee received, a court fee reimbursed, an emission share" — and names the returns as "a guarantee, bond or escrow returning". The engine credits a name's unlocked balance on these events, and the list should be read against them:

| Inflow (ledger event, the engine's note) | What it is | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| `feepot_out` "review fee" / "nested review fee"; `fee_out` "emission share"; `transfer` from the duty fund "duty uplift" | the review's pay | **income** — as drafted |
| `escrow_out` "delivery price" | the price at vesting | **income** — as drafted (reading 3 on what it is gross of) |
| `escrow_out` "kill fee from escrow"; `transfer` "kill fee from unlocked" | §9.3's kill fee to a cancelled child | **income** — as drafted |
| `forfeit_to` "forfeit: …" — a child's guarantee to the requester of a nested case | compensation to a parent at a lapse or a rejection stood | **income** — not named in the rc; the same kind as the kill fee (not the parent's own money) |
| `deposit_out` "reimburse …" / "deferred reimbursement"; `transfer` from Ω "Ω advance …" / "Ω claim serviced" | **the reviewer's cost reimbursed** (§4.3): the care it paid by `spend`, a burn, replaced from the allowance, an Ω advance or the deferred claim | **a return** — not named in the rc, and the one that matters |
| `forfeit_to` "court fee" from the loser's bond to the challenger | the challenger's own $D$, paid into Ω at the appeal, coming back (the engine marks it `court_fee_reimbursed`) | **a return** — the rc names it income |
| `release` of a guarantee, bond, posting lock or nested lock; `escrow_out` "escrow returned"; the pots' "unused allowance / fee pot returned" at a node's closure | the name's own money | **a return** — as drafted |

The reviewer's cost is two or three real a review against a fee of about one. A careful arrival pays its care by `spend` and is made whole by the reimbursement; if the reimbursement is read as income, every review takes two to three times its fee out of the float, and the float is gone in about twenty reviews — before a bond is forfeited, long before the loan is repaid, and the probationer is unseated for want of a bond. The §6 example ("earns about $+1.2$ a review; each fee goes home to the bank") is right only under the table above. The court fee the same way, smaller: a name that appeals a false rejection and wins would pay $D$ to the bank for winning. Two sentences in §2.1 and §7 fix it: *returns* are releases **and reimbursements** (the reviewer's cost, from the allowance, Ω's advance or the deferred claim) **and a court fee coming back to the challenger who paid it**; the forfeited guarantee to a parent is income. The test the rc asks for ("the kinds it covers are listed in the note and checked by the test") should check this table, event by event.

## Corrections of fact

1. **"The entry-cell goldens move by the constant and are re-pinned once" (H4, §7).** No golden scenario arrives. `tests/test_perf_equivalence.py`'s scenario has no `arrive`; the only test file that arrives is `test_v23`. Every golden is unchanged under this revision, to the decimal, and nothing is re-pinned. The entry cell's figures are results (run 16, run 24's baseline), and those move by the constant as H2 says.
2. **"The float is booked through `BANK` as before" (§7).** The bank is counters and kinds, not a wallet (2.1.0 as implemented: `bank_minted`, `bank_destroyed` by kind). The float is minted straight into the name's wallet as `arrival`; `arrival` is a source in I1 already (`Ledger.MINTS`). What is new is the sink — `arrival_repayment` joins the burns — and the loan is a counter on the name. Wording only.
3. **"The lifetime measure … is now net by construction" (§7).** Not as the drivers stand. The exit rule and the learner's boundary step read the wallet *before* `depart` and record that as what the name took out (`depart_wealth_real`); and the live name's wealth is read gross. Under the loan, a stamper's name reads 67 carried out when it carried out nothing, and the live name's float counts as wealth: Hypothesis 1 would reproduce the grant's numbers from the instrument. The measure must read **the wallet out** — what `depart` burned as `departure`, returned by `depart` (engine and view) and carried in the history's entry beside the recovery and the default — and the live name's wealth **net of its loan outstanding**, $W_U-\min(W_U,L)$. One change in three places in the driver; "net by construction" is then true.

## Readings

1. **The hook is the ledger hook, and the record shows both legs.** Income is credited to the name as today; in `_on_ledger_event` (where `_bank_on_inflow` runs the order for Ω) an inflow to a name with a loan outstanding is followed by an `arrival_repayment` burn from the name of $\min(\text{amount}, L)$, reentrancy-guarded as Ω's is. The event log then carries the fee *and* the repayment, so I1 reconstructs, the drivers' income tallies by event still see the fee, and "repaid from income first" is one line in the record per inflow. The kinds are selected by event kind and note, per the table.
2. **The exit rule reads earnings gross of the repayment.** The driver's exit rule reads a member's earnings as its wallet's change over the period, in real terms, against the reservation. A name still repaying reads its earnings as nothing, though it earned them, and takes a strike toward `--exit-patience`. The arrivals in probation are skipped already; the names admitted with a loan still outstanding are not. The driver adds the period's repayments back, so the exit decision is the one 2.6.1 makes on the same earnings — otherwise H2's departures are confounded for those names. The entry decision reads nothing of the float (expected earnings against the reservation) and does not move, as the rc says.
3. **The delivery price is gross of the effort cost.** A worker burns $c_D$ by `spend` while it works and is paid the price at vesting; the rc takes the whole price for the loan. An admitted name with a loan outstanding therefore sees its wallet fall by $c_D$ per job until the loan is repaid, and its guarantee $G\approx 0.5\,p_T$ must still lock from it. At the §6 figures (a few credits outstanding at admission) nothing follows; a name admitted early with most of its loan outstanding could be unfundable for an award for a job or two. **Recommend as drafted** — the price whole, the exposure small — with the reading in §8: awards for which an arrival-born member was not fundable while its loan stood. The alternative, income as the price net of the case's recorded execution cost (`c.actor_cost`, which the engine keeps for §4.6), is available if the reading is not small.
4. **The honest probationer's liquidity is tighter than under the grant, by construction.** Under the grant the fees replenished the wallet during probation; under the loan the wallet is the float less forfeits until the loan is repaid. The rc's reading ("a bond or two") is mine too; H2's "probationers never refused a seat for want of a bond" is the measurement, and beside it the count of arrival-born names whose unlocked balance fell below $G^V$ before admission.
5. **I41 and the bank's line.** Lent to names $=$ repaid from income $+$ recovered at departure $+$ outstanding $+$ defaulted, every tick; outstanding $=\sum$ live arrival-born names' loans; a departed name's loan is zero and its default a counter. I28's identity keeps `loan` alone (the loans to Ω); the names' line stands beside it in `view.bank()` and the period record: lent (count, amount), repaid, recovered, defaulted, outstanding (stock, peak). `arrival_repayment` in `bank_destroyed`.
6. **The learner needs no new field.** Its observation carries its own wealth and unlocked balance, which read flat while it repays — the honest signal. A new field (its loan outstanding) would be a new learner version and would owe a calibration; not for this line. Run 23's policy replays from `wm-online-10.pt` (the final evaluation's model) through `run_q --model` on the 45 fields unchanged; the Q1 line under 2.7.0 then trains as ruled after run 23.
7. **The genesis gift is the engine's already.** Genesis balances are transfers from `SPONSOR` (or `issuance` for a crowd's requester), never `arrival`; no code, and I41 scopes to names with `arrived_at`.
8. **Departure's admissibility is untouched, and the four refusals keep the world unchanged.** The recovery and the default happen after the last check, as the burn does today; the whole-world snapshot for the refused cases is the same test as 2.3.0's. `EXPECTED_FAIL` 32 on my reading: no reviewer test arrives or departs (confirmed: only `test_v23` calls `arrive`). `test_v23`'s own departure figures pin the grant; where they do they pin `arrival_loan=False`, as `test_v101` pins `supply=False`.

## For the record

One precision: under the loan a name carries out $\max(0,\ \text{fees}-\text{forfeits})$ — the default absorbs a negative net — so the lifetime equals the own earnings name by name only where the net is positive; the §6 cycler (fees 6, forfeits 16, carries out nothing, default 10) is exactly that, and the harvester's positive net per name is why its figure is $-1{,}090$ and not lower.

- §8's order as drafted; the red team's "a member that tries to move its float to an accomplice through a nested request" is answered in advance by the posting lock (a parent locks the all-in from its unlocked balance, so the float can fund a child's pots, and the child's price is the child's income — repaying the child's loan first if it has one; what the parent carries out is still $W_U-\min(W_U,L)$).
