# Review of 2.2.0-rc.1 — design revision 10, "the rebate from income"

**Date:** October 19, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_2.2.0-rc.1.md` · **Against:** the 2.1.0 text in force, the engine at `f081e15f`, run 14 · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict. Implementable, with one correction and five readings.** The revision is the smallest of the three candidates run 14's notes set out — the claim stays the measure, the payment is the period's fee income pro rata, the rest is forgone and read — and the §6 arithmetic checks against run 14's figures (a quiet 60/40 period's fees about 465 against claims of about 9; a flooded period's claims about 5,400 against a pool near 900, a forgone share of 0.84; the crowd's two periods near 0.83). The two rulings carried are the ones the notes asked for. One sentence in §5 says more than §2.2 does and would lock the state's working capital if implemented as written; that is the correction. The rest are readings, stated so the text can say otherwise before the engine fixes them.

## The correction — the pool takes fee income, not every inflow

§2.2 says the pool is **Ω's fee income** — the base fee and the troops' review fees. I32 says **"every inflow to Ω after the debt accrues to the current period's pool."** Those differ, and the difference is the state's working capital. An inflow to Ω under 2.1.0 is one of two things: *income* — the base fee at an award or a summons, a troop reviewer's fee at settlement, a court fee a troop wins — or *capital coming back* — a troop delivery's price and released guarantee at vesting, a troop seat's reimbursed cost and released bond at settlement, which under §10.4 repay the summons's own loan first and then land in Ω's balance. If every inflow were pooled, the returns of capital would sit untouched until the close, Ω's balance for summonses would be a period stale, and every summons would be the bank's loan from the second period on — the honest cells of run 14, where Ω funded every summons itself, would turn into cells of loans and the loans line would stop reading the stress. I will implement §2.2: **the pool is the fee income after the debt** — base fees, the troops' review fees, and court fees a troop wins (the one fee the text does not name; the troop's bond is the state's and a court fee it wins is income of the same kind) — and the returns of capital go where 2.1.0 puts them. I32 should say "every fee inflow"; if the reviewer means the delivery's surplus $b_T(\bar B+G)$ too, say so, and note that it is the expected-cost margin that nets zero over successes and failures, so pooling it would pay the customers out of the state's failures' cover.

## Readings

1. **The pool is a lock on Ω, not a wallet.** Fees land in Ω's wallet by transfer; "untouched until the close" is then a lock record on Ω (`pool:<period>`) raised as each fee arrives after the debt, so Ω's *unlocked* balance excludes it, the summons and `_fund_can_carry` read the unlocked balance alone, I15 reconciles the record, and I30's "Ω's unlocked balance is zero while debt > 0" holds without a special case. At the close the lock is released, the claims are paid from it, the remainder stays unlocked as Ω's. A second wallet would do the same job with more bookkeeping (I1, I23, the fund flow) and no gain.
2. **Hypothesis 2 is too strong by one period's fees.** In run 14 Ω kept 1,800–1,900 at its lowest from the second period on and funded every summons; under the pool a period's fees — 465 at 60/40 — are locked until the close, and in the first period Ω has only its float of 300. The quiet cells will show loans in the first period or two and a lower retained balance by about one period's fees; everything else should be identical. I will read it as that, not as "every other reading identical", and the hypothesis should say so.
3. **The claim's indexing — a conformance gap found on the way, fixed with this revision.** §4.5 lists $r_s$ among the indexed amounts and §2.1 here says "indexed"; the engine indexes the default ($r_s=0$ means $\omega_0 B$, which scales with the price) but not a stated $r_s$ — the tests' $r_s=1$ is 1 at every index. No run was affected (every driver uses the default). It is a conformance correction under §19 — the engine misapplies a rule in force — and I will fix it with a regression test in `test_v22` and no change to the rules, noted here so the reviewer knows the figure moved for that reason.
4. **The close is inside the tick.** The boundary runs at step 5 before the summons pass; a claim recorded at a stop earlier in the same tick (a vesting or a final at steps 2–3) belongs to the closing period and is settled at once; one recorded at a stop later in the tick belongs to the next. That is "stopped in the period" read at the tick's granularity, as 2.0.0's "at once" was.
5. **Two small things.** A rebate paid at the close reaches a request that may have vested earlier in the period; I10 admits it by its note, as it admitted the serviced liability — a new exempt note, `rebate (settled at the period's close)`. And `test_v21`'s rebate tests pin the 2.1.0 liability stock and will pin `rebate_pool=False`, as `test_v20` pins `bank=False`; no reviewer test touches the rebate's timing, so the expected failures stay at 28.
