# Run 7 — design revision 5 (1.5.0, the reserve is sized to the need) implemented, and its §8 runs

**Date:** October 17, 2026 · **Rules in force:** 1.5.0 (`spec/agentic_economics_loop_spec_v1_5_0.md`; rc.2 verbatim and the two implementation reviews in `spec/design_revisions/`; 1.4.0 in history) · **Suite:** 225 passing, 26 expected failures, in all three modes; `test_v15` (7) carries the rule · **Data:** `agentic_sim/results/studies/1.5.0-reserve/` (also `1.5.0-reserve.zip`): `tipping/` (160, 1.5.0 and the 1.4.0 control on this engine), `windows/` (60), `adaptive/` (40), `drift/` (80), `redteam/` (140), `ramp/` (2, the §6 pair logged every tick) — 482 runs, 20 seeds per cell, 20 actors, every final audit passed, engine 01b75e2. Appeals truthful, no disputes. The 1.4.0 control reproduces run 6 to the unit. The write-up is `HANDOFF.md`, "Design revision 5" and "Run 7".

## Chosen at implementation (Appendix J)

- The refused demand of a pass is accumulated inside the summons pass and read with the capital held at its end, so D_s is one number per seating; the panel loop adds the seats it did not attempt only when the refusal was for funds.
- The period's peak is reset at the period's end, before the same tick's seating, to the capital still held; the seating then takes the maximum with its D_s — "starting at the standing demand".
- The uplift check compares, as under 1.4.0, the fund's *unlocked* balance against a reserve that counts capital held.
- `Params.reserve_demand=False` is the 1.4.0 reading (the larger of this and the previous period's peak capital carried), the declared control `--rules 1.4.0`.

## The result in one line

The rule does what it says — the reserve tracks the demand, the tests prove it — and the fund still does not carry the need, because the need as the rule measures it is a never-expiring queue, and the capacity that binds is the reviewing seat, not the treasury.

Three things the text did not see:

1. **The demand stock counts the queue.** Requests do not expire; at 50–60% about 300 cases are open and each waiting request is refused at every seating, so D_s is the queue's price and R_T is unreachable. The uplift stops as designed, the fund carries 59–94 deliveries at once (1,200–1,900 of capital), and the backlog still grows. §6 assumed the flow; the engine met the stock.
2. **The reviewing seat is the cap.** On duty 4.5–5, audits assigned 1,800–2,150, reviews that found no reviewer 7,300–11,400 case-ticks a run under both rules at every split. A troop's delivery waits for its initial review and its pinned audit; its capital stays held; cash tied in deliveries waiting for review is cash the fund cannot put into a review seat (290–530 seat refusals) or the next delivery. Vesting is pinned at 2.2–2.4 a tick by reviewing capacity whoever delivers.
3. **The boundary reset saw-tooths.** When the in-flight cohort has just vested, the standing demand at the boundary seating is near zero and the accumulated balance pays out in one batch (3,135 and 3,146 in two periods of the ramp while the fund was refusing nine summonses a tick) — the sawtooth rc.2 rejected for the shortfall-zero rule, arriving by the reset. The two-period reading damped it.

At a fixed split the lagging auditor is a stamper the plant record has not yet caught, not a careful actor turned. The lag is the record's, not the drift's. A member in standing misses 2–3 a run, a troop under 1, the ruling 10–15%.

**Red team.** Decliners (careful actors that offer for nothing, 60/40): a quarter of them end 901 poorer than the careful who offer, half of them 2,040 poorer; with half declining troop deliveries collapse to 17, vesting falls a quarter and the stampers get richer — fewer awards, less tax, a fund that cannot carry. The prediction holds and the strategy also starves the state. A requester flood (six tasks a tick): vested +21%, refused 83,924, backlog 2,812, R_T 18,088, uplift *lower* than at three; not served and not an exploit — the seat caps it and the reserve becomes the queue's price. The reformer cycles 28–56 times and ends 2,479–3,958 poorer; camouflage costs its user.

## What I would put to the next decision, in order

1. **The queue.** Demand on a never-expiring stock is unbounded and a reserve sized to it is "retain everything until a boundary". Rules that would bound it: a request unawarded for some T withdraws (the human's `withdraw` exists; §6.1 has no such deadline; the drivers never use it); or D_s counts the refused *flow* — cases first refused in this period — not the stock; or the refused component is capped at what the period's inflow could carry.
2. **The reviewing seat.** X_t ≈ 5 on duty, the duty cap ⌈E_t/2⌉, and an audit of every pinned and every troop approval set the market's size at every split under every rule since 1.3.0. The 1.4.0 text left the cap's loosening open; the evidence now says the cap, not the fund, is the limit, and the reserve rule is downstream of it.
3. **The boundary reset.** Once 1 is settled, restart at max(standing demand, a fraction of the last peak) or a decayed peak — the stock-tracking reserve rc.2 recorded and rejected — removes the sawtooth. The smaller fix; not first.
4. **Q14.** The probation leak is the plant record's detection lag on the *auditor*. Whether a pinned approval's auditor should itself have a determined record is now the question the text deferred, with the figure that decides it.

I would not call 1.5.0 wrong: it is implemented as written, it measures what it says, and its readings are what found the three things above. It did not do what §3 hoped because the hope was about the flow and the rule met the stock.
