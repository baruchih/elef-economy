**One driver change between the first launch and this one, recorded.** The first wave's crowd never left: a request whose ceiling the market overtakes *after* posting is below market but carries no flag (the flag is set at posting, §2.8), and the driver's raise policy read the flag — so those requests waited forever (no troop comes for β < B̄, the members bid above it), the crowd's wallets swelled with issuance, and M ran to 219,000. The policy now reads the live condition β < B̄, as a requester watching the price would; the engine's flag is unchanged and remains the reading. Every sweep was rerun; the first wave was discarded.

## In one paragraph

The credit can be worth more or less than it was, and in these runs it was worth less: at every split the price level rose from P_0 = 20 to 31–37 with bidding members and to the ceiling cap of 40 under any pressure, and a 2,000-credit shock lifted it another 15% inside a window and the stampers' real wealth fell 13% and did not come back. **The price is set by the free members' margins, not by cost:** at 60/40 six of eight members are on duty, the two who deliver are awarded every tick they are free, so their margins ratchet (+0.3 an award, −0.25 per three idle ticks) to about 3 and their bids to 29, and the state follows at B̄ — the worked example's "near cost, P_t ≈ 9" assumed members plentiful, and in the adaptive run, once all twenty are honest, the price does fall, to 23. **The state at cost is below cost:** a troop delivery that fails loses B̄ + G and nothing offsets it, so from the first period in which the state's share rises Ω's unlocked balance is zero and the deficit grows at 10–20 a tick for the rest of every run — Hypothesis 7 fails at every split; the control at the 1.x margin (half the price) has a deficit of zero on the same paths. **The capacity case is where the loop breaks loudest:** under N_T = 40 the flood fills the field, a third of requests are on time, rebates run at 6,300 a window, the deficit reaches 59,700, and the requesters — paid rebates, issued 10 a tick, unable to spend — hoard: M 99,000. **Honesty does not move:** false approvals 10.7 / 10.4 / 12.7 / 14.7 at 50–80% against run 10's 11 / 14 / 15 / 18; the lagging auditor zero at every fixed split; the stampers' real wealth a tenth of the honest's everywhere. **Two things to decide** (the last section): the state's cost rule (at cost loses the failure rate) and the ceiling cap (β_max = 40 is the price level under pressure).

**Hypothesis 1 — price decides, and finds itself: half.** Price decides: the honest spread of ρ is below λ·ΔB/β and λ = 0.5 changes nothing (knobs). The price does not sit near cost: it sits where the free members' margins take it. At 60/40 by window: P_t 17.6 in the first hundred ticks (margins from 0.3), 32.9 in the second and 33–34 from there; the margin mean 3.0 ± 0.1 for eight hundred ticks. The mechanism is the award frequency against the idle time, as Appendix M's reading anticipated: two to three free members, two awards a tick, every free tick an award, so +0.3 outruns the decay and the bid climbs until the ceilings (40, or a third of a drained wallet) stop it. The state then prices at B̄ and never undercuts, so there is no competitive anchor below the members' margin except the ceiling. P_t rises with the stamper share (31.6 → 37.4) because fewer members are freer; at 80/20 the state delivers 57% at the members' price. **The ceiling control is cheaper than the market** — P_t 21–24 — because every bid is the requester's ceiling and the requester's ceiling is a third of its wallet, which the at-ceiling bids drain to 53–60: the price follows the customers' poverty down. Under bidding the members' 28 is below the ceilings and the customers keep 70–90; under the ceiling they keep nothing and 1,400–1,900 requests a run are raised to the market price after posting. The at-the-ceiling members are *richer in real terms* (2,494–4,012 against 1,840–2,263): P_t is lower, their nominal income is the same.

**Hypothesis 4 — honesty is price-neutral: holds.** False approvals at or below run 10 at every split, the lagging-auditor kind zero everywhere (the panel rule of 1.6.0 holds under prices), suspensions 72–113, readmissions 0, the stampers' real wealth 220–410 against the honest's 1,840–4,012 — the gap keeps its sign and its order at every split and under both bid policies. The care cost named in §4.6 (nominal against an indexed fee) shows as `effort_unpaid` 18–23 a run under bidding and 61–67 at the ceiling: a few members, poor in the first period, cannot pay their effort of 8 in full.

**Hypothesis 6 — the state does not profit: holds, and inverts.** Troop awards counted in B̄ (red team) move P_t by −0.9: nothing. At cost the state does not profit; it loses — §3 below.

**What binds on the demand side.** `posts_k_open` 1,000–2,500 and `posts_unaffordable` 2,500–3,200 a run: with K_open = 5 and a request's life of 15–20 ticks the six requesters post 1.8–1.9 a tick, and from the second period on their wallets sit at 70–90 (unlocked 35) — the posting lock of a request at β = 40 is about 77 at P_t = 33, so a requester can hold one or two. The queue sits at 24–28, the K_open ceiling of 30 nearly reached. Demand is lock-bound, not price-bound; issuance at 15 (knobs) doubles the state's deliveries and puts P_t at the cap.

## 3. The state at cost is below cost (Hypotheses 3, 6, 7; Q16)

In every 2.0.0 cell Ω's unlocked balance reaches zero between ticks 350 and 450 and stays there; the deficit then grows at 10–20 a tick for the rest of the run. By window at 60/40 (bidding): Ω 1,611 at tick 310, 693 at 410, 91 at 510, 0 from 560; created 283 / 3,056 / 5,479 / 6,237 / 6,292 a window against retired 255 / 2,371 / 4,604 / 5,213 / 5,240 — a net of about 1,000 a window. The cause is §7.7 as written: the state delivers at c^D_T = B̄, burns B̄ at summons, recovers B̄ at vesting — and a delivery that ends in a final N (the base fail rate, 20%) recovers nothing and forfeits G: a loss of about 1.5 B̄ on a fifth of the state's deliveries, 0.3 B̄ per delivery, 10 a delivery at P_t 33 — against a base fee of ω₀B ≈ 1.5 an award to Ω. At 90 troop deliveries a window that is about 900 lost and 150 earned. **The control proves it:** `c-d-t-mode half` (the 1.x margin, c^D_T = 0.5 P_t) runs the identical path — same awards, same prices, same honesty to the unit — with the deficit at zero throughout. Hypothesis 7 ("the deficit never binds and always clears") fails at every split; Hypothesis 6 holds in its letter (the state does not profit) and inverts in its spirit (the state loses, and the loss is created money). The deficit's size scales with the state's volume: 3,700 at 50/50, 10,100 at 80/20, 13,700–15,900 under drift, 17,300 at issuance 15, 30,000–60,000 in the riots. It is retired only in the adaptive run, where the state's deliveries stop (the last at tick 962) and the fee income clears the 30 created in the first period.

## 8. For the decision

1. **The state's cost rule.** §7.7's "at cost" is c^D_T = B̄ on every delivery while a fifth of them fail and forfeit; the state's expected cost per delivery is B̄(1 + b) + bG and its expected income B̄, so it loses 0.3 B̄ a delivery and the deficit grows without bound wherever the state delivers — every split, every riot, every drift cell; zero only when the state stops (adaptive). The control at the 1.x margin is solvent on the identical path. The text says a failed delivery is "a real cost, read in Ω's income by source"; Hypothesis 7 says the deficit always clears; the two cannot both stand. Candidates, in order of size: the expected cost — c^D_T = B̄(1 − b) with b the trailing fail rate of the state's deliveries, which is "at cost" in expectation; the base fee ω₀ raised to carry it (0.3 B̄ a delivery against 0.05 B an award is a factor of six — not a fee); or the margin kept as a reading of what the state must charge to be neutral. A rule decision, not a default.
2. **The ceiling cap is the price level.** Under any pressure — the stamper share above 60%, a shock, a crowd, issuance 15 — P_t goes to β_max = 40 and stays; the cap is the drivers' declared policy, so the finding is that the market's price has no anchor below the customers' maximum except the free members' margin dynamics, and the state at B̄ never undercuts. Whether that is "a market" or "two members and a cap" is the reviewer's reading; the adaptive run shows the price falling to 23 with twenty members free, which is the direction the text expects. If a cell is wanted where members are plentiful from the start, it is 20 members all honest at 60 a tick of issuance (the adaptive tail), or K_open and the lock relaxed so demand is price-bound rather than lock-bound.
3. **The rebate under a capacity refusal.** A request refused for capacity is owed a rebate the state creates; in the flood that is 6,300 a window of creation paid to requesters who cannot spend it, and 34,000 a run. The text's I28 and the clock are each doing what they say; together, under N_T, they print. Whether the rebate should run while the refusal is for capacity (the state's own limit) rather than for the market's slowness is a question the 1.6.0 clock did not have to answer.
4. **The three reviewer tests the index moves** (HANDOFF, Appendix M): P_t on B̄'s window moves within a period; at the period boundary the index would be constant within a period and those three would pass. One line; the text's call.
5. **Delegation (2.1.0).** The requesters' wallets end at 70–90 and the members' at 1,900–4,800 real: the money is where the text said it would go. Members have nothing to buy.

Everything here is an evaluation under the fixed rules (§19). No rule, default or permitted operation was moved for a finding; the one driver change (the raise policy's condition) is a strategy's reading of its own declaration and is recorded above.
