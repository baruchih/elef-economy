# Review of 1.2.0-rc.1 — design revision 2, "the loop's own clock"

**Date:** October 17, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_1.2.0-rc.1.md` (prepared October 17, 2026) · **Against:** the 1.1.0 text in force (`spec/agentic_economics_loop_spec_v1_1_0.md`), the engine at `cb60f9b`, and runs 2–3 (`agentic_sim/results/studies/1.1.0-stamping/`, `1.1.0-combined/`) · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict.** The mechanism — planted work, a plant record, an effort dial — is sound and correctly classified under §19 as a design revision motivated by strategy findings. rc.1 is not implementable as written: four points contradict the 1.1.0 text or the engine (items 1–4), one figure is wrong (5), and three defaults need a word (6–8). An rc.2 that settles 1–4 can go straight to implementation.

## What checks out

- Every run figure cited in §1 and §3 matches run 3: +200 and −489 per actor at 50/50, X = 8; break-even dispute rate ≈ 0.07; careful review pays above ≈ 92% accuracy (ε 0.08); 23 of 135 disputes won at 10/90; 159 awards to 66; 1,295 runs; a ≈ 0.98; effective catch rate ≈ 3%.
- The worked example's arithmetic holds: ρ^P_C = (1+1)/(1+3+2) = 0.33; ρ^P_D = 0.83; F̂ = 15/37 = 0.41; ratio 4.1 clamped to ×2.
- The plant table follows 1.1.0: a reviewer with no challenger forfeits its bond whole and it is burned (§7.2); a wrong reviewer gets its costs back and nothing else (§7.3); a guarantee forfeited on N goes to the requester (§5.3), so with Ω as both plant requester and plant actor a bad plant to a stamper costs Ω nothing net, as §6 says.
- §7.5 is adopted as the engine's driver reads it (volunteers; round-robin in id order from the last assigned; wait). "Panel chosen by ρ" goes to Appendix B with the run-3 reason.
- p_start = 1.0 is a default move, not a rule change: the §8.3 rule already halves the odds after five consecutive verdicts that stood, so "audited every time until five in a row have stood" is that rule started at 1.0.
- "The task world already knows truth" is right: the stamping driver draws each task's fulfillment (`--fail-rate`), so a plant supplier is a small addition to it.

## Must fix before rc.2

1. **Indistinguishability (§2.1) contradicts §14.** The reviewer's view carries the submission's **actor** — §14's table ("the submission: result, evidence, actor, time of submission, whether resubmitted") and the engine's `ReviewView.actor`. A plant actor "that belongs to the orchestrator" is therefore named in every plant's view and is known to every reviewer after the first revealed plant. Two ways out, and the text must choose: (a) each plant carries a fresh pseudonymous actor — then the actor field tells a reviewer nothing on plants, but a never-seen actor id is itself a weak signal in a small population; or (b) §14 drops the actor from the view — an information-contract change, to be labelled as part of this revision. Related: I20 as written ("no reviewer view of a plant differs in any field from the view of an ordinary case in the same state") is not a predicate over an explicit scope and cannot be checked per tick. Split it: the invariant is *no challenge is ever opened on a plant and a plant's outcome is set only by its known truth*; the view property is a test — the view is a function of case fields that exclude the plant marker.

2. **I9 and I21 conflict when eligible actors are fewer than X_t.** I9: the reserve holds X. I21: every actor on duty is eligible by plant record. Hypothesis 2 suspends 18 stampers at 10/90 within about 250 ticks (≈ 200 reviews per actor per 1,000 ticks in run 3, × π β_P = 0.075 plant failures per review) and leaves two careful actors for a reserve of four or more. Either the reserve may run short — counted, like the solvency shortfall of §12, and "otherwise wait" applies — or suspension is bounded so that X eligible actors remain, which readmits stampers. The text must say which; §8 runs 2 and 4 depend on it. Recommendation: let it run short and count it. A market with two careful reviewers in twenty lacks reviewers, and the record should say so rather than seat the negligent to keep a number.

3. **ρ^P_min = 0.5 does not match F* = 0.10.** ρ^P mixes good and bad plants. A reviewer that approves half the bad plants and all the good ones has s = 0.625 n, o = 0.375 n, ρ^P → 0.625: eligible forever, while contributing a bad-plant approval rate of 0.5 — five times the target. The dial saturates at m_max, the alarm fires, and "re-seat from the highest ρ^P" has nothing better to seat if the reserve is made of such actors. Define eligibility on the quantity the clock measures — an actor's own bad-plant approval rate over its trailing plants, against F* or a multiple of it — or raise ρ^P_min to about 0.85. The worked example's C (0.33) is suspended under either.

4. **Newcomer plants at twice the rate (§2.4) cannot coexist with round-robin (§2.5).** Under §7.5 the orchestrator does not choose the reviewer, so it cannot land a plant on a newcomer except by timing the plant's creation to the newcomer's turn — and a reviewer that receives plants out of proportion when its turn comes has a signal §2.1 says does not exist. Either drop the doubling (p_start = 1.0 already closes the cheap entry route; the plant record forms in ≈ 20 reviews at π = 0.1) or admit a targeted assignment for newcomers and state it as an exception to §7.5.

## Figures and defaults

5. **§1.4 is wrong.** The solvency measurement (run 3, G^V = 20) is 291 actor-ticks over 4 slots × 1,000 ticks: **7% of slot-ticks**, or 0.29 of one slot on average — not "29% of duty slots". The conclusion (the reserve starves) stands on the other figures: 4,500–4,700 review-ticks waiting, awards unfilled in 590–640 ticks, vesting −4%.

6. **Ω's working capital is not stated.** A plant locks award 20, allowance 10, fee pot 5 and the plant actor's guarantee ⌈0.5 B⌉ = 10 from Ω until the tree closes: 45 per plant outstanding. At π_0 = 0.1 of 3 tasks per tick and 15–20 ticks to closure that is 200–270 credits outstanding at m = 1 and four times that at m_max. Appendix A gives Ω a cold-start float of 20; ω brings 3 per tick at B = 20. Hypothesis 3 counts cost (≈ 2 per tick), not capital. State the rule: the required plant count is capped by what Ω can fund from its unlocked balance, and the shortfall is recorded (the "a run that supplies fewer declares it" clause covers the driver, not the rule) — or raise Ω's float and say by how much.

7. **p_start = 1.0 exceeds the §8.3 cap.** The adaptive rule doubles on overturn with cap 0.5. Say the cap becomes 1.0 (then a plant failure sends an established reviewer back to full audit) or that the start may sit above the cap and only the doubling is capped.

8. **Hypothesis 1 names the wrong channel.** At ≈ 200 reviews per actor per 1,000 ticks, 0.6 per review is ≈ 120 per actor at m = 1 — the order of the 50/50 gap (+200 at X = 8, appeals only) but far from the 10/90 gaps (+1,336 to +1,794), which are selection effects (66 awards to 159). The flip at 10/90 rests on plant failures counting in ρ (≈ 7.5 extra overturns per 100 outcomes for a stamper at π = 0.1 against ≈ 0.5 for a careful reviewer, so ρ ≈ 0.90 against ≈ 0.95, reversing run 3's 0.973 against 0.953) and on suspension — not on the per-review money. Say so; it also tells the §8 runs what to measure. And explain f̄ = 4 in §3: f is 2; if f̄ = f + ē includes the emission share, give the figure and its decay.

## Smaller

- §2.3 item 3: the example (X from 4 to 6) cannot be derived without d̄; at m = 1 the formula gives ⌈4.1 d̄⌉, which is 5 for d̄ = 1. The default sizing should reproduce X = 4 at rest.
- "Nature of this revision" omits two rule changes the text makes: X_t endogenous (§12) and ω_t scaled by m_t (§4.1). List them; §4 "what does not change" names "the reserve" among the unchanged in effect, which it is not.
- §4.2: emission is distributed by weight among reviews that settle in the period, so plants dilute every real review's emission share by about π rather than minting extra. Neutral in aggregate; worth one sentence.
- Provenance [S2]: the engines of runs 2–3 are `a1e726a` (1.1.0) to `f3aede2`; `7020c80` is the appeals-run driver commit.
- The period "50 settled plants, or 200 ticks" is consistent at 0.3 plants per tick (≈ 60 per 200 ticks); plant records form at ≈ 20 plant outcomes per actor per 1,000 ticks, so n_min = 4 is reached in ≈ 200 ticks and 1,000-tick runs are long enough for §8.

## After rc.2

Items 1–4 change what the engine must do, so implementation waits for them. Once settled, the work is: `create_plant(truth)` as an orchestrator operation with the marker sealed in the History entry; adjudication by truth at the initial verdict; refusal of every challenge operation on a plant; the plant record, ρ^P, the eligibility filter in rotation and assignment, suspension with its clock; the dial's period accounting, clamps, π_t, audit scaling, X_t sizing and ω_t; I20/I21 per tick, I1/I13/I17 amended; `test_v12.py` with the §6 figures, the suspension, the clamp bounds, the alarm and re-seating, and the view test; the stamping driver's plant supplier, camouflage disposition and new columns; goldens re-pinned once at the implementing commit; then the §8 runs, starting with the §6 reproduction.
