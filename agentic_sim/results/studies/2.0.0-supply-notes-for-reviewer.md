# Run 13 — the price question under 2.0.0 (evaluations under the rules in force), notes for the design reviewer

**Date:** October 19, 2026 · **Engine:** `9628b941` (2.0.0), unchanged; the driver at `e05cf3fb` (the market-aware ceiling policy and K_open 0 as no limit — the one driver change, below) · **Study:** `results/studies/2.0.0-supply/` — `supply/` (60: 20 / 30 / 40 actors, all honest), `strategy/` (80: the same three cells and 60/40 with the margin falling 0.25 per idle tick instead of per three), `demand/` (200: the market-aware ceiling β = 1.2 B̄ × K_open 5 / none × β_max 40 / 60, at 20 honest and at 60/40; and K_open none alone under the wallet rule), `cap/` (60: β_max 30 / 40 / 60 at 60/40). 400 runs, 20 seeds a cell, 1,000 ticks, six requesters issued 10 a tick each — 60 a tick — every per-tick check and the final full audit passed. Read from the second period on; wealth in real terms. The summariser is `run13_cells.py` (scratchpad); the figures are means over seeds. The run manifests record the engine as `cdc0ccfa-dirty` — the tree with the driver change, committed unchanged as `e05cf3fb` after the sweeps had started; a rerun of `supply-actors20-seed1` at `e05cf3fb` reproduces the fingerprint to the hash.

**The one driver change.** `--ceiling-policy market`: a requester's ceiling is min(β_max, 1.2 B̄) instead of min(β_max, a third of its unlocked wallet) — it locks less and posts more, as asked; `--k-open 0` lifts K_open. The wallet rule stays the default and the engine is untouched. Two words of the strategy matter for what follows and are stated here: a member's margin falls while it is *free and unawarded*; a member working off an award, or on duty, keeps its margin — that is what the declared "idle" means.

## In one paragraph

**The oligopoly is the strategy's ratchet, and supply is what triggers it; the state's pricing never lowers the price, and the cap is the price level only when the requesters let it be.** Adding workers takes the price to cost, not to a plateau: 20 honest actors give P_t 22.5, 30 give 11.6, 40 give 10.9 — against a nominal effort cost of 8 and a bid of 8(1 + m). The cliff between 20 and 30 is the ratchet's constant: in every cell where the margin rule runs, the awards a free member wins per tick converge on margin_down/(idle_k · margin_up) = 0.278 — 0.270 at 20 honest, 0.273 at 60/40 at every cap, 0.25–0.27 across run 12's splits — so the price rises until the requesters buy exactly one task per 3.6 ticks per free member, and where the demand model cannot reach that rate at any price (30 and 40 honest: K_open holds posting at 2.8 a tick over 19–28 free members) the margins decay to the floor and the price is cost. The faster decay puts every honest cell at cost (9.2 / 8.8 / 8.7); at 60/40 it slows the climb from two hundred ticks to nine hundred and does not stop it, because the two free members wait under a tick between jobs and do not decay while working. With market-aware requesters the cap is the price level to the unit (39.6 under 40, 59.5 under 60); with wallet-rule requesters the wallet rule binds first at 34 (β_max 40 and 60 give the same 34.1; 30 binds at 28.8). The state at B̄ never undercuts the members, so it sets the price only where the requesters' ceilings sit at B̄ — K_open lifted under the wallet rule — and there it delivers 39–75% of the work at 17–27 and runs the deficit to 6,000–14,000. Demand unbound at 60/40 is the state's share at 37–54% and the capacity case (3,364 requests refused for capacity, on time 0.80–0.82, rebates 930). Honesty is unmoved by any of it.

**Reading.** What run 12 read at 60/40 was not the cap. β_max 60 gives the same 34.1 as β_max 40: the binding ceiling is the wallet rule — β = a third of an unlocked wallet of 34–36 is 12, flagged and raised to B̄, so a request's ceiling is B̄ itself unless the requester has over 100 unlocked, and the price can rise only as fast as the wallets refill at 10 a tick. The cap binds at 30 (28.8, margins held at 2.7, 2.5 requests waiting below market) and is the price level with market-aware requesters at any value (§3). Lowering the cap to 30 lowers the price 16% and raises the state's share and the deficit (the state delivers at the capped B̄ and loses the same fifth); raising it to 60 changes nothing. So the reading of "the cap is the price level under pressure" is: *the requesters' ceiling policy is the price level under pressure* — the cap when they bid above the market, the wallet rule when they bid what they have — and the ratchet climbs to whichever of the two it meets first.

## 5. The classification

1. **Supply** is the trigger and the cure. The price goes to cost once a free member waits longer between jobs than the strategy's decay horizon (3.6 ticks at the default): 30 honest members against 2.8 posts a tick is 11.6, and 40 is 10.9. The transition from 20 to 30 is a cliff, not a plateau. At 60/40 the two free members never wait, at any decay rate tried.
2. **The strategy** has no interior equilibrium: the margin rides until the wait between jobs equals its constant, so in every margin cell the awards a free member wins per tick converge on 0.278 (0.25–0.27), and the price is whatever makes the requesters buy at that rate. A different constant moves the 20-honest cliff (the faster decay puts it at cost) and the honest members' earnings with it; it does not move 60/40.
3. **The state's pricing** at B̄ never undercuts a member and never lowers the price; it sets the price only where the requesters' ceilings sit at B̄ (the wallet rule with K_open lifted), and there it delivers 39–75% at a loss of 6,000–14,000.
4. **The cap** is the price level under pressure when the requesters' ceilings exceed the market (market-aware: 39.6 / 59.5 to the unit); under the wallet rule the wallets bind first at 34, and the cap at 40 was not what run 12 read.

## 6. Readings for the 2.1.0 draft (no rulings asked)

- **The state at cost in expectation** (c^D_T = (1 − b_T) B̄ − b_T G) prices the state *below* B̄. Under 2.0.0 the state never undercuts a member because it delivers at B̄ and the members bid above it only when their margins are high; a state priced below B̄ becomes the lowest offer on every request past w_A. In §3's wallet-rule cells with K_open lifted that is already the market (75% the state's); the draft should say whether a request past w_A goes to the state at its expected cost or at B̄ — the first makes the state the price-setter wherever members are scarce, the second keeps 2.0.0's floor.
- **The demand model is the driver's**, and it set two of run 12's readings: "lock-bound" was K_open 5 and the wallet rule, and "the cap at 40" was the wallet rule. For the §8 of 2.1.0 the declared requester should be named — the market-aware ceiling makes the cap the price level and opens the capacity case at 60/40; the wallet rule makes the state the price-setter when K_open is lifted.
- **P_t at period boundaries** does not touch the ratchet (which reads B̄, the trailing mean, and the ceilings), so none of the four studies would change in kind; the three reviewer tests would pass.
- **The capacity case told** (a refusal recorded and told) would be visible in §3's 60/40 cells with K_open lifted — 3,364 refusals a run — before any crowd arrives.
