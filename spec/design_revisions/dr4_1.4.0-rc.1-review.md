# Review of 1.4.0-rc.1 — design revision 4, "the orchestrator's troops"

**Date:** October 17, 2026 · **Text reviewed:** `spec-1.4.0-design-revision-4.md` (headed 1.4.0-rc.1, "Proposed") · **Against:** the 1.3.1 text in force, the engine at `6009ee0`, run 5 (`agentic_sim/results/studies/1.3.0-membership/`) · **Reviewer:** the implementer.

**Verdict.** The mechanism is the right shape for what run 5 found: the two shortages — an auditor nobody was free to be, a deliverer nobody was free to be — are one shortage, and a participant of last resort that is used last, used once, paid from the tax the dial already levies and counted in public answers both without touching settlement, verdicts or challenges. The audit obligation (§2.2) alone closes most of the probation-safety leak and would stand on its own. rc.1 is not implementable as written: one of its rules contradicts its own invariant (1), the engine's division of labour with the driver is not stated where it matters (2), and three figures and one classification need a word (3–6). The 1.3.1 sentences (Appendix A) are applied to the text in force today, as the revision directs.

## What checks out

- Every run-5 figure in §1 (2,100–2,500; 0 of 20; 3,311 → 1,221; twenty cycles and 1,700–1,970; tick ~800 against 1,468; 7,150 against 6,800; 20–42%; 816–899 of 1,000; 770 against 865; 8,048; 65 of 97 at m_max 2). The run-1 figure is right too: 647 audits assigned of 2,666 rolled, and the drivers have dropped a roll that found no auditor since then (`audit_unassignable_ticks`, `audits_lapsed`).
- §6's arithmetic: an audit seat nets the fund +3 (fee 3 = 1.5 × 2, cost 3 reimbursed up to K = 5); a delivery nets +10 realised, +4 in expectation (0.8 × 10 − 0.2 × 20); surcharge 3 a tick at m = 2; capital outstanding about 48.
- The obligation is the engine's existing shape: a vacated audit already holds a case through `open_items` / `audit_vacated` until a replacement or a waiver; a rolled-but-unseated audit is the same obligation one step earlier.
- The emission and uplift exclusion is a one-line change to the batch's weights; a troop's single-use identity sits outside I14 and the record's `rotation` events.
- The fund as the troop's wallet reuses the 1.3.0 wallet; the advance is Ω's existing unlocked balance; the reserve R_T is a per-period peak, computable from what the dial already tracks.

## Must fix before rc.2

1. **§2.3 contradicts §2.2 and I25.** §2.2: a rolled audit is an obligation and the case does not vest until it completes — "nothing a probationer approved vests unaudited — by a member, by a troop, or not at all". §2.3: an approval of a troop's delivery by a troop reviewer carries audit odds 1, and if no member is free within the pending window "the case vests as the rules provide" and is recorded unverified by the market. I25 states both halves — "a case with a rolled audit outstanding does not vest" and "an approval of a troop's delivery by a troop reviewer carries audit odds 1" — so under I25 that case can never vest unaudited, and §2.3's reading can never occur. Choose. (a) The §2.3 audit is an obligation like any other, seated by a member or, after w_R, by a troop — then "unverified by the market" means *audited by a troop*, a reading the History can carry, and the deadlock §2.3 fears cannot arise because a troop can always take the seat when the fund carries it. (b) The §2.3 audit is the one exception to the obligation — then I25 must say so and §2.2's "or not at all" must except it. I recommend (a): one rule for every rolled audit, and the reading becomes "cases whose audit a troop took", which is the stress gauge §2.5 wants anyway.

2. **Who summons, and who answers.** §2.2 says the summons check "runs after each tick's seating"; §7 says `award` and `assign_*` reach a troop "only through a summons operation". The engine has no roll, no selection and no truth; the driver has all three. The division that fits the baseline: **summons is the engine's** — at step 5, after the seating, it can see that a required seat has waited w_R ticks with no free eligible on-duty reviewer, or that a root request has waited w_A with no free fundable member, and that the fund can carry the cost — so the summons is a Rule the engine applies, recorded in the History; **the troop's verdict and delivery are the driver's**, a declared disposition (truth with probability 1 − ε_T; failure at b), supplied through `complete_review` and `submit` on the troop's seat, exactly as every member's verdict is. Say this in §2.2/§2.3 and §7, because it decides what a run must declare: the engine summons, the driver answers, and a run that answers otherwise declares it under §18.2.

3. **The troop's cost is a resource purchase, not a burn.** §2.1 says the cost is "credits that leave the loop, as burned bonds do"; §7 says it is spent "through the existing resource-spend operation". A bond burn removes credits against nothing and raises v_t; a resource purchase burns credits and pays the real cost from the reserve, value-neutral (§4.4). A troop's cost is a real cost — ELEF's servers — so it is the purchase, and I3's "the only burns are bond forfeiture and resource purchase" holds without amendment. Fix the §2.1 sentence; §7 is right.

4. **A case whose audit nobody can take waits for ever.** Under §2.2 a probationer-approved case whose pinned audit finds no member and whose summons the fund cannot carry holds until one or the other comes. Its actor's capacity is released at the verdict, but its guarantee stays locked and the requester's price and pots stay in escrow. Say that this is accepted — it is the honest outcome, and troop shortfall counts it — or bound it: after the window a second summons attempt each tick until the fund can carry it, which is what the engine would do anyway. One sentence.

5. **Semver.** The revision adds a participant for the first time. "Major = a change of approach" is the one line of the versioning rule it brushes against. I read it as minor: the loop's rules for members are untouched and a troop is a last-resort instrument of the orchestrator, a party that already exists — but the text should say why it is 1.4.0 and not 2.0.0, in one sentence, so the next reader need not re-decide.

6. **Three figures.** (a) w_R's default is "d̄ = 2 ticks"; in every driver so far a review completes the tick after assignment, so d̄ = 1 and w_R = 2 is two review-durations, not one — say "2 ticks" and let the sweep speak. (b) B̄ in the drivers is β = 20 always (every offer at the ceiling), so §2.3's "summoned only when β ≥ B̄" never binds in the runs; name that as a limit of the drivers, not of the rule. (c) §2.3's audit of a troop-reviewed troop delivery is "seated by a member": say that a troop never takes it — the orchestrator does not audit itself — and under fix 1(a) it is then the one audit seat that can wait past its window.

## Smaller

- **The obligation is also a conformance correction of the drivers.** Since run 1 the drivers have dropped a rolled audit that found no auditor; the engine never offered a way to hold the case for one, because the roll was the driver's. §2.2 gives the roll an engine operation, so the driver's drop ends as a conformance matter even where troops are off. Worth saying, because it changes the 1.3.0 and 1.2.0 controls' audit counts in the runs after this revision.
- **Troop reviewers and the panel floor.** §2.2 summons a troop for an appeal or dispute seat too; a panel of one member and one troop, or two troops, decides a challenge. §2.1's "honest by construction" makes that acceptable; the text should say that a split between a member and a troop goes to the orchestrator's ruling as any split does, and that a troop never sits on a plant's panel (plants have none, so this is a no-op, but the reader will ask).
- **R_T and the first stress.** The fund starts a stressed period near its reserve and draws Ω's advance for the first summonses (§6 says so). A_T = 100 is five troop deliveries' capital; at 80/20 with two of three tasks going to troops that is about one tick's demand. Fine as a default to sweep; name it in §8 run 6.
- **Hypothesis 4's "roughly its size".** The gap will shrink: troops deliver the tasks members could not, so members' delivery income is unchanged but total vesting rises and the fund's uplift to members rises with troop earnings. Say "sign unchanged; size changes by the uplift", so the run is not read as a failure.
