## In plain words

1. **Skipping is a trap.** With skippers and careful members in one world, half and half, the careful end ahead in every setup. The skippers keep 40 to 55 in 100 of what the careful earn, and three in four lose their place. What takes the good seats from them is their record, not the seat's price.
2. **A bigger deposit moves a short-sighted agent only part of the way, and the honest pay for it.** With tests at 1 in 5 and six times the deposit, skipping at the member's audit seat fell from about 95 in 100 to 25 / 13. Bad work that got through fell by a half to two thirds and stayed three to four times the calm world's. Careful members lost 18 in 100 of their lifetime income and their customers got 10 in 100 fewer tasks for the same money.
3. **There are three levers and each has a different payer.** A deposit is paid by the honest, in money and in idle time. More frequent tests are paid by the system, and they bite hard: twice the tests made the same deposit bite 2.6 times as hard. A higher fee at the seat costs the honest nothing and the customers very little.
4. **The orchestrator's audit with a light stake did better than the member's audit seat with a heavy one.** A doubled deposit at the first review brought the leak back to the calm world's, at no cost in money to the honest.
5. **Honest reviewers who share mistakes look like a captured panel.** They lose the same share of true complaints. A joint measure tells the two apart.
6. **Six of the reviewer's stated expectations missed in size, and one was refuted.** They are listed in section 6.

## 7. What it means

- **For agents that count their own future, the record does the work.** A skipper ends with half the income and loses its place. This is the first test of a seat worth keeping, and of Baruchi's premium seat: where members differ, the record hands the audit seat to the careful.
- **For agents that weigh one decision at a time, only a stake works, in part, and it is dear.** At an expected loss of about twice what skipping saves, the audit seat was still skipped 13 to 25 times in 100, and the first review 8 to 10 times in 100 at 1.7 to 2.6 times.
- **The order of levers, by what the honest pay:** the fee at the seat first, more frequent tests second, the deposit last.
- **In this instrument the orchestrator's audit with a light stake beat the member's audit seat with a heavy one**: a leak of 9 / 9 against 34 / 23, and no money taken from the honest against 18 in 100 of their lifetime. This is evidence for the recommendation Baruchi did not adopt. It does not reach the staged loop as ruled, where a member's second opinion moves no money and the reviewer is kept by planted tasks, the mechanical audit and the court. It does say what those planted tasks must be: frequent.
- **Two figures in the economy's answer were too mild.** A rate of planted tasks of 1 in 10 came from the world where care is reimbursed; where the reviewer carries its own cost, 1 in 10 closed the seat at no deposit read. And a margin of 2 in the rule for the odds is not enough for short-sighted agents. The answer's fifth edition corrects both.

## 8. Limits

- The joint read is on 20–65 panels a run.
