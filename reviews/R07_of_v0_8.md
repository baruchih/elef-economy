## A. A post-settlement correction can change the objection without reaching appeal reviewers

**Classification:** confirmed correction-logic defect. Relevant to reliability/learning signals; no clawback is requested.

### Rule basis

- v0.5 §8.1 defines two answers in a missing-evidence appeal: the objection disposition and the fulfillment verdict.
- Its appeal-reviewer contradiction rule applies when the objection differs, or when the objection is rejected and fulfillment differs.
- v0.8 §15.2 requires the post-settlement correction to use the same contradiction rule, with no financial movement.

### Reproduction

1. C says `⊥`: the submission cannot be assessed because evidence is missing.
2. A appeals.
3. D and E both answer **upheld, N**.
4. The case and its reviews settle.
5. H corrects the outcome to **rejected, N**: the evidence was present, but the task still did not fulfill the request.

The fulfillment outcome remains N. The objection has changed, which expressly contradicts both appeal reviewers.

### Observed result

| Reviewer | Before correction | After correction | Required distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| C, original `⊥` | Reliability 2/3 | Reliability 1/2, marked contradicted | This part works |
| D, upheld/N | Reliability 2/3 | Still 2/3, unmarked | Its objection was contradicted |
| E, upheld/N | Reliability 2/3 | Still 2/3, unmarked | Its objection was contradicted |

No money moves. All 18 invariant checks pass.

The reverse case also fails: appeal reviewers initially answer **rejected, N**, and H later corrects to **upheld, N**. Once again their verdict is N, but their objection answer conflicts with the accepted correction.

### Cause

`override()` at `sim/engine.py:982–986` passes only `r.verdict` to `_contradicts()`. That helper handles the special meaning of an original `⊥`, but does not receive `r.objection`, so it cannot assess an appeal reviewer's complete answer.

By comparison, the ordinary challenge-settlement branch at `sim/engine.py:819–828` explicitly checks `r.objection` against the settled disposition.

### Minimal repair direction

Use the complete assessment, including the objection for appeal reviewers, when deciding contradiction. Do not infer the objection from F/N, and do not introduce a new financial clawback.

### Controls

Changing both the objection and fulfillment to **rejected, F** works, because the fulfillment mismatch alone triggers the existing code. Repeating **upheld, N** with no contradiction correctly leaves the reviewers unchanged.

Tests: `test_post_settlement_correction_uses_both_appeal_answers` (four parameterized runs).

## B. An accepted correction between case finalization and review payout is lost

**Classification:** confirmed settlement-boundary/reliability defect. The tests do not impose a new fee or bond policy at this boundary.

### Rule basis

- The inherited tick order allows several participant actions at a tick before that tick's review payout batch.
- A final-N case ends the actor's financial obligation immediately; its surviving reviews settle in the tick's batch.
- v0.5/v0.6 §15.2 and v0.8 §15.2 require the accepted correction to reach contradicted reviewer assessments. The difference between a ruling and a financial-finality-preserving correction must not make the recorded correction disappear.

### Reproduction

1. C returns N.
2. A accepts the rejection. The case is `FINAL_N`; C is `done`, not yet `vested`.
3. Before the payout batch runs, H overrides the accepted outcome to F.
4. Advance one tick.

### Observed result

C receives a 20-credit fee in this one-review, zero-review-cost, tick-0 fixture: ten credits of unused verification deposit plus ten of current emission. Its bond returns, it receives a positive reliability outcome and duty credit, and it is not marked as contradicted.

The final record says F while C's review says N.

The money figure is not a proposed fixed review fee. It is the computed payout under the existing defaults for this particular batch.

A neighboring control changes only step 3's timing:

| Correction timing | C's status when corrected | Final reviewer reliability |
|---|---|---:|
| Before review payout batch | `done` | **2/3**, only a positive outcome |
| After review payout batch | `vested` | **1/2**, positive outcome plus recorded correction |

The monetary payout is the same in this control. The missing reliability adjustment depends solely on whether the review has reached its payout batch. Both states pass all invariants.

The same defect occurs for a missing-evidence appeal that has just resolved to N and is corrected before its completed reviews are paid.

### Cause

`_finalize_N()` at `sim/engine.py:918–941` sets the case's `final` flag while scheduling completed reviews for later settlement. `override()` uses `c.final` at line 963 to choose its post-settlement branch, then processes only reviews with status `vested` at line 984. A completed review in status `done` is skipped. `_settle_reviews()` later grants it a success at lines 1111–1123 with no correction applied.

### Minimal repair direction

Keep an admitted correction associated with all relevant completed assessments, including reviews awaiting the batch. Apply the selected nonfinancial consequence consistently when they settle. Clarify the fee/bond boundary if needed, but do not lose the correction merely because the actor's obligation and the review's payment finish at different times.

The tests require the correction to reach the reviewer's adverse-outcome record. They deliberately do not demand a particular new financial reversal.

Tests: `test_correction_reaches_completed_review_on_both_sides_of_batch` (four runs: N and `⊥`, before and after batch).

## C. A view-only reviewer is offered four answers, but audits secretly accept only two

**Classification:** confirmed interface/validator mismatch. Resolving it requires making the declared output contract and the audit rule compatible, not choosing an evaluator.

### Rule basis and interface

v0.6 §14 makes initial reviews and blind audits indistinguishable in `ReviewView`, with `question="fulfillment"`. v0.8 says the stale policy guidance was replaced by the actual contract.

The supplied `ActorPolicy.review()` documentation at `sim/strategies.py:25–30` now explicitly says a fulfillment question—initial or audit—returns **F/N/P/⊥**, with no objection disposition.

However, `complete_review()` at `sim/engine.py:579–580` refuses P or `⊥` for an audit.

### Reproduction

I constructed two independent worlds with exactly equal `ReviewView` values, including equal review/case identifiers, reviewer assignment terms and timing:

```text
review_id='r2', case_id='q2', actor='A', t_submit=0,
deadline=5, bond=5, cap=5, resumed=False, is_nested=False,
question='fulfillment', appealed_assessment=None, objection_text=None
```

In one world r2 is an initial review; in the other it is a surprise audit. No hidden state is modified.

| Returned assessment | Initial review | Audit |
|---|---|---|
| F | Admitted | Admitted |
| N | Admitted | Admitted |
| P | Admitted | **Refused** |
| `⊥` | Admitted | **Refused** |

All invariants pass. The refusals are financially atomic; that is not the defect.

### What this does and does not mean

A reviewer can always restrict itself to F/N under the current implementation. The finding is **not** that no valid policy exists.

The finding is that a reviewer following the documented four-category contract cannot tell from its permitted inputs whether P or `⊥` is legal. Merely retrying after rejection can reveal the hidden role. For genuine uncertainty, that also pressures an audit policy to state an F/N answer even though the identical initial-review interface permits asking for evidence or revision.

### Minimal repair direction

Make the visible fulfillment-question contract and the hidden role restrictions agree. Preserve audit blindness as an intentional choice rather than bypassing it by giving the reviewer the entire world. This review does not choose between revising the audit response handling and revising the documented/common contract.

Tests: `test_identical_fulfillment_views_have_same_response_admissibility` (four runs; F/N controls pass).

## D. A delegated requester can invoke the human-only override path

**Classification:** role-boundary finding under the literal human-only rule; confirm intended authority before treating this as only an implementation repair.

### Rule basis

v0.8 §15.3 distinguishes human and delegate authors. It brackets **which human is authorized over a delegate's request**, treating every human as authorized in the harness. It does not explicitly grant delegates themselves human-ruling authority.

This test follows that narrow reading. If delegates are intended to possess final-ruling authority too, that is a broader authority rule that should be stated; the test should then change with that selected rule.

### Reproduction

The standard fixture contains H and S in the request-budget argument and separately identifies S with `delegates=['S']`, just as the supplied tests do.

1. H—not S—commissions a ten-credit task.
2. C approves A's submission.
3. S calls `override(q, 'S', N)`.

### Observed result

The override is admitted without a court fee, reviewer panel or actual human event:

| Item | Before S's call | After S's call |
|---|---:|---:|
| A total wallet | 50 | 45 |
| C total wallet | 50 | 45 |
| S total wallet | 100 | 100 |
| H total wallet | 979.5 | 1004.5 |

A's guarantee is forfeited, C's bond is burned, and the case becomes `FINAL_N` with `delegate-explicit` grade. The grade correctly identifies S as a delegate, but the authority gate treated S as a human. All invariant checks pass.

### Cause

The constructor at `sim/engine.py:229–234` places all requester-budget identities into `self.humans`, then also marks delegates in `self.delegates`. `override()` at lines 955–958 checks only membership in `self.humans`, whereas `_grade()` at lines 1059–1062 distinguishes delegates from humans.

### Minimal repair direction

Make the role accepted by the override guard agree with the declared actor classification. This does not require implementing a full authorization system between individual humans and delegated accounts; that feature can remain bracketed. If delegated override authority is intended, document that power explicitly instead of inheriting it from the constructor argument's name.

Tests: `test_delegate_budget_does_not_itself_grant_human_override_power` fails; the control `test_control_actual_human_can_override_delegate_request` passes.

## Why all 18 invariant checks can pass

These examples conserve recorded money, preserve lock accounting and keep their histories internally consistent. They expose different properties:

- I13 checks a final grade's author but does not establish that the author had the intended role permission.
- The reliability fields are not reconciled against every component of an appeal assessment and each accepted correction.
- There is no invariant comparing permitted policy outputs across identical visible observations.
- A batch can pay a review consistently with its status while that status failed to incorporate a just-admitted correction.

That does not make the existing invariants useless. It bounds what the passing checks establish. These particular counterexamples do not corrupt the hash chain or lose credits.

## Scope and recommended disposition

The selected v0.8 repairs work on the preceding counterexamples. The new counterexamples do not justify reopening price formation, emission, reserve size, the purpose of currency or the actor's objective. They concern existing contract boundaries.

I would repair A and B as correction logic, harmonize the interface in C, and make the role interpretation in D explicit. None requires a new economic mechanism.

A first **scoped** strategy run need not wait for a proof covering every imaginable path. For example, root cases with F/N-only reviewers, actual humans as override authors and no post-settlement corrections avoid the reported failures. Such a run must disclose those exclusions; it would not test the model's missing-evidence, partial-fulfillment or correction behavior.

For an experiment using the complete advertised decision interface, these findings matter: otherwise a skipped reliability penalty or a forced F/N audit response could be interpreted as an incentive or learning effect when it arose from implementation semantics.

No strategy, learned selection model or evaluator was implemented or run during this review. No uploaded source/specification or _line map was modified.
