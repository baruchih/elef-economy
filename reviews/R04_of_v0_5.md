# Agentic Economics v0.5 — independent conformance review

**Reviewed materials:** `agentic_economics_loop_spec_v0_5.md`, its inherited v0.4/v0.3/v0.2/v0.1 rules where relevant, and the actual contents of `agentic_sim_v0_5.zip`.

**Scope:** Executable protocol conformance and the boundary between the reference engine and future policies. This is not an incentive experiment, a judgment of evaluator accuracy, or a production security audit.

## Executive assessment

v0.5 repairs the earlier concrete examples substantially. The previous regression file is byte-for-byte preserved. Its only failing pytest case is the old call form for an appeal of “cannot assess,” which now legitimately requires an explicit objection disposition. The new explicit-disposition tests pass.

The remaining findings are mostly interactions between repaired paths. A human override during an appeal is handled differently from an override during an audit; an audit replacement that works before the window becomes impossible after it; deferred cost handling works with surviving reviewers but not without them. There are also incomplete atomicity guarantees, incorrect attribution of multiple claims by one reviewer, gaps between the protected history and its working copies, and an appeal-policy interface that omits information the engine requires.

These are reasons to complete the conformance pass before drawing economic conclusions from strategy runs. They do not invalidate the chosen economic model.

The archive contains 13 non-directory files. All 13 remain byte-for-byte unchanged in this review bundle. No supplied code or tests were patched. `review_outputs/input_manifest.json` records their hashes and the comparison with the upload.

The uploaded flat files from v0.4 were not used as the v0.5 implementation. The reviewed source is the package extracted from the new ZIP.

Compared with the earlier upload, the inherited `test_spec.py` was changed to exercise the new two-step evidence resubmission. The previous independent `test_review_regressions.py` is unchanged. ### Two test-runner issues, distinct from engine defects

1. The custom runner recognizes the fallback helper's `_params` attribute, but not actual pytest parametrization metadata. With pytest installed, it invokes `test_review_16_invalid_resource_cost_is_rejected` without its `cost` argument. The failure is `TypeError`, not a failure to reject negative or nonfinite costs.
2. The expected legacy failure is listed only in `run_tests.py`. Raw pytest has no matching expected-failure marker, so the old API call is reported as a failure. Its actual exception is the intended `RuleError` asking for the objection disposition.

The v0.5 objection decision is legitimate. The test entry points should communicate that decision consistently, rather than claiming both commands produce a clean result without qualification.

## 2. Genuine repairs verified

The supplied cases now execute for: unfundable awards and unpaid filings; rejection of tested negative/nonfinite resource costs; overlapping-review rejection; basic initial/appeal/audit timeout replacement; review-cost conversion to credits; first-lock denomination of nested bonds; normal FIFO servicing; deferred reimbursement for an overturned reviewer when other reviews survive; ordinary before/after-vesting reviewer consequences; terminal sibling cleanup; subcontract prices in the kill-fee basis; unused deposit return; rolling duty credit; rotation; and the new two-step resubmission and two-question appeal examples.

## 3. Findings

### R4-A — Atomicity is still action-specific, not a boundary-wide guarantee

**Requirements:** v0.5 §2.2 / R57: an action completes under its rules or changes nothing; recording an attempted action is permitted.

**Source:** `sim/engine.py:366–373, 861–893`; `sim/ledger.py:326–337`.

**Tests:** `test_r4_01`, `test_r4_02`, `test_r4_03`.

**A1. An unknown case is checked after the resource purchase.**

Calling `w.spend('A', 3, 'not-a-case')` raises `RuleError`, but A's balance falls from 50 to 47, the reserve and supply each fall by 3, and a purchase event is committed. `_case(cid)` is called after `ledger.purchase()`. All current invariants pass afterward.

**A2. A refused fee-pool payment consumes the deposit.**

With a ten-credit deposit and no minted pool, requesting an eleven-credit payout through `Ledger.pay_from_fee_pool()` raises `LedgerError` after reducing the deposit to zero. No recipient is credited. Total accounted credits fall from 10,000 to 9,990 without a burn. This lower-level ledger test is not a claim that the ordinary payout calculation routinely overdraws; it demonstrates that the public financial primitive is not atomic on rejection. I1 can detect the resulting corruption, but detecting it later does not undo the mutation.

**A3. A refused override becomes later human-confirmation provenance.**

On an AVAILABLE request, `override(q, 'H', F)` correctly raises because there is no submitted result to override. However, the method appends the assessment before checking the state. Awarding that request afterward, completing it successfully, and allowing it to vest in silence produces `human-explicit`, sourced from the refused override. It should be `protocol-default` in this sequence. All current invariants pass.

Recording a failed attempt would be permitted; storing it as an accepted fulfillment assessment is different.

**Required correction:** validate the complete operation before mutating money, case state, or admissible assessments, or use rollback-safe execution. The existing award prechecks solve that particular case but are not a general transaction mechanism.

### R4-B — Overrides during challenges bypass the new override rules

**Requirements:** v0.5 §7.5, §8.1, §15.2: an override voids unfinished reviews without a burn, resolves the relevant assessments, and honors the two-answer constraint for a BOT appeal.

**Source:** `sim/engine.py:717–749, 861–911, 1066–1084`.

**Tests:** `test_r4_04`, `test_r4_05`, `test_r4_13`.

**B1. Unfinished appeal reviewers are penalized after a human has already resolved the case.**

C rejects. A appeals. D and E take the two review positions. The human rules F before D or E finishes. The challenge becomes resolved, but D and E remain assigned with locked bonds. At their deadlines they are withdrawn and each loses 2.5 credits plus a reliability outcome. Their wallets move from 50 to 47.5. This contradicts the new no-fault voiding rule. The direct audit-override test passes because it takes a different branch of `override()`.

**B2. A human BOT ruling accepts an inconsistent answer pair.**

In a split BOT appeal, `override(q, 'H', F, objection='upheld')` is accepted. The case therefore records both “the missing-evidence claim is upheld” and F. The explicit v0.5 rule says upheld entails N. `rule()` and `complete_review()` enforce that constraint; `override()` does not. Current invariants accept the inconsistent settlement.

This is not an objection to human final authority. Under the selected representation, changing that authority's evidentiary decision would need to be recorded consistently rather than accepting a prohibited pair.

**B3. Directly overturning a nested rejection strands its bond.**

A rejects B's submitted child result. The human overrides to F without a paid challenge. B's case vests and pays out, but A's five-credit `rej:<child>` lock remains indefinitely. The direct override reconciles `Review` objects, not the requester-actor's nested judgment. The normal appeal path does resolve that bond.

The regression asserts only that a finished verdict cannot leave its rejection bond unresolved; it does not invent a new allocation of forfeited funds.

**Required correction:** apply one coherent ruling/voiding/settlement procedure to all supported entry paths, including nested requester judgments. Validate the entire ruling before recording it.

### R4-C — A late audit timeout lets a case vest before its replacement can be assigned

**Requirements:** v0.5 §7.5 / R58 permits replacement after the window if the withdrawn audit was assigned in time; v0.4 §6 suspends vesting for pending reviews.

**Source:** `sim/engine.py:452–468, 1033–1060, 1066–1084`.

**Test:** `test_r4_06`.

Reproduction:

| Tick | Event |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Initial approval; window ends at 10 |
| 9 | D receives an audit; its review deadline is 14 |
| 14 | The audit times out during tick processing |
| 14 | The same processing pass vests the case |
| 15 | Assigning E as replacement is rejected because the case is VESTED |

Withdrawal lifts `open_items`, sets `audit_vacated=True`, and the immediately following vesting pass ignores the vacant position. Consequently, the new post-window replacement permission is unreachable for this normal timing case. All invariants pass.

**Required correction:** specify and enforce whether an unfilled replacement position remains an unsettled case obligation. If the intended rule is instead to abandon the audit and vest, that is a change to the stated replacement rule, not the currently claimed implementation of it.

### R4-D — Deferred reimbursement is still conditional on an unrelated surviving review, and FIFO loses review identity

**Requirements:** v0.5 §4.3 / R60: cost obligations survive overturn; obligations are paid FIFO and attributed to actual costs.

**Source:** `sim/engine.py:932–992, 1006–1020`.

**Tests:** `test_r4_07`, `test_r4_08`.

These two tests retain the standard ten-credit root deposit and five-percent service fee, but set the initial Omega float to zero to exercise the defined shortfall branch. The first uses nested reviews to exhaust the root deposit; the second uses three reviews per case. The tests do not estimate the frequency of such shortfalls under the default twenty-credit float. Emission is disabled to isolate accounting.

**D1. No surviving review means no debt servicing or debt registration.**

Two appeal reviews of a child exhaust the root's ten-credit deposit. C then completes the root's initial review: approximately 4.9985 credits are spent, 4.25 are reimbursed from Omega, and approximately 0.7485 are deferred. A human override overturns C, leaving no surviving reviews on the root. `_settle_reviews()` takes an early return before converting C's deferred remainder into an Omega claim. The case's settlement-pending flag is cleared while `r.deferred` still holds the remainder and `omega_claims=[]`. The amount remains in an inert review field; it is not erased from memory, but later Omega receipts cannot service it. The noninteger credit cost follows the ordinary nested bond burn and moving credit value, not a manually edited balance.

This is the same general obligation as the already-passing “overturned reviewer keeps its costs” example, with the additional condition that no other review survives.

**D2. Two claims by C can be paid correctly to C but attributed to the wrong reviews.**

C first performs a three-credit appeal review whose split case awaits an orchestrator ruling. C later performs a five-credit appeal review on a case that settles earlier. The first has 0.5 deferred and the second 2.5 deferred, so the queue becomes `(C,2.5), (C,0.5)` in reverse review-creation order. A subsequent sixty-credit award supplies a three-credit service fee.

Actual money to C is correct: three additional credits, and the queue empties. The per-review accounting is not:

| Review | Cost | Reimbursed, observed | Pending, observed | Correct accounting |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| First-created | 3 | 5 | -2 | Reimbursed 3; pending 0 |
| Second-created | 5 | 3 | 2 | Reimbursed 5; pending 0 |

Claims identify the reviewer but not the obligation. `_service_omega_claims()` chooses the first review belonging to that reviewer with a positive pending field, rather than the review whose debt is being paid. Current invariants do not reject the negative pending-reimbursement field.

**Required correction:** separate processing of unconditional cost debts from conditional fee entitlements, and preserve the originating obligation in each claim. No new compensation mechanism is necessary.

### R4-E — The protected history is not the same record the rest of the harness exposes and uses

**Requirements:** v0.5 §14 and inherited history/label provenance rules.

**Source:** `sim/ledger.py:72–98, 144–153`; `sim/engine.py:239–241, 913–929`; `sim/invariants.py:246–248`.

**Tests:** `test_r4_10`, both parameters of `test_r4_11`.

**E1. Explicit acceptance is not appended to the chain.**

`accept_explicit()` changes `case.assessments`, but the history head and its entries remain unchanged. It changes the information later used to produce a human-explicit grade without committing the acceptance event itself. This is a supported API call, not an attacker rewriting a hash.

**E2. Ledger and case-log working copies are not checked against their protected snapshots.**

Editing the recipient of an existing `ledger.events` transfer, or changing an existing `case.log` message, is not detected by `check_all()`. The central `History` payload copy remains intact, so I16 verifies that copy. The edited operational/exported record is not compared with it.

This does not break SHA-256, prove a production exploit, or require an external head commitment. The supplied test correctly detects an edit to `History.entries` itself. The additional tests show that the broader “ledger events and case logs are one protected record” claim needs a canonical-record rule or consistency checks between copies. The trusted-runtime/external-head limitation remains as explicitly bracketed.

**Required correction:** identify the authoritative record, commit relevant human actions into it, and ensure any separate record used for replay, accounting, or reporting agrees with it. These tests do not require hashing private model reasoning or implementing a new consensus system.

### R4-F — The reviewer policy interface cannot express the new appeal task from the information it receives

**Requirements:** v0.5 §8.1 requires an explicit objection answer for BOT appeals; §14 and §22 hand policies only the filtered view. Initial/audit blindness does not eliminate the need to identify the question posed in an appeal.

**Source:** `sim/engine.py:82–94, 431–437, 504–526`; `sim/strategies.py:25–28`.

**Test:** `test_r4_12`.

Two independently constructed cases give D identical `ReviewView` values:

`ReviewView(review_id='r2', case_id='q1', actor='A', t_submit=0, deadline=5, bond=5, cap=5, resumed=False, is_nested=False)`

In one case D is an auditor; in the other D is reviewing a BOT appeal. Valid output formats are different:

- The appeal requires `objection='upheld'` or `'rejected'`.
- The audit forbids an objection disposition.

The current policy interface's docstring acknowledges that a policy unable to tell should return `None`, which the engine rejects on the appeal. Giving the policy the whole World would bypass the visibility rule instead of completing the intended interface.

The harness deliberately omits actual request content and semantic judgments; that is not the finding. The finding is that even an injected/scripted policy cannot choose the required answer schema from its permitted input alone. A distinction between an ordinary/blind-audit assignment and an appeal question is needed. The original reviewer’s assessment and disputer’s objection can be represented without exposing hidden audit labels.

**Classification:** policy-interface incompleteness, rather than evidence that the hand-scripted settlement engine cannot settle a supplied explicit disposition.

### R4-G — P has a route the selected response table does not allow

**Requirement:** v0.5 §6.1 permits revise or accept N for P; appeals are defined for N and BOT.

**Source:** `sim/engine.py:582–609`.

**Test:** `test_r4_09`.

`respond(q,'appeal')` accepts a P verdict because the appeal branch does not check the verdict. It consumes the challenge round and court fee. This is a missing guard, not a request to add a new appeal mechanism.

## 4. What the invariants currently show

The improvements to conservation reconstruction, amount checks, named locks, occupancy guards, and the central history chain are real. They should be retained.

The diagnostic probes also show current invariants passing after: an unknown-case spend; a refused override supplying human provenance; appeal reviewers penalized after a human has resolved the case; an inconsistent BOT override; premature vesting on audit timeout; a stranded cost claim; negative per-review pending-reimbursement accounting; a rejection bond held after successful nested vesting; and an uncommitted human acceptance.

An invariant checks the property it encodes. A matching assessment in a mutable list does not prove it came from an admitted action. A lock matching a still-nonzero field does not prove that field represents an obligation that should remain live. A valid chain over one copy does not verify a separate copy.

This is a coverage issue, not a reason to remove the checks or to treat every assertion failure as an economic phenomenon.

## 5. Review priorities

1. Unify outcome/override/voiding paths and complete action atomicity. These can currently create financial consequences or human provenance the action should not produce.
2. Repair post-window audit replacement and make reimbursement processing independent of surviving-fee recipients. Preserve per-review debt identity.
3. Align recorded history with the authoritative events and complete the appeal view's required input. Correct the smaller P guard and the two runner entry points.

Keep the selected economic rules unless a branch truly requires a rule choice. The supplied tests now provide useful positive controls. Add interaction regressions to them rather than substituting only isolated happy paths for the corrected behavior.

## Closing assessment

**The earlier concrete defects are mostly repaired; important interactions still violate the selected rules.** The tests do not establish whether the economy's incentives work, and the remaining conformance defects would distort such an experiment if treated as actor behavior. This is still an implementation reconciliation, not a need to invent another economic layer.
